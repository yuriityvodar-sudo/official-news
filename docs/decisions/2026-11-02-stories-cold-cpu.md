# A country's stories built for a call that starts cold (#230, the aggregator)

- **Decision:** The stories' build is made for a call whose code has not warmed up, as each StoryBuilder call on Workers is. Three changes, with the same stories as before, in the same order, in all 23 countries of 1 Nov:
  - **The fetcher reads each headline's words once** and keeps them with it (`words` in `outlets.headlines`, a name's with `^` first): at most 40 headlines a check, and again when a headline's title changes. The build reads them instead of reading every headline again; a headline kept before has its words read in the build.
  - **The grouping** (`topStories`, `server/src/stories/cluster.ts`) sorts and picks each headline's words in plain loops, keeps a story's centre in a typed array, and makes only the 50 weightiest groups into stories, of the 250 or so a big country has.
  - **The headlines' check** (`toTitles`) applies the translator's rules for one sentence without Unicode regexes: in Ukrainian and Russian, Cyrillic and none of the other language's letters and words (now lists the translator's regex is made from, `OTHER_LETTERS` and `OTHER_WORDS`, the same regex); in Spanish and German, not the English and no more function words of English or of the other language than of its own. Its verdicts are the old check's on the trial's 19 answers and on 17 spoiled on purpose.
- **Why:** Yurii's check of #282 on Workers (2 Nov, 14:15 UTC): 40 ms of CPU for Germany's 300 headlines, 29 for India's, 23 for the US's, 12 for South Africa's 31, against the 10 ms a call has; Cloudflare stops a Worker that goes over consistently. The laptop's 3 ms were measured after warming the code up; a fresh process, as a cold call, takes as long as Workers did. Measured that way (5 fresh processes each, 1 Nov's headlines):
  - the build itself took India 36 ms, Germany 21, Ukraine 23, Czechia 10, South Africa 4: most of it reading the words (5 to 9 ms) and making stories of every group (2 to 6 ms);
  - the headlines' check took 10 ms for its first answer, compiling Unicode regexes (`inLanguage` and the Latin-word check), then 0.1 ms; that was most of South Africa's 12 ms on Workers;
  - now a whole build with two answers to check takes about 40% of that for the biggest countries (India about 16 ms, Germany about 12) and a quarter or less for the others (Czechia about 5, South Africa about 2).

  Capping each outlet at 25 headlines and a country at 150 would have saved as much, but lost big stories (Germany's FBI on North Korea's hackers, 5 outlets; Spain's Márquez, 4). The biggest countries are still over 10 ms: next, the grouping's loops in WebAssembly, which runs fast from the start.
- **Status:** Proposed (Roman's Claude, on Roman's word of 1 Nov: «Да, строй»)
