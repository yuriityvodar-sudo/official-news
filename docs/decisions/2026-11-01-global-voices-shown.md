# Global Voices is shown, with its author, the original and its licence before the text (#230)

- **Decision:** Global Voices (`ww-global-voices`) is no longer `hidden`. The API gives every article its source's `credit` (`docs/source-credits.csv`: the licence, its link, the exact wording) and a story's `author` (never a decision's: only a `stories` source keeps one, since a government feed's `dc:creator` is often "admin"). The app and the site show a news story, an event whose link is not Wikipedia's, with "Story by {author} · {outlet}" linking the original, then the outlet's wording and licence, before its text, as Global Voices' attribution policy asks; "Read on {outlet}" for its link and "Summary by AI of {outlet}'s text" for its label, where a Wikipedia event names Wikipedia. Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty's feeds, first in this pull request as text sources, are left out: its Use Our Content allows republishing a story whole or a marked excerpt, but not altering it (an AI summary in 5 languages is neither), forbids selling its content (we have a paid chat that answers from the feed), and is revocable; stories with AP, Reuters or AFP material need those owners' permission too. Radio Svoboda comes in as a headline and a link only, which the fixed principle already allows, with the other news outlets of the aggregator (`docs/decisions/` of that pull request).
- **Why:** Roman, 1 Nov: «пользуем то что есть бесплатно без проблем или сложностей / главное чтобы лента была всегда заполнялась свежими новостями вместе с тем что у нас уже есть». Official sites publish little at night and at weekends (16 articles in the 24 hours to Sunday morning), and Global Voices publishes every day. Yurii's review of #275 (his Claude, 1 Nov): RFE/RL's terms do not clearly cover AI summaries or a paid chat; split it and keep Global Voices with its credit. Asking RFE/RL for written permission is the kind of complication Roman ruled out.
- **Status:** Proposed (Roman's Claude, on Roman's word of 1 Nov and Yurii's review)
