# Contract

The shared definitions both the app and the backend build against. Changed only by agreement, in a pull request that also updates `docs/decisions.md`. The code that implements it: `app/lib/core/article.dart`, `app/lib/core/decision_types.dart`, `app/lib/core/topics.dart`, `server/src/api/index.ts`, `server/src/api/filters.ts` (the feed's filters: groups of countries, topic, country and group names), `server/src/translator/languages.ts` (languages), `server/src/translator/queue.ts` (the translation queue), `server/src/push/` (push notifications), `server/migrations/`.

## Article

What the API returns and both screens read.

```json
{
  "id": "01M5CNQBYGP8FJEA7V29R90PHQ",
  "country": "PL",
  "source": { "id": "pl-council-of-ministers", "name": "Council of Ministers, government decisions (gov.pl)", "url": "https://www.gov.pl/web/premier/decyzje-rzadu" },
  "publishedAt": "2026-10-20T12:00:00Z",
  "dateOnly": true,
  "headline": "Council of Ministers adopts bill to delay KSeF penalties and extend reverse VAT charge",
  "headlineType": null,
  "shortSummary": "The Council of Ministers adopted a bill to delay KSeF e-invoice penalties until the end of 2027 and extend the reverse VAT mechanism for gas, electricity, and emission allowances until June 30, 2030.",
  "summary": "The Council of Ministers has adopted a bill to amend the VAT Act and other laws. … (news-style, short paragraphs)",
  "decisionType": "bill",
  "originalTitle": "Projekt ustawy o zmianie ustawy o podatku od towarów i usług oraz …",
  "originalLanguage": "pl",
  "topics": ["economy", "energy"],
  "tags": ["Council of Ministers", "Poland", "KSeF", "VAT"],
  "importance": 3,
  "imageUrl": "https://… or null",
  "originalUrl": "https://www.gov.pl/web/premier/projekt-ustawy-o-zmianie-ustawy-o-podatku-od-towarow-i-uslug-…",
  "feedSeq": 307,
  "addedAt": "2026-10-20T12:52:50Z",
  "countries": ["PL"],
  "impact": {
    "general": "The bill delays fines for e-invoice errors until late 2027 and extends the reverse VAT mechanism for energy and emissions until mid-2030.",
    "byTopic": [
      { "topic": "economy", "text": "Businesses will not face monetary penalties for errors related to the National e-Invoice System (KSeF) until the end of 2027." },
      { "topic": "energy", "text": "Buyers of gas, electricity, and greenhouse gas emission allowances must continue to settle VAT instead of sellers until June 30, 2030." }
    ],
    "residents": "Entrepreneurs get more time to adapt to the KSeF system without facing fines, while energy buyers continue to settle VAT directly.",
    "abroad": null
  }
}
```

- Since #133 a summary is useful news, not a retelling (`docs/style-guide.md`): `headline` is a hook headline and `headlineType` its kind, `question`, `consequence`, `contrast` (the article's two strongest numbers) or `plain` (war, disasters, deaths, and decisions that change nothing practical); `null` for articles summarized before #133, as in the example above. The kind is picked by the server for each article, at random but always the same for one article, so the app's analytics can compare how often each kind is opened. `summary` says who it is for and what changes for them, "What to do:" when the article gives something to do, and "What we do not know:". These articles have `impact: null` and `tags: []`: what it means is in the summary, and the app does not show tags.
- The feed card reads `headline`, `shortSummary`, `imageUrl` and `topics` (up to 3 tags, the filtered topic first, #66); the detail screen reads `summary` and shows `decisionType` as a red label above the headline ("DECISION"). `topics` is set by the AI; the feed card shows them as tags, up to three (decision 2026-10-16), and the feed's Topics filter (#67) uses them. `country` is the source's (`EU` for the EU institutions, `WW` for a source about the whole world, such as Wikipedia's current events, #230): the Where filter (#68) filters on it, and `GET /countries` lists neither.
- `feedSeq` is the article's place in the feed: a number given when it is released (the AI marked it an official decision, and every enabled language has its translation: release together, below), only ever rising and never changed afterwards. `addedAt` is when that happened (column `added_at`, set with `feed_seq` and, like it, never changed by a later re-summary). The card shows `publishedAt`; the feed order follows `feedSeq`, so an article that appears late lands on top, not among the ones already read (docs/research/2026-10-15 Feed ordering for late items.md).
- `impact` (#63) is what the decision means in practice, written by the AI with the summary and shown by the app as an AI analysis: `general` for any reader, `byTopic` one line per topic of the article from that topic's angle, `residents` for people in the country or bloc that decided, `abroad` for people outside it. Every part may be `null` (or `byTopic` empty), and `impact` itself is `null` for articles summarized before #63 or since #133, articles that are not news, and in a language feed (it is not translated yet). Only direct consequences of the decision's stated terms; no predictions.
- Before the AI step has run, `headline`, `shortSummary`, `summary`, `decisionType` and `importance` are `null` and `topics` is `[]`; the app shows `originalTitle` instead.
- `publishedAt` is UTC. `dateOnly: true` means the source gave no time.
- `countries` (#230, additive): the countries the article concerns, from the summarizer, the one it concerns most first (ISO codes, `EU` for the Union); `[]` before the AI step. `country` stays the source's. A card of an event (`decisionType` `event`, whose source's country is `WW`) shows the first of `countries` instead.
- Translations (#46, after the MVP): with `?lang=uk`, `ru`, `es` or `de` the API returns only articles fully translated into that language. Their `headline`, `shortSummary` and `summary` are in that language, and they have `"lang"` (`"uk"`, `"ru"`, `"es"` or `"de"`). `"feedSeq"` and `"addedAt"` are the English ones: every language has the same feed (release together). Every other field stays as it is (`originalTitle` in the original language, `headlineType` the English headline's kind), except `source.name` (#76): the source's name in that language (`config.names.<lang>` of the source, from `docs/source-names.csv`, such as "Федеральний уряд Німеччини" for "Federal Government (Bundesregierung)"), or its English name when it has none in that language; and `impact` (#63), which is `null` until the translator carries it. With `lang=en`, or without `lang`, the article is exactly as above, without `lang`, and `source.name` is the English name.
- Release together (#46, Yurii 15 Oct): the feed is the same in every language, the same posts in the same order with the same top stories; only the language differs. A summarized decision is not shown yet: it is queued for every enabled language, and when each enabled language that is not paused has published its translation (or failed it 3 times), it is released: it gets its `feedSeq` and `addedAt` and appears in English and every language at once. English waits a few minutes too. A post that failed 3 times in a language is simply absent from that language's feed. A language whose provider is down for more than 2 hours is paused automatically, so the release stops waiting for it; it catches up when the provider is back, at the articles' own `feedSeq`, so its order still matches English. With no language enabled, an article is released at once. While the translator is not running (no run in the last 10 minutes: a new cron trigger can take about an hour to start firing, #50, or it stopped), the release waits for no translation either: the summarizer's run releases each decision at once, and every language fills in later at the English places, as after a pause.

## Decision types

A fixed list. The AI gives each news article one of them; the app shows its label above the headline. Code: `app/lib/core/decision_types.dart`.

`law` (Law), `bill` (Bill), `amendment` (Amendment), `proposal` (Proposal), `cabinet-decision` (Cabinet decision), `decision` (Decision), `meeting-agenda` (Meeting agenda), `meeting-outcome` (Meeting outcome), `event` (Event)

`event` (#230, additive) is not a decision: an event from Wikipedia's current events, given only to articles of an event source, and never to an official one. Until the app labels events (#230 step 3b) those sources are hidden, so no article in the API has it; its label is `decisionTypeEvent` in the app's texts, and an app that does not know it shows no label. The app (#230 step 3b, Roman's variant A) shows an event without the official-source seal: its cards name the first of `countries` the app has a name for (else "World"), and its page says "Spain · Event", "Summary by AI of Wikipedia's text", "Read on Wikipedia" and Wikipedia's licence (CC BY-SA 4.0, a link), and shares it as "Source:".

## Push notifications in the app

The app's side of the breaking-decision pushes (#62); what the server sends, and when, is its part (`server/src/push/`). Code: `app/lib/core/push.dart`.

- Topics: one per feed language, `breaking_<lang>` with the API's `lang` values: `breaking_en`, `breaking_uk`, `breaking_ru`, `breaking_es`, `breaking_de`. A phone that allows notifications is subscribed to the topic of its feed language, and only that one: a language change unsubscribes the old topic first, then subscribes the new one. The topic is saved on the phone (`push.topic`) before it is subscribed, and stays saved until Firebase confirms its unsubscribe; every start subscribes once more (in case Firebase lost it) and replaces a saved topic of another language. A change with no answer within 30 seconds (offline) may still be carried out by Firebase later: on iOS it keeps such a change in its own queue, across launches, and sends it once the phone is online. So the saved topic is the one that may be subscribed, and the change is sent again when the app comes back to the front, when the FCM token changes, or at the next language change or start; Firebase carries out the queued and the new ones in order. Notifications turned off in the iOS Settings app: the topic is dropped, and taken again when they are back on.
- The AI chat's note (#141, #160) has nothing to do with push: "Tell me when it's ready" is one tap that turns into a checkmark, kept on the phone (`aiChat.interested`), and sends `ai_chat_interest` once per phone, next to `discuss_ai_tap` for each tap on "Discuss with AI". When the chat ships, the app tells readers itself. The earlier switch's topic (`ai_chat_<lang>`, saved as `push.aiChatTopic`) is unsubscribed on the phones that took it; the server never sent to it.
- The message's data, both strings: `articleId` (the article's `id`) and `lang` (`en`, `uk`, `ru`, `es` or `de`; anything else is English). The title (the headline) and body (the short summary, cut at about 110 characters) are the notification's own text; the app does not read them. On iOS a line under the title (the APNs subtitle, #157) gives the source's country, its flag and name in the push's language, and, for importance 5 only, "Breaking" in that language: "🇧🇷 Brazil · Breaking", "🇮🇹 Italy".
- A tap, with the app open, in the background or not running, opens that article over the feed: `GET /articles/:id?lang=<lang>` (no `lang` for `en`). When it does not load (404, offline) or the id is missing, the app shows the feed. While the app is open, a notification shows as a banner too.
- When the app asks: never on the first launch. Once, when the reader is back on the feed after the third different article read (an `article_read`: 15 seconds open with the app in front), the app explains first: "Breaking decisions. Get the most important official decisions as a notification as soon as they come, never at night." (#157: every decision of importance 4 or 5, no daily cap) with Not Now and Turn On, in the app's language like every other word (`pushOffer*` in `app/lib/l10n/app_<lang>.arb`). Only Turn On shows iOS's question (alerts, badge, sound). Whatever the answer, the app never asks by itself again (`push.offered` on the phone).
- The Settings row "Notifications" (`app/lib/features/settings/settings_screen.dart`) shows `push.permission`, live, and is the reader's way in later:
  - iOS has not asked yet: "Turn On" in the accent colour. A tap shows iOS's question at once (`push.enable(trigger: 'settings')`, no explanation first: the reader asked for it) and the row shows the answer.
  - Allowed: "On"; not allowed: "Off". Both with a chevron; a tap opens the iOS Settings app (`app-settings:`, `UIApplication.openSettingsURLString`, through `url_launcher`), meant to land on this app's page, the only place iOS lets notifications be turned off, or on again after a "Don't Allow". On the iOS 26.3 Simulator it opened Settings at its root page; a real iPhone (TestFlight) is still to be checked. Back in the app, the permission is read again on the return to the front, and the row and the topic follow.
  - No push in this build: "Unavailable", no tap.
  - The words are `notifications*` in the ARB files, as the iOS Settings app writes them on its Notifications screen: "Увімкнено" / "Вимкнено" in Ukrainian, the short "Вкл." / "Выкл." in Russian, "Ein" / "Aus" in German. Spanish has "Activadas" / "Desactivadas", right in every region (iOS writes "Sí" / "No" in Spain and "Activado" / "Desactivado" in Latin America). VoiceOver's hint names the Settings app as an app ("Відкриває застосунок «Параметри»"), so it is not taken for the app's own Settings screen.
- Analytics: `push_permission` with `result` and `trigger`: `granted` or `denied` only when iOS really asked, with `trigger` `reads` (Turn On in the offer) or `settings` (the row's Turn On); `not_now` when Not Now is tapped in the app's own explanation (`trigger` `reads`, iOS did not ask). A second Turn On tap while iOS's question is still open asks nothing more and sends nothing. Opening the iOS Settings app sends nothing, and a change made there shows in the user property `notifications`.
- Without the Firebase values in the build (tests, local runs) push is off: no question, no topic, the Settings row says "Unavailable". Firebase gives the phone an FCM token only once notifications are allowed (`FirebaseMessagingAutoInitEnabled` is off in `Info.plist`). Push starts with the feed's language (`FeedController.language`), so a first launch with nothing chosen uses the phone's language, as the rest of the app does (#52).

## Feed filters in the app

The app's side of what the feed shows (#170, #67); what the server answers is in the API below (`GET /articles` with `for` and `topic`, `GET /topics`, `GET /locate`). Code: `app/lib/core/filters.dart`, `app/lib/features/feed/feed_filters.dart`, `app/lib/features/feed/feed_controller.dart`.

- One pull-down under the date: For you (the default), The whole world, then the topics `GET /topics` lists, the whole world's, busiest first, one at a time, no counts. A topic is the whole world's: choosing one shows the whole world with it, and For you clears it. A look may offer the same choices its own way (Garnet and Seal: a row of pills, #166).
- For you sends `for=XX`, the home country (Settings → My country, else the phone's region, else `GET /locate`), and a topic `topic=<id>`. Every request of a feed sends them: the first page, every next page (`cursor`) and the "N new" check (`after`). The app never filters on the phone, and sends neither `country=` nor `group=`: the server keeps them for builds from before For you (#68).
- The chip, as in the chips prototype after Yurii's review (`prototype/chips` 3d88cf3, 16 Oct): "For you" or "The whole world" on the grey chip. A chosen topic shows short: its icon and the chevron in the filter red (`filterRed`: `#C41230` in light mode, `#FF3B55` in dark, at least 4.5:1 on the grey chip, a test checks it; the brand red `#FA2D48` is 4.48:1 there in dark mode). Each topic has one icon (`topicIcons` in `app/lib/core/topics.dart`: health a star of life, climate a leaf, elections a ticked document; none that means something else in an app, such as a heart or a ticked box). VoiceOver reads the full name: "Topic: Economy" (`filterTopicChip` in the ARB files). The same red marks the menu's checkmark and the empty feed's "Show all topics".
- The menu shows an icon before each name (secondary label colour): For you's sparkles, the whole world's globe, and each topic's, the one the chip shows once it is chosen.
- Names are the app's own (ARB files), so a menu saved in another language is never shown in it. The server's name shows only for a topic the app does not know yet, and a topic without an icon shows a grid. A chosen topic the menu does not list now stays in it, checked.
- Saved on the phone: `feed.where` (`world` for the whole world, nothing for For you), `feed.topic`, and the topics per language (`feed.topics.<lang>`). The saved first page is For you's for its country, or the whole world's, never a topic's, so a topic opened offline says it could not load. What older builds saved opens For you, and their keys are removed at start: the first filters build's Local (`feed.scope`, `feed.country`, `feed.countries.<lang>`), and the Where menus of #68 (`feed.whereMenu.<lang>`, `feed.noCountries`). A saved group or country (`group:g7`, `country:GB`) opens For you too.
- Older servers: without `GET /topics` the menu offers For you and the whole world only. A topic chosen before stays in it, so it can be cleared.
- Analytics: `filter_changed` when the reader changes what the feed shows, with `where` (`forYou` or `all`) and `topic` (its id, or `all`). Builds before For you also sent `group` or `country` as `where`, with `place` (the group's id or the country's code). Not `value`: Google Analytics keeps that parameter for an event's number and drops a text in it (`firebase_error` 18, firebase.google.com/docs/analytics/errors).

## AI chat in the app (#146)

What the reader sees and what the app keeps; the server's side is `/v1/chat` and `/v1/appstore/notifications` (API, below). Code: `app/lib/features/chat/`.

- "Discuss with AI" under the summary (until now the note about the chat to come, #141) opens the paywall when the phone has no active subscription, and the chat when it has one. iOS only for 1.0: on Android the button keeps the note until Google Play billing is added. Until the chat is released, only the team's builds have it (`ChatScreen.opened`): the demo (`CHAT_DEMO=1`, a debug build) or a build with a Sandbox key in `CHAT_SANDBOX_KEY` (`X-Chat-Sandbox-Key`, below), Roman's own (28 Oct) and the TestFlight workflow's `testflight` builds (29 Oct); `store` builds keep the note.
- The paywall is one screen, variant B of Roman's mockup: the decision's chip, a sample question ("Does this concern me?") and the start of a sample answer, the first lines of the article's summary in the app's language, fading under the offer: "Continue the conversation with the AI agent", what it does (only the official text, the source link, conversations not kept), the price and period as the largest text, auto-renewal and where to cancel, Subscribe, Restore Purchases, the Terms (Apple's standard EULA) and the Privacy Policy, and a close button back to the article. No free trial and no free questions.
- The purchase: Flutter's `in_app_purchase` (StoreKit 2 on iOS), one product, `com.officialnews.app.agent.monthly` (`CHAT_PRODUCT_ID`, the same on the server). The app makes a UUID once and keeps it on the phone (`chat.accountToken`), sent with the purchase as `appAccountToken`. After a purchase or a restore it finishes the transaction and keeps nothing else: StoreKit holds the subscription, and each question sends its latest signed transaction.
- Before the first question, once: a consent sheet, "Your question and this article's text are sent to OpenAI for the answer. We don't keep your conversations. If you ask Apple for a refund, we may tell Apple how much you used the chat.", with Allow and Not now (guideline 5.1.2(i)). Kept on the phone (`chat.consent`); a Settings row to take it back is still to come.
- The chat screen: the decision at the top, suggested questions ("Does this concern me?", "What changes and when?", "What should I do?"), the answer streamed as it comes, the official link under each answer. `402` shows the paywall again; `403`, `503` and an answer cut off offer Try again.
- The limit as Claude shows it (variant A of Roman's mockup, 27 Oct): the app asks `/v1/chat/status` as the chat opens and reads `X-Chat-Remaining` and `X-Chat-Units` after each answer. Nothing about the limit until 3 or fewer questions are left: then "3 questions left today" over the field. With none left (the status, or a `429`), a card takes the field's place: "No questions left for today", "New ones at 00:00. Or buy more: they don't expire.", and "Buy more questions"; a question refused with `429` goes back into the field. Over a long document (`units` 2 or more): "Long document: a question about it counts as 3".
- "Buy more questions" opens the top-up sheet: the two packs as the App Store prices them, +50 and +200 ("better value"), "Buy for $0.99", "A one-time App Store purchase. A question about a long document can count as several." The packs are consumables (`buyConsumable`): a bought pack goes to `/v1/chat/credits` with the subscription's transaction and is finished only once the server has credited it, so one the phone could not hand over (offline) comes back from StoreKit at the next start and is credited then, once. The sheet closes once it is credited, and the chat asks for the status again.
- Nothing of the conversation goes to analytics. Events: `paywall_view`, `subscribe_tap`, `subscribe_result` (`purchased`, `cancelled`, `failed`, `pending`), `restore_result` (`restored`, `nothing`, `failed`), `chat_question` (the article's id and the question's number in the conversation, never its text), `topup_view`, `topup_result` (`credited`, `cancelled`, `failed`, `pending`, and the pack's `questions`).
- When the chat ships, the server sends one notification per language to `ai_chat_<lang>` (Push notifications in the app, above), for the readers who asked to be told.

## API

Base URL: `https://api.officialnews.workers.dev` (deployed), `http://localhost:8787` (local, `npm run dev` in `server/`).

- `GET /articles?limit=20&cursor=<c>` → `{ "items": [Article], "nextCursor": "…" | null, "top": [Article] }`: news from the last 7 days where `is_news = 1`, in the order they appeared in the feed, newest `feedSeq` first. `limit` is 1 to 50, default 20. `nextCursor` is an opaque string to send back as `cursor` for the next page, `null` on the last page. `top` is filled only on the first page (no `cursor`, no `since`); otherwise `[]`. Top stories (#164): the 3 decisions of the last 72 hours (by `publishedAt`) with an importance and the highest score, `importance − 0.5 × age in days`, at most 1 per country (any number in a one-country view, `country=`), listed best score first (on equal scores the newer). It may be empty or hold fewer than 3, so the app must not depend on it. Top articles also appear in `items`; builds before #164 place them by `feedSeq` there, whatever the order of `top`.
- `GET /articles?after=<feedSeq>` → only articles that appeared after that `feedSeq`, late ones included: the "N new" check. The app sends the highest `feedSeq` it holds.
- `GET /articles?since=<ISO time>` → kept for app builds before `after`, which send the publication time of the first article they show: returns articles with a higher `feedSeq` than the newest article published at or before that time (the one those builds show first), so late articles count once and only when they are new.
- `GET /articles/:id` → `Article` shown in the feed, or 404 (hidden articles, and articles not released yet, too).
- `lang` (#46) on both: `en`, `uk`, `ru`, `es` or `de` (the list is `LANGS` in `server/src/translator/languages.ts`, plus `en`). `en`, or no `lang`, is the English feed above, unchanged. With any other, consistency comes first: an article shows in that language only when its translation is finished, never as English mixed in.
  - `GET /articles?lang=uk&limit=20&cursor=<c>` → `{ "items": [Article], "nextCursor": "…" | null, "top": [Article], "translationsDelayed": false }`: the English feed above, the same articles in the same order (`feedSeq`), keeping those with a shown translation into that language. `cursor`, `nextCursor`, `after=<feedSeq>` and `since` work exactly as in English, and a cursor is valid in every language (the same positions). `top` (first page only) is the English top stories that are translated into that language, in the same order, so it may have fewer. `translationsDelayed` is `true` while the language is paused (automatically, its provider down for more than 2 hours, or by hand) or turned off: its newest articles may be missing for a while, and the app can say so. It is only in language feeds.
  - `GET /articles/:id?lang=uk` → the translated `Article`, or 404 when the article is not released or not translated into that language: not yet, never (after 3 unusable answers), or no longer (its English was summarized again and the new English got 3 unusable answers, so the old translation no longer matches it).
- Filters on `GET /articles` (#67, #68), each optional, combinable with `lang`, `limit`, `cursor`, `after` and `since`. Without them the feed is all countries, all topics, exactly as above.
  - Where, at most one of:
    - `country=XX` (2 capital letters, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2): only that country's sources, the articles whose `country` is `XX` (Germany: `DE` only; the EU institutions' decisions are never mixed in). `country=EU` is the EU institutions' own decisions (not offered in the menu: they are in the European Union group). A code with no sources gives an empty feed.
    - `group=<id>`: a group of countries, its member countries' sources together, plus the EU institutions' where the group includes the EU. The groups are `GROUPS` in `server/src/api/filters.ts`, the one place they are defined, with their members among our source countries (memberships read on the organizations' official pages, 16 Oct):

      | `id` | Name | Member countries | EU institutions (`EU`) |
      | --- | --- | --- | --- |
      | `eu` | European Union | CZ, DE, ES, FR, IT, PL | yes |
      | `g7` | G7 | CA, DE, FR, GB, IT, JP, US | yes |
      | `g20` | G20 | AR, AU, BR, CA, CN, DE, FR, GB, ID, IN, IT, JP, KR, MX, SA, TR, US, ZA | yes |
      | `nato` | NATO | CA, CZ, DE, ES, FR, GB, IT, PL, TR, US | no |

      Russia is not a source country (#42), so it is in no group; Saudi Arabia has no source yet, and the G20 shows it once it has one. BRICS waits until its membership is confirmed. A new group, or a new member, is a change to `GROUPS` and to this table.
    - `for=XX` (2 capital letters), For you (#170): what concerns a reader in country `XX`, which the app works out on the phone (Settings → My country, else the phone's Region setting, read from iOS, else `GET /locate`) and the server never stores (like every request, it can sit a few days in Cloudflare's request logs: `observability` is on in `server/wrangler.jsonc`). Every released decision of `XX`'s own sources, whatever its importance, and every one that concerns `XX` wherever it was published (`article_countries`, #230: a US tariff on Canadian cars reaches a reader in Canada); the EU institutions' too when `XX` is an EU member (all 27, `EU_MEMBERS` in `server/src/api/filters.ts`, also those without sources of their own); and from every other country (the EU institutions' too, for a reader outside the EU) only what matters: importance 3 or more, at most 2 a day (the `publishedAt` date, UTC) from one country, the most important first, then the first released. In `top`, a decision that concerns `XX` (its source's country, or one in `article_countries`) gets a point more, and for an EU member one that concerns the EU half a point ("Top stories for you"); within a source country the same score ranks them. It reads about one and a half times a group's rows: the world's picks go through the feed's 7 days, in the page and again in `top`.
  - `topic=<id>`: one id of the topic list below; the articles the AI gave that topic. Combines with `country`, `group` or `for`.

Managing the queue is `server/scripts/queue.mjs` (`docs/queue.md`).
- `POST /v1/chat` (#146) → the AI agent's answer about one decision, for subscribers only. The body is JSON:
  ```json
  { "articleId": "01JA2B3C4D5E6F7G8H9J", "lang": "uk",
    "messages": [ { "role": "user", "text": "Чи стосується це мене?" } ],
    "transaction": "<the subscription's signed transaction from StoreKit 2>" }
  ```
  - `messages`: the conversation so far, oldest first, ending with the reader's new question (`role` `user`); at most 20 (10 questions and their answers), a question up to 500 characters, an earlier answer (`role` `assistant`) up to 4,000. The server keeps none of it.
  - `lang`: the app's language, one of the API's `lang` values: the agent gets the summary in it. It answers in the language the reader writes in (Roman, 29 Oct), and in this one when that is unclear (a number, "ok"; Yurii's review of #226).
  - `transaction`: the subscription's JWS signed transaction as StoreKit 2 gives it (the app sends its latest, from the current entitlements). The Worker checks it itself, with no Apple key: the certificate chain up to Apple Root CA - G3, the ES256 signature, our bundle ID `com.officialnews.app`, the product `CHAT_PRODUCT_ID` (`com.officialnews.app.agent.monthly`), and the environment, `Production` or `Sandbox` (TestFlight and App Review buy in the sandbox). Its `originalTransactionId` is the subscription.
  - Header `X-Chat-Sandbox-Key` (#146): a Sandbox purchase costs nothing, so until the chat is released a `Sandbox` subscription or pack gets an answer only when this header equals one of two Worker secrets, compared in constant time: `CHAT_SANDBOX_KEY`, which only Roman's local builds send (Roman, 28 Oct), or `CHAT_SANDBOX_KEY_TESTFLIGHT`, which the TestFlight workflow builds into `testflight` builds, never `store` ones, so the team's testers try the chat for free (Roman and Yurii, 29 Oct). Without the secrets no Sandbox purchase gets an answer. The same for `/v1/chat/status` and `/v1/chat/credits`. Sandbox questions take the same daily limit, and a subscription gets one free pack a day (`/v1/chat/credits`).
  - The subscription is active while its row in `subscriptions` (Database, below) is not revoked and its latest known expiry is in the future. A transaction that expires later than the row (a renewal whose notification has not come yet) updates the row; a revoked row stays closed whatever the transaction says.
  - `200`: `Content-Type: text/plain; charset=utf-8`, the answer streamed as it is written; `X-Chat-Remaining`, the questions left after this one (today's and bought ones together, at this article's units), and `X-Chat-Units`, how many of the limit one question about this article takes. The agent (OpenAI `CHAT_MODEL`, at most `CHAT_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS` tokens, `server/src/chat/`) is an analyst (Roman, 29 Oct): it gives insight into the decision for the reader's own case (what changes, who gains and who loses, what comes next), asks the reader about their situation when the answer depends on it, and has no set length. It answers the question in its first sentence or two, then adds only what changes the reader's understanding, with no headings or labelled sections, bold and lists sparingly, and no closing offers of what else it could explain; when the answer depends on the reader's case, it ends with one short question about it instead (Roman, 30 Oct). It builds on the article's original text (`articles.text`, up to 20,000 characters) and its summary in that language (English when the translation is missing), then on real facts and precedents it is sure of, and makes clear which is which. It never makes up facts, numbers, dates, names or examples, says when it is not sure, marks its own reading and forecasts, gives no opinion on parties or politicians, and for a step with legal, tax, medical or money consequences says what to check and with whom. The app shows the official link under each answer, so the answer needs none.
  - Errors, JSON as below: `400` (a bad body), `402` (no active subscription: no transaction, a bad signature, another app or product, expired, refunded or revoked; the app shows the paywall), `403` (a Sandbox subscription without a valid `X-Chat-Sandbox-Key`: `{ "error": "Not open to test purchases" }`; nothing is asked or kept), `404` (the article is not in the feed), `429` (no questions left: `{ "error": "…", "resetsAt": "<ISO time>", "units": 1 }`, `resetsAt` when today's come back), `503` (no `OPENAI_API_KEY`, OpenAI did not answer, or another question of the same subscription held its limit for over 2 seconds; that question is not counted).
  - The limit (Roman, 27 Oct; `server/src/chat/usage.ts`) counts what the answers cost, so a subscriber never costs more than a set sum. Its unit, a question, is $0.002 of measured OpenAI cost (`QUESTION_MICROS`, 2,000 micro-dollars). A subscription has 20 a day (`DAILY_QUESTIONS`: $0.04 a UTC day, so at most $1.20 a month); unused ones do not carry over. Bought ones (packs, `/v1/chat/credits`) are spent after them and never expire. A question about an article takes `units` of them by its official text (`unitsFor`: about 800 tokens of rules, summary and question, the text at 3.5 characters a token, an answer of 250 tokens, at GPT-5.4 mini's $0.75, $0.075 and $4.50 per 1M tokens; 13,000 characters take 2, 20,000 take 3). The question's units are taken before OpenAI is asked, today's budget first, then the wallet, by one question of a subscription at a time (`chat_locks`, below; #219), so questions sent together never pass the check together; they are given back when OpenAI does not answer, so an answer the reader leaves still counts and one OpenAI never gave does not; once the stream ends, its real cost (`stream_options.include_usage`) takes the estimate's place, the difference given back where it was taken from, or taken. Each question logs its tokens and cost, never its text.
- `POST /v1/chat/status` (#146) → `{ "questionsLeft": 17, "boughtQuestions": 50, "units": 1, "resetsAt": "<ISO time>" }`: what a subscriber has left for questions about one article, before asking, at that article's units. The body is `{ "articleId": "…", "transaction": "<signed transaction>" }`. `402` without an active subscription, `403` a Sandbox one without a valid `X-Chat-Sandbox-Key`, `404` for an article outside the feed.
- `POST /v1/chat/credits` (#146) → `{ "credited": true, "boughtQuestions": 50 }`: a pack of questions bought in the app, credited to the wallet of the subscription of `transaction`, once, however often the app sends it (`credited` false the second time). The body is `{ "pack": "<the pack's signed transaction>", "transaction": "<the subscription's>" }`. The packs, consumables (`PACK_QUESTIONS`, `server/src/appstore/products.ts`): `com.officialnews.app.agent.questions50` (+50 questions, $0.99) and `com.officialnews.app.agent.questions200` (+200, $2.99). A pack already refunded is not credited, also when Apple's refund came before the app sent it. `400` for anything but a pack of this app, `402` without a subscription's transaction, `403` a Sandbox pack or subscription without a valid `X-Chat-Sandbox-Key`, or a pack from another store than the subscription (`{ "error": "The pack and the subscription come from different stores" }`: never a free pack into a paid subscription's wallet, nor the other way), `429` a second Sandbox pack for one subscription on the same UTC day (`{ "error": "One test pack a day", "resetsAt": "<ISO time>" }`; Yurii's review of #217): the app keeps it unfinished, so StoreKit gives it back at the next start and it is credited on a later day.
- `POST /v1/appstore/notifications` (#146) → `200`: Apple's App Store Server Notifications V2 about the subscription (the URL set in App Store Connect, for production and sandbox alike). The body is `{ "signedPayload": "<JWS>" }`, checked like the transaction above; one that fails the check gets `400`. A notification that carries a signed transaction of the subscription updates its row as a transaction from the app would (`subscriptions`, Database): a renewal moves it to the new period, `REFUND` and `REVOKE` bring the revocation, so the chat closes at once, and `REFUND_REVERSED` alone takes it away. For a pack of questions, `REFUND` and `REVOKE` take it back out of its wallet (below zero when its questions were already asked) and `REFUND_REVERSED` puts it back. A pack refunded before the app sent it gets a `chat_credits` row with no subscription, so it is never credited, until `REFUND_REVERSED` removes that row. Every type gets `200`, so Apple does not send it again; `CONSUMPTION_REQUEST` is only logged until an In-App Purchase key lets the server answer it (`docs/setup.md`).
- `POST /v1/support/messages` (#220) → a reader's message to us, from the app (Settings → Contact & Support → Write to us). No login. The body is JSON:
  ```json
  { "device": "<the phone's random id>", "text": "Hello…", "lang": "uk", "app": "1.0.0 (47)", "push": "<FCM token>" }
  ```
  - `device`: 32 to 128 characters of `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -`, a random id the app makes once and keeps. The server stores only its SHA-256, never the id.
  - `text`: 1 to 2,000 characters after trimming, counted as Unicode code points (an emoji is 1 or more).
  - `lang`: `en`, `uk`, `ru`, `es` or `de`, the app's language: the push for our reply is in it. `app`: the app's version, at most 32 characters (may be empty).
  - `push` (optional): the phone's FCM token, at most 4,096 characters. Kept on the conversation for the push on reply; a message without it keeps the one before.
  - `200` `{ "thread": Thread }`, the conversation with the new message.
  - `409` `{ "error": "Waiting for our reply", "writeAfter": "<ISO time>" }`: the reader cannot write yet (rules below).
  - `429` `{ "error": "Too many messages today", "resetsAt": "<ISO time>" }`: 5 messages already came from this network address this UTC day; `resetsAt` is the next UTC midnight. The address is counted as a SHA-256 of a random salt for the UTC day and `CF-Connecting-IP` (an IPv6 address by its /64 network, its first 4 groups), never stored as it is; the hash and the day's salt are deleted after the day.
  - `400` `{ "error": "…" }` for a bad body.
- `GET /v1/support/thread` (#220), with the header `X-Support-Device: <device>` (a header, so the id never lands in a URL or a log) → `{ "thread": Thread | null }`: the phone's latest conversation, or `null` when it has none. `400` without a valid header.
  - `Thread`: `{ "status": "waiting" | "answered" | "closed", "messages": [ { "from": "reader" | "team", "text": "…", "at": "<ISO time>" } ], "canWrite": true, "writeAfter": "<ISO time>" | null }`. Messages oldest first. `writeAfter` is set only when `canWrite` is false.
  - The rules: a reader can write when they have no conversation, the latest message is ours, the conversation is closed, or 7 days have passed since their latest message. Writing after our reply, or after 7 days, continues the same conversation (`waiting` again); writing after it was closed, or with none, starts a new one. When they cannot write, `writeAfter` is their latest message plus 7 days. The check and the insert are one statement, so two sends at once never both pass.
  - Both are never cached (`no-store`), like every response but the lists and articles below.
- Caching (#121): `GET /articles`, `/topics` and `/countries` answer with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60`, and `GET /articles/:id` (any `lang`) with `public, max-age=300`. Cloudflare's cache in front of the Worker (Workers Caching, `cache` in `server/wrangler.jsonc`) keeps them, keyed by the path and the whole query string, so every reader of the same URL gets the same answer: a newly released article can take up to a minute to appear in the feed and in `after=`, and a changed one up to 5 minutes. Every other response is `Cache-Control: no-store`: `/health`, `/queue`, the `/v1/` paths, and every error, so a 404 for an article not released or not translated yet is never kept. The same parameters in another order are another URL, so a client keeps one order (the app builds them in a fixed order, `app/lib/core/api_client.dart`).
- Errors are JSON `{ "error": "<message>" }`: 400 for a bad `limit`, `cursor`, `after`, `since`, `lang`, `country` (not 2 capital letters: `de`, `DEU`), `group` (not one of the groups: `G7`, `brics`), `country` and `group` together, or `topic` (not one of the list), the message saying what is expected, 404, 405 for any method other than GET (other than POST on the `/v1/` paths), 500.

## Push notifications

Breaking decisions only (#62, after the MVP): a push exists to bring a reader back to the app with something useful. The server sends them through Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM HTTP v1, project `official-news-48e16`), as the service account in the Worker secret `FCM_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` (`docs/setup.md`). Code: `server/src/push/`.

- Topics: one per feed language, `breaking_en`, `breaking_uk`, `breaking_ru`, `breaking_es`, `breaking_de` (`breaking_<lang>`, with the API's `lang` values). The app subscribes to the topic of its feed language and moves to the new one when the language changes.
- The message:
  ```json
  { "message": { "topic": "breaking_uk",
                 "notification": { "title": "<headline in that language>", "body": "<short summary, cut to about 110 characters at a word, with …>" },
                 "data": { "articleId": "01JA2B3C4D5E6F7G8H9J", "lang": "uk" },
                 "apns": { "payload": { "aps": { "sound": "default" } } } } }
  ```
  On a tap the app opens `data.articleId` in `data.lang` (`GET /articles/:id?lang=uk`; no `lang` for `en`). Both data values are strings. The headline and short summary are the article's (`en`) or its published translation's.
- What goes out, per language (English, and each language enabled in `translation_langs`; Yurii, 16 Oct):
  - Breaking: a released decision (`feedSeq` set, published in the feed's 7 days), never an event (`decisionType` `event`, #230: readers turned on official decisions), with `importance` 4 or 5, released (`addedAt`) in the last 24 hours, never pushed in that language. In a language other than English only when its translation of the current English is published (state `published`), and in that language.
  - At most 2 a local day per language, and at least 1: from the daily slot, 18:00 local, a language that had no push that day gets its top story of the day, whatever its importance (highest `importance`, then newest `feedSeq`, among the decisions released in the last 24 hours and not pushed before; the last 24 hours, not the local day, so a decision from the evening before that got no push can be it). When several decisions qualify, the highest importance goes first, then the newest.
  - Quiet hours: nothing from 21:00 to 08:00 local; a decision released at night goes out in the morning while it is less than 24 hours old.
  - At least 3 hours between two pushes in the same language.
  - Each language's main time zone (`TIME_ZONES` in `server/src/push/index.ts`): `en` Europe/London, `uk` and `ru` Europe/Kyiv, `es` Europe/Madrid, `de` Europe/Berlin. The local day of the cap runs from local midnight.
- Each translator run ends with the push step (`runPush`): at most one push per language a run. While the translator is not running (no run in 10 minutes, as for the release: its new trigger can take an hour to start), each summarizer run ends with it instead; the summarizer reads one row (`step_runs`) to know. Each push is claimed in `push_log` before the request to FCM, in one statement that checks the article, the cap and the spacing again (so two overlapping runs cannot both send), and completed after it by FCM's answer:
  - Sent: FCM's message name.
  - Refused (FCM refused the message itself: HTTP 400, or another 4xx below): the error, `refused` 1; never tried again in that language. Counts for the 3-hour spacing but not for the daily cap: it surely did not go out.
  - No answer (a network error, the 10-second timeout): the error. FCM may have sent it, so it counts for the spacing and the daily cap, and is never tried again.
  - Not now (HTTP 401, 403, 408, 429 or 5xx: the token, the permission, the quota or FCM itself, nothing about the message): the run stops sending, deletes its claims that were not sent, so a later run sends those articles, forgets the token on a 401, and pauses the push step for 10 minutes, or FCM's `Retry-After` when longer.

  A run cut off between the claim and its completion leaves a claim, which counts as sent: nothing is ever sent twice. Without `FCM_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` the step does nothing (no request, no database read) and logs `push skipped: FCM_SERVICE_ACCOUNT not set`; a push error never breaks the translator, the summarizer or the release. While every language is in its quiet hours the step reads nothing; a run with nothing to send reads about 60 D1 rows.
- The access token: a JWT (RS256, signed with the service account's key through WebCrypto, scope `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/firebase.messaging`) exchanged at `https://oauth2.googleapis.com/token`, kept in D1 (`kv_cache`) until 5 minutes before it expires (about 55 minutes), so most runs neither sign nor ask Google. Asked for only when a run has something to send; FCM refusing it (HTTP 401) drops it, so the run after the pause gets a new one. Without a token nothing is claimed, and the step pauses for 10 minutes too, so a refused key is tried at most every 10 minutes, not every run. The pause is one `kv_cache` row, read with the token: while it lasts a run neither signs nor sends.
- Logs one `push run` line a run: `pushes` (language, article, `breaking` or `daily`, FCM's message name, or the error with `outcome` `refused`, `unknown` or `retry`) and `skipped` (why each other language sent nothing: quiet hours, language off, daily cap, spacing, nothing breaking, no access token, paused until a time and why, claimed by another run).
- The push for our reply (#220): when we reply on the admin page, the push step of every summarizer and translator run (about every minute) sends it to the phone's own FCM token from its latest message, at most 3 a run, at any hour: `{ "message": { "token": "<FCM token>", "notification": { "title": "Outer News replied to your message", "body": "<our reply, cut to about 110 characters>" }, "data": { "type": "support" }, "apns": { "payload": { "aps": { "sound": "default" } } } } }`. The title is in the conversation's `lang` (`REPLY_TITLE` in `server/src/support/cron.ts`). On a tap the app opens its message screen. A conversation without a token gets no push; a token FCM refuses is forgotten. When FCM cannot take it just then, it waits for a later run and the push step pauses, as above.

| `/favicon.ico`, `/apple-touch-icon*.png`, anything else (`/{lang}/local` is gone; reserved for later: `/.well-known/apple-app-site-association`, sitemaps) | | 404 |
| Any method but GET and HEAD | | 405 |

**URLs of a place.** One segment after the language is a place to read the decisions of, as in an article's `/{lang}/{cc}/…`: a country's code or a group's id, never both forms for one view. A group's id is never a country's code (`eu` is ISO 3166's code reserved for the EU itself, so `/{lang}/eu` is the European Union group, which also holds the EU institutions' decisions; `country=EU` alone is not a page) nor an information page's name; a test keeps them apart, so a new group needs an id that is neither (an African Union would not be `au`, Australia's code).

The filter chips (#67, #68, as the app's, Yurii 16 Oct; `server/src/site/pages/filters.ts`): **Where** (All countries; then every group of `GET /countries?lang=`, always, in its order: European Union, G7, G20, NATO; then its countries by name) and **Topics** (All topics, then `GET /topics?lang=&country=|group=`, busiest first, no counts), plus the current country or topic, checked, when the list does not have it. Each is a `<details>` with plain links (no script); each link is a list's one URL, keeping the other filter. A chip is the grey chip; once its filter is on it stays grey and is short, its label or icon and chevron red: a country's code (`GB`), a group's short name in the page's language (`EU`, `ЄС`, `ЕС`, `UE`; `G7`, `G20`; `NATO`, `НАТО`, `OTAN`: the app's `filterGroupEUShort` and the groups' names), a topic's icon (`TOPIC_ICONS`, one per topic of the list); a screen reader hears the full name ("Country: United Kingdom", "Countries: G7", "Topic: Economy"). The menus show full names, and the Topics menu each topic's icon before its name. A menu opens over the list under the chips, so neither chip moves. An empty filtered list shows "No decisions here in the last 7 days." with "Show all countries" (to the list without the place) and/or "Show all topics" (without the topic). The canonical, the hreflang alternates, the language links and the paging links keep the filters; x-default is the English list, or `/` for all countries, all topics, page 1.

What it reads: only through the API's own handler (`handleApi`, in the same request): `GET /articles?limit=20&lang=<lang>&country=<CC>|group=<id>&topic=<id>&cursor=<c>` (no `lang` for English, no `country`, `group` or `topic` without that filter; the cursor is the API's `encodeCursor(before)`) and `GET /articles/:id` with each `lang` of the site; on a list's first page also `GET /countries?lang=` and `GET /topics?lang=&country=|group=`, except for a country or a group without a topic whose first page has no next page: it holds every article the menu counts, so its menu is `rankTopics` (`server/src/api/filters.ts`, the function `GET /topics` uses) of those articles. No SQL of its own, no other binding than D1, no AI. So the site shows exactly what the API shows, and a change to the API's rules reaches the site with its next deploy (after the api's). It relies on `GET /articles/:id` serving a released article of any age (no 7-day limit): article pages are permanent because of it.

Every response: `X-Robots-Tag: noindex` (a page's robots meta tag always has the same value, from `robotsFor()`; robots.txt disallows every path, so crawlers read neither until the domain), `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src '<sha256 of the inline CSS>'; img-src data:; base-uri 'none'; form-action 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Language` on pages, `Cache-Control` and `Cloudflare-CDN-Cache-Control` per kind of page (`server/src/site/respond.ts`), no cookie and no `Vary`. Nothing that differs per reader is in a page or a redirect (Local, the one answer that did, is gone). Every absolute URL starts with `SITE_ORIGIN`, never the request's host. A page loads nothing else: one request per page view.

The countries the article concerns, from the summarizer's `countries` (at most 5), or its source's country when it names none; the articles summarized before #230 got their source's country. Primary key (`article_id`, `country`), index on (`country`, `article_id`): For you asks by them.

One row per article and language (primary key `article_id, lang`), which is also its place in the translation queue. A row exists from the moment the translator queues the article for that language, and is finished once its texts (and `lang_seq`) are set.
  - `lang_seq`: the order in which that language's first translations were stored (the language's highest plus 1, set with `translated_at`; neither ever changes; a unique index on (`lang`, `lang_seq`) refuses a number given twice). Since migration `0010` it orders nothing: every language feed follows `articles.feed_seq`. The column stays because the all-or-nothing CHECK reads it.
  - All or nothing: a CHECK constraint lets a row have either none of headline, short_summary, summary, model, source_summarized_at, lang_seq and translated_at, or all of them (the three texts not empty).
  - The queue (migration `0009`, was `0008`): state (below), state_at (when it entered that state), next_attempt_at (`retrying`: not claimed before), last_error (why it is `retrying`, `failed` or `skipped`), published_at (when the text shown was published; `translated_at` is the first time), outage_retries (tries no provider answered, for the backoff; never an attempt). `queued_at` is set while it waits (`queued`, `updating`, `translating`, `retrying`) and NULL otherwise.
  - States: `queued` (waiting for its first translation) → `translating` (claimed by a run, `claimed_at`) → `published` (the translation of the current English is shown). `retrying` (an unusable answer, `attempts` + 1, waits 10 then 20 minutes; or no provider answered, no attempt, waits 2, 4, 8 … up to 60 minutes) until `next_attempt_at`. `failed` after 3 unusable answers for one English, with the reason; not shown until the English changes, except a text of that same English (requeued by hand, its redo failed), which still matches it and stays shown. `updating`: the English was summarized again; the old translation is shown until the new one is published. A claim older than 10 minutes goes back to `queued` (or `updating`). `skipped`: the article left the feed (hidden, older than 7 days) or its English is being redone, with why; a new English queues it again.
- `translation_langs` (migration `0009`): lang (primary key), enabled, paused, updated_at; paused_reason and down_since (migration `0010`). The switch per language: only an enabled language is queued and waited for by the release. A paused one is queued, but the release no longer waits for it. Paused by hand (`queue.mjs pause`, paused_reason `manual`), nothing of it is claimed until `resume`. Paused automatically (paused_reason `auto: provider down since <time>`, when down_since is more than 2 hours old), it is still probed with one claim a run, and its first answer unpauses it. down_since: the first try of the current outage that no provider answered (or the first run without its provider key); cleared by any answer. Migration `0009` enabled `uk`, `ru`, `es` and `de`; `0010` turns `de` off (Z.ai blocks Cloudflare Workers).
- `step_runs` (migration `0011`): step (primary key), at. When a scheduled step last ran: the translator writes `translator` at the start of every run, and the release waits for translations only while that is less than 10 minutes old (`TRANSLATOR_ALIVE_MINUTES`).
- `translation_events` (migration `0009`): article_id, lang, state, at, attempts, detail (the model for `published`, the reason for `retrying`, `failed`, `skipped` and a claim that ran out). Every change of state of `article_translations`, written by a trigger, whoever makes it (the translator or `queue.mjs`); kept 30 days.
- `translation_counts` (migration `0009`): lang, state, n. How many translations each language has in each state, kept by triggers on `article_translations`, for `GET /queue`.

Written before the request to FCM and completed after it; a row with neither `message_id` nor `error` is a run that was cut off, and counts as sent. ## Scheduled job

Four cron triggers (`server/wrangler.jsonc`), each its own run with its own free-plan budget of at most 50 outside requests and 10 ms of CPU. Every minute (`* * * * *`), `runFetcher(env)` (item 6) takes the source with the oldest `last_checked_at`; right after it, `recordChecks(env, checks)` (item 8) gives the checked sources their status. Every odd minute (`1-59/2 * * * *`), `runSummarizer(env, { maxArticles: 5 })` (item 7) makes one AI call per article, then releases what is ready into the feed (everything at once while the translator is not running), and, while the translator is not running, ends with the push step. Every even minute (`0-58/2 * * * *`), `runTranslator(env, { maxTranslations: TRANSLATIONS_PER_RUN })` (#46) makes up to 4 translations: one article in each language, also when the other languages have nothing to do or are paused; then it releases the articles that are ready into the feed, and ends with the push step (`runPush`, #62, Push notifications above): at most one push per language. Once a day at 03:17 UTC (`17 3 * * *`), `runCleanup(env)` (item 8) clears old texts, and the AI chat's daily counts older than a week (#146). `server/src/index.ts` picks the step by `controller.cron` (`CRONS`; a test checks it knows every trigger in `wrangler.jsonc`). A newly added Cloudflare schedule can take about an hour to start firing (#50: the summarizer's started about 55 minutes after the deploy), so check `wrangler tail` an hour after the deploy before calling it broken. Locally: `npx wrangler dev --test-scheduled`, then open `/__scheduled?cron=1-59/2+*+*+*+*` for the summarizer, `/__scheduled?cron=0-58/2+*+*+*+*` for the translator or `/__scheduled?cron=17+3+*+*+*` for the cleanup (any other `cron` runs the fetcher).

What the fetcher writes (`server/src/fetcher/`):
- Only items published in the last 7 days; an item already stored (same `url`) is never saved again. Items without a date are skipped, and a time later than the fetch is stored as the fetch time.
- Only links on the source's own site (its host or a subdomain, or a host in `config.allowedHosts`), always as `https`.
- A run claims its sources first (sets `last_checked_at` in the same statement that picks them), so a run cut off by a limit never blocks the others.
- New articles: `is_news` `NULL` (the AI decides), `original_language` from the source, `text` up to 20,000 characters with `text_expires_at` 30 days after `fetched_at`, `image_url` from the list or the item page's preview image (never the item page's when the source's `config.pageImage` is `false`: a source whose only picture is its emblem); an address that is a site's root (`https://vlada.gov.cz/`, given by pages without a photo) is not a picture and is left out.
- Where `text` comes from (#71): the list (feed content or description, the page's `summary`, the API's `text`); when that is under 1,000 characters (a teaser), the source's `config.fullText` steps when it has them (a JSON data endpoint, or a page that links to the text, on the source's own or allowed official hosts; read also when `config.itemPages` is `false`, robots.txt checked for each URL), else the item page's main text when the source allows item pages (`config.textSelector`, else `main`, `article`, `body`; picture captions left out). A page is read up to 20,000 characters of text. The read text is stored when it is longer than the list text and holds it; when it does not hold it, the list text, a line break, then the read text (cut at 20,000 characters). A teaser waits for a later run's page budget (2 reads a run; a text of 20,000 characters counts as both) instead of being saved. A site answering 429 or 503, or not reachable, is not asked again in that run; another 5xx or no answer in time holds only that item (after 3 such pages in a run the site counts as busy). Either way the item is asked again on later checks while under 24 hours old, then saved with the teaser. A saved item still out of the feed is read again (#227), at most once an hour while its list keeps it in the feed's 7 days, when its text never came or the list gives it another title (a publisher's rename: La Moncloa lists a council's summary under a placeholder first); when its text or title changed, it is summarized again (`summarized_at` back to NULL, attempts to 0). An item in the feed is never read again. `text` is NULL when nothing but the title exists (the summarizer then works from the title).
- A date without a time is stored at 12:00 UTC (or the fetch time if that is earlier) with `date_only = 1`.
- `url` may end in `#…` when one link holds several items (bill stages); the part before `#` is the page.
- Every check sets `last_checked_at`; `last_item_at` is the newest item seen. `runFetcher` returns one `SourceCheck` per source (`ok`, `httpStatus`, `found`, `saved`, `waiting`, `undated`, `offSite`, `error`), which source health (item 8) can use, and logs it as JSON.

What source health writes (`server/src/health/`, item 8):
- After every check, the source's `status`: `active` when the check worked and the source listed an item from the last 14 days; `stale` when it worked but nothing listed is newer; `broken` after 3 failed checks in a row (no answer, robots.txt, an HTTP error); `moved` the same, when the list page answers 404 or 410. One failed check keeps the status. `failed_checks` counts the failures in a row, `last_ok_at` is the last check that worked, `last_error` why the last one failed.
- `blocked` is set by people (`docs/sources.csv`) and never changed by health. The fetcher keeps checking `broken`, `moved` and `stale` sources, so a source that recovers turns `active` on its next good check.
- Once a day: `text` is cleared where `text_expires_at` has passed (30 days after fetching). Title, summary and link stay; a later re-summary of such an article works from its title.
- Once a day, too (#220): readers' conversations closed, or with no new message, for 90 days are deleted with their messages, and the address hashes of past days are cleared.

What the summarizer writes (`server/src/summarizer/`, rules in `docs/style-guide.md`):
- It takes articles with no `summarized_at`, newest `fetched_at` first, also those with only a title.
- One call to Workers AI (`@cf/google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it` since #63) fills `headline`, `headline_type`, `short_summary`, `summary`, `decision_type`, `topics`, `importance`, `reach`, `is_news`, `summarized_at` and `model`, and the article's rows in `article_countries` (its `countries`: up to 5 codes of the countries whose people it concerns most, the source's when it names none; #230); since #133 `tags` is stored as `[]` and `impact` as NULL. The headline kind asked for is picked before the call (`headlineKind` in `prompt.ts`): from the article id, so a retry asks for the same kind, among `question` and `consequence`, and `contrast` too when the text gives two amounts or percentages. Before it is stored, a news answer is checked (`checks.ts`; an answer marked not news is stored unchecked): a number of two digits or more that the article does not give (other than the fetch year and the years next to it, for dates in another calendar; the day and month of a date written 13.10.2026 or 2026.10.19, full-width digits, the full value of "5 tys.", "1천" or "3 mln", and "27" for "2026-2027" count as given) makes it unusable, as does a summary not in English (under 10% common English words such as "the", "of", "and", in a text of 20 words or more). A ruled-out word ("aims to", "could", "significant" and the others in `docs/style-guide.md`), 12 or more words in a row copied from the article into the summary or the short summary, a headline over 90 characters or a summary over 1,000 (the prompt asks for 80 and for 600 to 900) make it unusable too, except on its last try, where it is stored (clipped to the limits) and logged with a warning, so a decision is not hidden for one word. `is_news` 1 makes it a decision waiting for its release, which gives it its `feed_seq`, in every language at once. The summarizer gives no `feed_seq`; its scheduled run ends with the release (logged as `summarizer release`), so decisions reach the feed also while the translator is not running.
- An unusable answer adds 1 to `summary_attempts`; after 3 the article stays hidden. An error from Workers AI itself (an outage, or the day's free allocation used up) costs no attempt and ends the run.
- A run claims its articles first (sets `summary_claimed_at` in the same statement that picks them), so an overlapping run takes other ones. A claim older than 10 minutes is from a run that was cut off and no longer counts; an error from Workers AI frees the claims at once.

What the translator writes (`server/src/translator/`). Consistency first, speed second: an article shows in a language only once its translation is complete and checked, and the feed is the same in every language (release together).
- The queue (`queue.ts`, states above): every news article in the feed (`is_news = 1`, summary fields and `summarized_at` filled, last 7 days) enters it once per enabled language and English version, also for a paused language or one without its provider key, so nothing is lost. Each run, in one batch before any AI call: drops the state log older than 30 days, sends claims older than 10 minutes back to `queued`, queues the news whose `summarized_at` is newer than the newest it has queued for that language (less 10 minutes, for a summary written a moment after its time) through an index, marks a language without its provider key as down, and claims, in one statement, one translation per language (`CLAIMS_PER_LANGUAGE`) in each language that is enabled, not paused by hand and has a provider: of what waits (`queued`, `updating`, or `retrying` that is due), an article not released yet first (the whole feed waits for it), then the oldest English, so a language catching up after a pause holds back no new article, and one language's backlog or outage never holds back another. Of the first 4 waiting rows it reads per language (`CLAIM_WINDOW`, plus 4 of articles not released yet), those whose article left the feed become `skipped` and take no place in the run. A language with nothing queued yet (a new one) queues the feed's 7 days. Each language's waiting rows are read through their own partial index, so a run reads about what it takes, also when a paused language has a backlog: a run with nothing to do reads about 80 D1 rows (the switches and the release included), however many articles the feed holds, and one article in four languages writes about 130 (the state log and the counts included).
- One AI call per article and language: headline, short summary and summary from English into the language, nothing else. The other fields are never translated.
- Providers per language, in order, from one constant (`TRANSLATORS` in `index.ts`): Ukrainian `groq:qwen/qwen3.8-27b`, then `groq:openai/gpt-oss-120b`; Russian `groq:openai/gpt-oss-120b`, then `groq:qwen/qwen3.8-27b`; Spanish `groq:openai/gpt-oss-20b`, then `groq:openai/gpt-oss-120b`; German `zai:glm-4.5-flash`, then `zai:glm-4.7-flash` (German is off by default: Z.ai answers requests from Cloudflare Workers with an HTTP 405 block page). `max_tokens` 700, temperature 0.2, JSON mode. Groq needs the Worker secret `GROQ_API_KEY`, Z.ai `ZAI_API_KEY`; without its key a language waits in the queue and the run logs it under `waiting`. Z.ai (`POST https://api.z.ai/api/paas/v4/chat/completions`, `thinking: {"type": "disabled"}`) gets one request at a time: a run makes its requests one after another. No Workers AI for translations for now (`workers-ai:<model>` works in the same constant if that changes).
- The answer is checked as a whole: valid JSON with all three fields, not cut off, each at most twice the English length (plus 20 characters), the summary with the English's number of paragraphs, and every field in the language (`server/src/translator/prompt.ts`):
  - Ukrainian and Russian, field by field: for Ukrainian none of ы, э, ъ, ё and none of a short list of Russian-only words (и, что, его, как, также, …), and at least one of і, ї, є, ґ in the answer and in every field of 100 letters or more; for Russian none of і, ї, є, ґ and none of a short list of Ukrainian-only words (що, це, його, також, …). Paragraph by paragraph and sentence by sentence: Cyrillic letters in every paragraph, and in every sentence more of them than letters of Latin words in lower case (untranslated English).
  - Spanish and German use the English letters, so the check counts short function words instead (`server/src/translator/words.ts`; no word is on two lists): in no sentence may English or the other of the two have more of them than the language's own; a field of 8 words or more needs at least one of its own; and a field may not be the English unchanged. A function word inside a name does not count: in lower case between capitalised words (`Bank of England`, `Ministerio de Sanidad`, also past a second name particle: `Ursula von der Leyen`, `Sindicatura General de la Nación`), capitalised after the start of a sentence (a name or a German noun: `in Not`), and one of the other language capitalised at the start before a capitalised word (`Los Angeles`, `El Salvador`, `Von der Leyen`, `Die Linke`); an English one at the start counts. Acronyms (`IT`, `WHO`, `UN`) are no function words, and a word the language itself uses is not foreign to it (German `los` and `her`, Spanish `bis`).
  - Names, acronyms and brands, quoted titles, parentheses and links may stay in their own language.
- No missing information (`server/src/translator/numbers.ts`): every number of each English field must be in the same translated field, each by a number of its own ("5 ministers in 5 regions" needs two fives, and the 5 of "3,5" is not a dropped "5"), compared by value, so the language's own format passes: 1,250,000 = 1 250 000, 3.5% = 3,5 %, 11:59pm = 23:59 or 23.59, 5pm = 17:00 or 17, 16.30 hours = 16:30. A word for millions or more after a number is read with it, on both sides, so €2.86 billion = 2.860 millones = 2 860 млн = 2,86 Milliarden (thousands in words are not: 500 тисяч for 500,000 is missing). In all four languages a comma is the decimal mark, so English thousands copied unchanged ("1,500") are missing, except in an identifier after №, Nr., n.º, núm. or No. (Law No. 9,394). In Spanish and German a full stop only groups thousands (1.250.000), so "3.5" copied from the English is missing too, and 17.00 is a time (17.00 Uhr). A date in numbers (15.10.2026, 14/10/2026, 2026-10-14) needs its day, its month (in numbers or by name: 15 жовтня, 15 de octubre, 15. Oktober) and its year; 5575/2026 is two numbers. The prompt asks for the language's number format (`1 250 000; 3,5 %` in Ukrainian and Russian, `1.250.000; 3,5 %` in Spanish and German).
- All or nothing: the three fields, the model and `source_summarized_at` are stored in one statement, which also makes it `published` and gives a first translation its `lang_seq` and `translated_at` (bookkeeping: the feed's order is the article's `feed_seq`). A partial or unusable answer stores nothing.
- English changes: when the article is summarized again, its new `summarized_at` puts it back in the queue with fresh tries, as `updating`. The old translation is served until the new one is published in its place (the article keeps its `feed_seq`), or until the new English has had 3 unusable answers (`failed`): then the article no longer shows in that language, since the old text no longer matches the English, until a later English is translated. A translation is stored only for the English the queue holds, never over a translation of a newer English, and only while the run's claim holds: a requeue made meanwhile (`queue.mjs requeue`) is not undone, and a claim that ran out is not stored twice.
- A provider that fails for a reason that is not the article's (HTTP 429, 5xx, network, quota; for Z.ai also error codes 1302, 1305 and 1234) is skipped for the rest of the run and the next one is tried; when none answers, the translation is `retrying` with a growing wait (2, 4, 8 … up to 60 minutes) and no attempt is counted. Only the run holding a claim changes its state or stores its text.
- An unusable answer (including Z.ai's content filter, error 1301 or `finish_reason` `sensitive`) adds 1 to `attempts`: `retrying` 10, then 20 minutes later; after 3 that English is `failed` with the reason: a new article does not show in that language, and a re-summarized one no longer shows its old translation, until the English changes again (the attempts count per English version, `attempts_source`).
- Release (`release.ts`), at the end of every run, also one with nothing to translate, and of every summarizer run: each article with `is_news = 1` and no `feed_seq` whose translations are ready gets `feed_seq` (MAX + 1, one statement per article, oldest `summarized_at` first, so the English order is kept) and `added_at` = now. Ready: for each language of `LANGS` that is enabled and not paused, its row for the article's current English is `published` or `failed`. Also released at once: every article while the translator has not run for 10 minutes (it records each run in `step_runs`), an article published before the feed's 7 days (shown nowhere) and one without an English summary. A visible row without a number from an older Worker is released this way too, not lost.
- Automatic pause: a language whose providers did not answer (every one down, rate limited or out of quota; or no provider key) is down from that try (`down_since`); any answer, usable or not, clears it. After 2 hours down (`AUTO_PAUSE_HOURS`), the run pauses it (`paused_reason` `auto: provider down since <time>`), and the release stops waiting for it; it is still claimed from, one translation a run, and the first answer unpauses it. Its missing translations then fill in at the articles' `feed_seq`. A pause by hand is never undone by the translator.
- Logs one `translator start` line (what it claimed, how many translations it queued, claims that ran out, any it skipped, languages waiting for a key) before any AI call and one `translator run` line with the result per article and language and the provider used, plus the articles `released`, languages `autoPaused` and `resumed`; a run that only queued, released or skipped something logs one `translator queue` line, and one with no provider key at all `translator skipped`.
