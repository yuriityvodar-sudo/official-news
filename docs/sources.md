# Sources

The list of official sites the fetcher reads lives in [sources.csv](sources.csv): one row per source. Every source was checked by hand on 2026-10-14 (Poland, Czechia, Spain and Ukraine on 2026-10-16, #72; the international organizations on 2026-10-16, below): official (how is in `verified_by` and `evidence`), allowed by `robots.txt`, no bot check on what the fetcher reads (UN press: its item pages have one, so only its feed is read, `itemPages: false`), items and dates in the page itself, and mostly official decisions (laws, bills, decrees, cabinet decisions, meeting agendas and outcomes).

## Changing the list

1. Edit `docs/sources.csv` in a pull request, and give a new source its names in the other languages in `docs/source-names.csv` (below). `npm test` in `server/` checks every row of both.
2. After the merge, the deploy loads it into the live database by itself (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). On your laptop run `npm run sources:local`. Loading adds new rows and updates changed ones; it never touches `status` or check times, and removing a row does not delete the source (mark it `blocked` instead).

3. Removing a row stops the source: loading sets it to `blocked` (the row stays, because its articles point to it). Remove its rows in `docs/source-names.csv` too; the check refuses names of a source that is not in the list. The blocked row keeps its last `config`, names included, so its articles still in the feed keep their translated source name. Ids of the Kickoff seed sources cannot be reused.

Never add media, aggregators or trending lists, and never a site that needs a bot check, CAPTCHA or login to read. This is about what the fetcher reads: a site whose list or feed is open while its item pages have a bot check is read from the list alone (`itemPages: false`, UN press), and the item pages are never opened. Sources are read over `https` only: plain `http` can be altered on the way, and we show the text as official.

**Official gazettes are allowed** (that is where decisions take effect), always with a title filter (`exclude`) for routine entries: exchange and interest rates, price indices, court edicts, company notices, tenders, routine appointments, technical standards. They never reach the AI, so they cost nothing.

## Names in the reader's language (#76)

Cards and the article screen show the source's name ("Federal Government (Bundesregierung) · Germany"). The English name is `name` in `sources.csv`; the names in Ukrainian, Russian, Spanish and German are in [source-names.csv](source-names.csv), one row per source and language: `id,lang,name`, with `lang` one of `uk`, `ru`, `es`, `de` (the API's languages, `LANGS` in `server/src/translator/languages.ts`). It is a separate file so that editing a source never conflicts with editing its names.

- **Adding a source:** add its 4 names in the same pull request. `npm test` fails until every source in `sources.csv` has a name in every language (without one, that language shows the English name), and refuses an id that is not in `sources.csv`, another `lang`, an empty name or a language twice.
- **Changing a name:** edit its row; the deploy loads it (it runs when either file changes). Loading puts a source's names in its `config` as `names` (`{"uk": "…", "ru": "…", "es": "…", "de": "…"}`), and the API sends the one of the reader's language as `source.name` (`docs/contract.md`). Never write `names` in the `config` column of `sources.csv`: the check refuses it, so the names live in one place.
- **How to write one:** the institution's name as that language usually writes it, short enough for one line on a card (the country follows it there). A generic name takes the country, the way that language says it: "Федеральний уряд Німеччини", "Cámara de Diputados de Brasil"; a name that stands on its own stays as it is: "Білий дім", "Comisión Europea". Keep a bracket of the English name when it helps a reader recognise the source: a site (GOV.UK, gov.pl, gov.cn), a well-known seat (Єлисейський палац, Монклоа), or the publication's own short name (DOF, SAIJ, LEGISinfo, Dziennik Ustaw). Leave out one that only repeats the name in the original language or is technical (Bundesregierung, TBMM, Kantei, Sansad, GCIS, Sejm ELI API). Only real institutions, under their real names; Ukrainian uses the apostrophe ’, as the app does.

## International organizations (16 Oct)

International bodies are sources of their own decisions (Yurii, 16 Oct), read with the fetcher and two small additions: the `pageImage` option (no picture from item pages, for the ILO) and the `en` date format (English month names, for the ILO's dates).

- **Codes.** `country` is 2 capital letters for every source. `EU` is the European Union's own bodies (the Commission, the European Central Bank); `UN` is the United Nations and its agencies (UN press, the ILO). Neither is a country: the filters' groups (#86, #87) find an organization's sources by this code, so a "United Nations" group is the sources with `UN`. The app names the code (`countryUN` in `app/lib/l10n`: "United Nations", "ООН", "ООН", "Naciones Unidas", "Vereinte Nationen"). The server filters' names (`COUNTRIES` in `server/src/api/filters.ts`, #86) need a `UN` entry too: its test checks that every country of `docs/sources.csv` has one. An organization whose usual code is a country's (the African Union is not `AU`, which is Australia) takes another code or a field of its own, settled when it is added.
- **European Central Bank** (`eu-ecb-decisions`, country `EU`): its press feed (about 15 items, 2 weeks) mixes press releases, speeches and interviews, without text. The title filter keeps "Monetary policy decisions" (8 a year) and "Decisions taken by the Governing Council" (about monthly); the text is read from the item page (`main .section`). robots.txt asks for 5 seconds between requests (`crawlDelaySeconds`). The feed's links have a double slash (`https://www.ecb.europa.eu//press/…`); they work as they are. Licence: reproduction with the source acknowledged, the statement on each press release page. Checked 16 Oct: no decision in the last 7 days (the latest: Governing Council decisions of 2 October), so nothing is saved until the next one.
- **UN Meetings Coverage and Press Releases** (`un-press-meetings-coverage`, country `UN`): the UN press feed (`https://press.un.org/en/rss.xml`), about 10 items, a day's worth. The title filter `(?i)\b(adopt(s|ing)?|approv(es?|ing)|extend(s|ing)|renew(s|ing)|elect(s|ing)|appoint(s|ing)|recommend(s|ing)|authori[sz](es|ing)|endors(es|ing)|demand(s|ing)|establish(es|ing)|impos(es|ing)|lift(s|ing))\b` keeps the decision verbs of UN press headlines: the Security Council's resolutions and presidential statements, also when the headline leads with what the resolution does (demands, authorizes, imposes or lifts sanctions, establishes, endorses) and ends "…, Adopting Resolution NNNN (YYYY)" (a style recalled from memory, not checked against past headlines), mandate extensions and renewals, the selection of a Secretary-General (the Security Council recommends, the General Assembly appoints; due this autumn), and the General Assembly's and its committees' approvals and elections. The AI hides the rest (a title such as "Fails to Adopt" passes the filter); the feed has about 10 items a day, so a wider filter adds a few AI calls at most. Checked 16 Oct on the saved feed: this filter and the first, narrower one (`adopts?|approves?|extends|renews|elects`) keep the same 2 of 10 items. Past headlines are not checked: `press.un.org/search` is disallowed by robots.txt, so a person can confirm two or three in a browser. The item pages have a bot check, so they are never opened (`itemPages: false`) and the summary is written from the feed's title and text, one or two sentences. Licence: personal, non-commercial use, without a right to redistribute or to create derivative works (UN terms of use, read 16 Oct). The feed has no pictures, so its cards have none.
- **ILO Governing Body** (`un-ilo-governing-body`, country `UN`): the ILO's list of all Governing Body documents filtered to "Record of decisions" (`https://www.ilo.org/governing-body/all-gb-documents?field_document_type_target_id=5966`), one entry per decision, newest first, 20 a page. Its URL stays the same from session to session, so the row needs no update after a session. It replaces the session page proposed in the research: a full session (March, November) lists its decisions on four section pages (INS, POL, LILS, PFA; 27 decisions on the March 2026 INS page alone), the June session on its own page, and the fetcher reads one list per source, so following the newest session from `/ilo-governing-body` would need a new kind of step; this list also has the special sessions (357th bis, 7 September 2026). The compilation of a session's decisions is left out (each decision is listed alone). List titles are cut at about 140 characters with "..."; the summary is written from the decision's page (`.ilo--richtext`, a few sentences). The date is read from the text after its label (`format: "en"`); the `datetime` attribute is a Unix timestamp. robots.txt asks for 10 seconds between requests, and its Content-Signal says `ai-input=yes, ai-train=no`. Licence (`https://www.ilo.org/rights-and-permissions`, read 16 Oct): CC BY 4.0 under the ILO Open Access policy, which covers all materials the ILO has published or made available since 3 May 2023, citing the ILO; a translation or adaptation must open with the ILO's set disclaimer (it is not prepared, reviewed or endorsed by the ILO, which disclaims responsibility), and the ILO emblem may not be used with translations or adaptations (the disclaimer is open, below). No picture: the decision pages' only picture (`og:image`, `twitter:image`) is the ILO emblem, so `pageImage: false` leaves it out and ILO cards have none. Only the 20 newest decisions are read: more than 20 posted between two checks (about 26 minutes with 26 sources), or one posted more than 7 days after its decision date, is missed. After the November session (9 to 19 November), compare the saved count with the session's section pages. Three sessions a year (the next: 9 to 19 November 2026), so the source is quiet in between. If this list stops working, the session's pages are the fallback: each section page of a full session as a row, updated after every session.
- **Quiet sources.** The European Central Bank and the ILO publish decisions a few times a month or a year, so their status turns `stale` (no item in 14 days) between decisions. That is expected, and the fetcher keeps checking them (it skips only `blocked` sources).
- **Preview pictures.** The ECB's press release pages carry only its default social image (`og:image`, a speech bubble with the euro sign in the EU's ring of stars, seen 16 Oct), which the app shows as the card's picture; the section on its name and logos in the ECB's disclaimer (`https://www.ecb.europa.eu/services/using-our-site/disclaimer/html/index.en.html`) is about their misuse in fraud. The ILO gets no picture: its only image is its emblem, which its terms forbid with adaptations (`pageImage: false`). UN press has none (its feed has no pictures, and its pages are not opened).

## `config`

How the fetcher (item 6) reads a source, as JSON. Types: `server/src/fetcher/types.ts`.

**Any kind**
- `match` / `exclude`: keep or drop items whose `title` or `category` matches a regular expression; `(?i)` at the start ignores case.
- `itemPages`: `false` never opens item pages (robots.txt forbids it, or they are built by JavaScript); the list text is used. A source's `fullText` is still read when set (Argentina has both); robots.txt is checked for each of its URLs.
- `pageImage`: `false` never takes the item page's picture (`og:image`, `twitter:image`), and opens no item page only for a picture (a teaser's page is still read for its text). For a site whose only picture is its emblem and whose terms forbid it with adaptations (the ILO). A picture from the list (`image`, a feed's enclosure) is still taken.
- `textSelector`: CSS selector of the main text on item pages (default `main`, then `article`, then `body`). Set it when the page holds more than the article (menus, share links, a site footer): only the text inside is kept, and reading the page costs far less CPU (#71, measured locally on a 122 KB Bundesregierung page: about 17 ms without, about 1 ms with). A selector list (`a, b`) works too, but keeps the handlers for the whole page, so it saves no CPU: prefer one selector. Picture captions (`figcaption`) are always left out.
- `fullText` (#71): where the whole text is when the item page does not hold it (built by JavaScript, or the text is on another official site). Read instead of the item page. A list of steps, or one step: `from` is a regular expression on the item link (first step) or on the previous step's result, whose groups fill `{1}`, `{2}`… in `url` (default `{0}`, the result as it is); `json` is the dotted path to the result in a JSON answer, or a list of paths whose results are joined in order (in the last step; before it the first found is used), where `*` goes through each entry of a list and after `*.` a `{path}` template is filled from each entry (the Commission's quotes: `docuLanguageResource.dolaQuoteResources.*.{author.authorName}: {extract}`); `link` follows the first element matching a CSS selector on a page (`selector@attr`, default `href`); `encoding` is the answer's character set when the server does not send one. The last step's answer is the text: its `json` value (HTML is turned into text), or the page's main text (`textSelector` applies). Every URL must be on the source's site or in `allowedHosts`, over https, and allowed by its robots.txt. At most 3 steps, each answer at most 200 KB, and one read counts as one page of the run's budget. An item whose link `from` does not match keeps its list text. Examples: the European Commission (one JSON step) and Argentina (JSON, then a link, then the page) in `sources.csv`.
- `encoding`: character set when the server does not send one, e.g. `iso-8859-1`.
- `newStageIsNewItem`: a new date on the same link is a new item (bill stages); the stored URL gets `#YYYY-MM-DD`.
- `crawlDelaySeconds`: wait between our requests to the site (from its robots.txt).
- `allowedHosts`: other hosts whose links count as the source's own (Brazil's open-data API links to `www.camara.leg.br`). Links to any other site are dropped.
- `conditionalGet`: send `If-Modified-Since` with the last check time; the site answers 304 when nothing changed, so a big list is not downloaded again (China's 266 KB archive).
- `textDays`: days to keep the extracted text (default 30).
- `note`: anything else people should know.
- `names` is not written here: loading adds it from `source-names.csv` (above).

**Feeds (`kind: rss`)**: RSS 2.0, RSS 1.0 and Atom are read without settings. `title`: another element holds the title (e.g. `description`). `date.field`: another element holds the date (e.g. `valueDate`).

**Pages (`kind: page`)**: `item` selects one list entry; inside it `link` (default `a`, reads `href`), `title` (default: the link's text), `summary`, `image`, `category` are CSS selectors. `selector@attr` reads an attribute; `@attr` reads the item element itself. A list such as `.card-time, .card-date` takes the first element in the item that matches any part; every part is looked for inside the item only.

**APIs (`kind: api`)**: `items` is the dotted path to the list (empty for a top-level list); `itemRoot` a path inside each entry; `title`, `link`, `text` and `key` are paths or `{path}` templates. A string holding JSON is parsed when a path goes through it. `keep`: `{ "path": "regex" }`, entries that do not match are skipped. `key` is added to the stored URL so one link can hold several items.

**Teasers (#71)**: list text under 1,000 characters is a teaser. Its item page (or `fullText`) is read for the whole text, also when the list has a picture, and the item waits for the run's page budget rather than being saved with the teaser. The read text replaces the list text when it is longer and already holds it (its start or its end); otherwise the list text stays in front of it (Germany's lead, SAIJ's summary). A page is read only up to 20,000 characters of text, whatever its size, and a text that long counts as both pages of the run. A site that answers 429 or 503, or cannot be reached, is not asked again in that run; another 5xx or no answer in time is that page only, and the site's other pages are still read (after 3 such pages in a run the site counts as busy). Pages that failed do not use the run's page budget. Items whose text could not be read wait for a later check while they are under 24 hours old, then are saved with the teaser. Check a new source's stored text length after its first day: under 1,000 characters for most items means a teaser, so find where its whole text is (item page selector, `fullText`).

**Dates (`date`)**: without settings, ISO 8601, feed dates and day-first `dd/mm/yyyy` are understood. `selector` + `attr` (pages), `field` (feeds; APIs accept a list, first non-empty wins), `format` (`fr` for "14 octobre 2026"; `en` for "7 September 2026" or "7 Sep 2026", also after other words, such as the ILO's "Record of decisions | 7 September 2026"; `uk` for "16 жовтня 2026", "15 жовтня 17:34" or "12:31, 16 жовтня", where no year means the latest such date; or a pattern with `yyyy mm dd HH MM`), `utcOffset` for local times without a zone (e.g. `+03:00`), `dateOnly` to ignore a meaningless time, `fromLink` (a regular expression whose group is `yyyymmdd` in the item link).

## Open points

- **Saudi Arabia: not in the list yet.** The only reachable official source, the Umm al-Qura gazette (`https://www.uqn.gov.sa/rssFeed/21`), forbids automated monitoring without written consent. We ask them before adding it. Everything else official there did not answer from abroad.
- **India:** PIB cabinet decisions (`pib.gov.in`) is the better source but blocks our fetcher's user agent; we ask PIB to allow it. Until then: Parliament's bill list, which is quiet between sessions (the health check must not call it stale then).
- **Russia: removed (Yurii, 16 Oct, #42).** The Kremlin site (`kremlin.ru/acts`) never answered from Cloudflare (broken since the first check, no article), and the earlier research excludes Russia as a source country. Russian stays as a reading language for readers outside Russia.
- **Retest from Cloudflare** once the fetcher runs; these did not answer from a test machine in Kenya and would be better sources: Türkiye's Official Gazette (`resmigazete.gov.tr`), Australia's Federal Register of Legislation (`legislation.gov.au`), Argentina's Boletín Oficial (`boletinoficial.gob.ar`), Brazil's Planalto legislation (`planalto.gov.br`).
- **Spain: the Official State Gazette (BOE) comes with a robots.txt change.** Section I (`https://www.boe.es/rss/boe.php?s=1`: royal decrees, orders, laws, about 2 to 5 a day) reads with today's `rss` kind and an `exclude` for `Sumario`, `Corrección de errores` and `Sentencia`, and robots.txt allows the feed and the item pages (`/diario_boe/txt.php?id=…`, text in `#textoxslt`). But that robots.txt is 494 KB with 12,312 rules, and the fetcher reads and checks it in every run: measured 16 Oct in the Workers runtime on a laptop, parsing takes 2 to 4 ms and checking the 3 URLs of one run 14 ms warm and 88 ms cold, against 10 ms of CPU per run. Add it together with robots.txt handling that keeps the verdict between runs, or reads only the rules that can match. `boe.es` is not a `gob.es` domain, so its row needs `portal` or `wikidata`. Until then Spain has La Moncloa's weekly Council of Ministers summaries.
- **Ukraine: only the Cabinet's home page.** The Verkhovna Rada (`zakon.rada.gov.ua`, `rada.gov.ua`, `data.rada.gov.ua`, `itd.rada.gov.ua`) resolves in DNS but did not answer a test machine abroad (TCP timeouts on 15 and 16 Oct); `president.gov.ua` answers its robots.txt with an Akamai 403; the Cabinet's RSS (`/api/rss`), its acts list (`/npas/`) and `/timeline` (where `/news` redirects) are disallowed by robots.txt. The home page dates its cards without a year in Kyiv time; on a busy meeting day, more decisions than the home page shows (about 12 latest) between two checks would be missed.
- **Czechia:** the binding government resolutions (`odok.gov.cz`) are `Disallow: /`; e-Sbírka (`e-sbirka.gov.cz`) is a JavaScript app with a CAPTCHA key; the Chamber's Collection of Laws list (`https://www.psp.cz/sqw/sbirka.sqw`) is allowed but needs windows-1250 decoding and a parser for its flat list.
- **Poland:** `dziennikustaw.gov.pl`, `monitorpolski.gov.pl`, `isap.sejm.gov.pl` and `www.sejm.gov.pl` disallow every agent except Googlebot, so the Journal of Laws comes from the Sejm's ELI API. gov.pl lists 10 decisions a page and only the first page is read. A Council of Ministers regulation can show twice, on purpose: gov.pl lists it when the cabinet adopts it (with its text), the Journal of Laws a day or more later when it is published (title and keywords). It stays in the Journal of Laws because not every one reaches gov.pl's list: checked 16 Oct, of the 2 cabinet regulations published 30 Sep to 15 Oct, DU/2026/1327 (13 Oct, ARiMR) is on gov.pl and DU/2026/1309 (2 Oct, open pension funds) is not on its 3 latest pages (22 Sep to 13 Oct).
- **Licences for a commercial launch:** Mexico (DOF), Canada (Parliament), South Africa (GCIS), Saudi Arabia and the UN press (`press.un.org`) allow non-commercial use only without permission. gov.pl text is CC BY-SA 4.0 (share-alike: check what it asks of our summaries), and gov.pl pictures, audio and video are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (non-commercial, no derivatives) unless a page says otherwise; the app shows gov.pl preview pictures today. Check before any paid feature.
- **Spain: La Moncloa's reuse terms ask for a set citation and a date** (`https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/Paginas/es_AvisoLegal.aspx`, read 16 Oct): the source cited as "Origen de los datos: sitio web de lamoncloa.gob.es. Ministerio de la Presidencia." and the date of the last update of the information used. The article screen shows the source's name, "Official source", the publication date and the link to the page, but not that wording or a last-update date. To settle: a citation line for this source on the article screen (the source's own wording, with the publication date as the update date), or confirm with La Moncloa that the source line and link are enough.
- **ILO: its terms set a disclaimer for adaptations** (`https://www.ilo.org/rights-and-permissions`, read 16 Oct): a translation or adaptation of ILO material published since 3 May 2023 must open, prominently, with the ILO's set wording (it is not prepared, reviewed or endorsed by the ILO and is not an official ILO translation or adaptation; the ILO disclaims responsibility for its content and accuracy). Our summaries are adaptations, and the uk, ru, es and de versions are translations. The article screen shows the AI label, the source's name and the link, but not that wording. The emblem may not be used with translations or adaptations, so ILO articles have no picture (`pageImage: false`). To settle: show the ILO's adaptation wording on ILO articles (article screen), or confirm with the ILO that the AI label and the source link are enough.
- **Texts still short (#71):** South Africa's notices and Brazil's bills have their text only in a PDF (the Government Gazette notice; the bill's `urlInteiroTeor`), which the fetcher does not read yet: ZA is stored with the notice's fields only (the PDF's name and size, and the public-comment deadline when there is one), BR with the ementa, author and date. A PDF cannot be read within 10 ms of CPU; Workers AI `toMarkdown` could convert it in one request, once we know whether a PDF conversion costs neurons. Mexico's DOF stays title only: `dof.gob.mx`'s robots.txt forbids its note pages; the ministry's SIDOF data service has the text (`https://sidof.segob.gob.mx/dof/sidof/notas/nota/{codigo}`, field `Nota.cadenaContenido`, allowed by SIDOF's robots.txt) and would work as a `fullText` step, but whether to read it is Yurii's decision.
- **African Union: not in the list yet.** The Peace and Security Council's communiqués (`https://www.peaceau.org/en/rss`, near-full text in the feed) suit the fetcher, but its legal notice (`https://www.peaceau.org/en/page/40-5029-static-legal-notice`, read 16 Oct) allows personal, non-commercial use, without a right to redistribute or create derivative works, and asks for written permission for anything more. We ask the AU before adding it, with a code other than `AU` (Australia).
- **UN press: the same terms as the African Union.** The UN terms of use (`https://www.un.org/en/about-us/terms-of-use`, read 16 Oct) grant personal, non-commercial use, without a right to redistribute or to create derivative works, in nearly the words of the AU's notice. The UN press source is in the list as agreed on 16 Oct; to settle: ask the UN (as for the AU), or agree that a summary with the link to the original is fine for both.
- **Not used on purpose:** `hcdn.gob.ar` (Argentina's Chamber of Deputies) lists AI agents by name as disallowed in `robots.txt`.
