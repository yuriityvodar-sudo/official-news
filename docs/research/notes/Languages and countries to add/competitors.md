# Competitors and substitutes for following official government decisions, by country and language (status October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-15. Method: about 30 web searches and page fetches. Most popularity figures come from App Store pages or company and ministry statements. Google Play download ranges could not be read: a fetch of a Play listing returned only truncated content. So download ranges are missing almost everywhere and are listed under Gaps. Where a fact comes from a search-result summary rather than a fetched page, the source link is still the original page the summary named.

## 1. Global and multi-country: apps that summarise government decisions or legislation, and AI news apps (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
No global consumer app was found that summarises official government decisions from government sources only, across many countries. The AI news apps launched or relaunched in 2024-2026 (Particle, Yahoo News with Artifact technology, Apple Intelligence notification summaries, Google Discover AI summaries) all summarise media and publisher content, not official sources. Apple's and Google's summaries have also become known for accuracy problems.

### Cited Findings
**AI news apps (all summarise media, not official sources)**
- Particle (by former Twitter engineers; CEO Sara Beykpour): free, with an optional paid tier, Particle+, at $2.99/month or $29.99/year, which TechCrunch calls its "first attempt at monetization". It released an Android app in early 2026 and added "Podcast Clips". It aggregates web news and podcasts and does not share user numbers. 55% of its weekly users are outside the US, and India (15%) is its largest market after the US. — [TechCrunch, 23 Feb 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/particles-ai-news-app-listens-to-podcasts-for-interesting-clips-so-you-you-dont-have-to/)
- Particle positioned itself in 2024 as an AI news app that helps publishers, not one that takes their work. — [TechCrunch, 12 Nov 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/12/particle-launches-an-ai-news-app-to-help-publishers-instead-of-just-stealing-their-work)
- Artifact (AI news app from the Instagram co-founders): it announced its shutdown in January 2024, saying "the market opportunity isn't big enough to warrant continued investment". Yahoo acquired its technology on 2 April 2024 to fold into Yahoo News, and the standalone app ended. **Discontinued as a standalone app: April 2024.** — [BGR](https://bgr.com/tech/artifact-the-ai-news-app-created-by-instagram-cofounders-was-acquired-by-yahoo/); [Business Today, 3 Apr 2024](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/yahoo-acquires-instagram-co-founders-ai-news-platform-artifact-424036-2024-04-03)
- Apple Intelligence: Apple paused notification summaries for news and entertainment apps in iOS 18.3 (January 2025), after false summaries of BBC headlines. An example is a summary that wrongly said Luigi Mangione had shot himself. — [T3](https://t3.com/tech/apps/apple-intelligence-news-notification-summaries-have-been-suspended); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/apple-intelligence-suspends-news-summaries-ai)
- The summaries returned for news apps in iOS 26, with a warning label. This is per the search-result summary of Yahoo Tech coverage; the page itself was not fetched. — [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/ios-26-public-beta-1-110000906.html)
- Google Discover: AI summaries replaced headlines in the US in July 2025, carry an "AI can make mistakes" label, and started with sports and entertainment. — [TechCrunch, 15 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/google-discover-adds-ai-summaries-threatening-publishers-with-further-traffic-declines)
- One analytics firm (Marfeel, December 2025) found that AI summaries made up 51% of Discover feed positions in the US, Brazil and Mexico. This is a single vendor's measurement. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-discover-feeds-users-ai-and-youtube-while-publishers-watch-traffic-vanish/)

**Multi-country official-law aggregators (free, but not consumer news)**
- Laws.Africa and AfricanLII run Gazettes.Africa, which they describe as the largest digital collection of free African gazettes. They note that gazettes in African countries are often available only on paper and at a price. — [Laws.Africa, 2020](https://laws.africa/2020/01/14/african-liis-and-laws-dot-africa-are-building-the-largest-free-and-open-access-repository-of-african-gazettes.html)
- EU Law Tracker (European Parliament, Council and Commission; run by the Publications Office): pilot launched 30 April 2024. It follows ordinary-legislative-procedure files on a timeline with real-time updates, and won the "excellence in open administration" category of the 2026 Ombudsman Award (30 June 2026). — [Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2209641/eu-wins-award-for-groundbreaking-law-tracker-exposing-legislative-process-to-public); [CDE Almería](https://www.cde.ual.es/en/las-instituciones-de-la-ue-reciben-el-premio-del-defensor-del-pueblo-por-su-herramienta-de-seguimiento-de-la-legislacion-de-la-ue/)
- Its language coverage is unclear: the institutions commit to all official EU languages, while an older Portuguese guide said it was English-only and still in development. — [IMT Portugal](https://www.imt-ip.pt/assuntos-europeus-e-relacoes-internacionais/uniao-europeia/legislacao-europeia/)
- European Parliament "Legislative Train Schedule": a web tracker of EU files, listed in the civic tech directory. — [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://directory.civictech.guide/listing/legislative-train-schedule)

### Inferences
- The positioning "official sources only, neutral, government decisions, many countries, mobile feed" appears unoccupied. Every AI-summary news app found builds on publishers, and every official-source tool found is a single-jurisdiction database or tracker, not a feed.
- The Apple and Google accuracy incidents are both a risk and a differentiator. A product that summarises the primary document, links to it and labels the summary as AI-generated meets a trust need that the big platforms have struggled with publicly.
- Artifact's shutdown shows that general AI news apps struggle commercially. That supports staying free and niche rather than competing head-on with general news apps.

### Gaps
- Apple News and Yahoo News user numbers, and whether Yahoo News uses Artifact-style AI summaries in its feed today: not researched in depth.
- Country rollout of Google Discover AI summaries beyond the US, Brazil and Mexico: not found.
- Not verified in this session (background knowledge only): EUR-Lex has no consumer app or notification feed of its own; the European Parliament's Legislative Observatory (OEIL) is web-only. Both need checking on their own sites.
- No source was found for any government-run app that pushes cabinet decisions to citizens in a neutral feed, apart from the gazette apps listed under sections 3 and 5.

## 2. US, UK, Canada and Australia

### Takeaway
The US has the most crowded field: open-data civic sites (GovTrack), many small AI bill-summary services launched recently (mostly web or email, often US federal only), and well-funded professional platforms (Quorum, Plural, BillTrack50). The best-known consumer mobile app, Countable, became an enterprise product after its 2020 merger with Causes. The UK and Australia are covered by parliament-focused civic sites (TheyWorkForYou, OpenAustralia) that track MPs and debates rather than summarising government decisions.

### Cited Findings
**US: consumer and civic**
- GovTrack.us is active. It is run by Civic Impulse, LLC and Joshua Tauberer, and tracks bills and members of Congress, including voting records and attendance. Its role as a bulk-data provider ended around 2017, when Congress began publishing structured data. — [Wikipedia: GovTrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GovTrack)
- Countable: a website and iOS/Android app with summaries of upcoming legislation, a way to contact representatives, and vote tracking. It acquired Brigade's technology and data in 2019. — [TechCrunch, 1 May 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/01/brigade-countable/)
- After merging with Causes in 2020, Countable relaunched as an enterprise SaaS platform for corporations, non-profits and campaigns; Causes.com is the consumer arm. — [Wikipedia: Countable (company)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable_(company))
- A civic-tech directory describes Countable in the past tense as "acquired by Causes". — [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://directory.civictech.guide/listing/countable)
- Bill Grokker: neutral summaries of US bills and executive orders, follow-up questions on the text, and an AI-assigned 0-100 bias score. — [billgrokker.com](https://billgrokker.com/)
- LawSummary.ai: daily email of AI summaries of recent federal bills and actions; email is the only channel. — [lawsummary.ai](https://www.lawsummary.ai/)
- TheBillRoom.org: free plain-language AI summaries of federal and state bills, plus campaign-finance and conflict-of-interest data. — [thebillroom.org](https://thebillroom.org/)
- MAPLE (Massachusetts testimony platform): uses AI for short bill summaries and topic tags. — [MAPLE](https://www.mapletestimony.org/about/how-maple-uses-ai)
- AI Law Tracker: plain-English summaries of AI-related bills (federal, Indiana, Illinois), aimed at business leaders. — [ailawtracker.org](https://ailawtracker.org/about)

**US: professional / B2B**
- BillTrack50 has AI-summarised every bill in its database, including past sessions, and regenerates the summary for each new bill version. — [BillTrack50](https://www.billtrack50.com/info/help/ai-assist-bill-summaries)
- Quorum offers AI plain-language bill summaries personalised to a client's issue areas. It is a B2B public-affairs platform. — [Quorum blog](https://www.quorum.us/blog/vendor-evaluation-scorecard-comparing-top-state-legislative-tracking-platforms/)
- Plural's "AI policy analyzer" is a paid tool for policy professionals, students, researchers and advocates. — [Plural](https://pluralpolicy.com/app/analyzer/)
- Billflo markets plain-language summaries of what a bill does and what changed, citing the official record. — [Billflo](https://www.billflo.ai/blog/best-legislative-tracking-software)

**UK and Australia**
- TheyWorkForYou (UK, mySociety) open-sourced its software. OpenAustralia is modelled on it and reuses that code to make parliamentary debates visible to the public. — [GovLoop / eGovAU](https://govloop.com/community/blog/whats-the-governments-role-in-improving-access-to-information-about-government-online); [Web Directions interview](https://www.webdirections.org/blog/openaustralia-interview-with-matthew-landauer)

### Inferences
- In the US, the AI bill-summary space is fragmented and new: many small, mostly web or email services, no clear mobile leader, and most cover bills only. Executive orders appear only in Bill Grokker; agency rules appear in none of those found. The US is crowded but has no dominant product, and Official News already covers it in English.
- In the UK and Australia, parliament monitoring is strong (TheyWorkForYou, OpenAustralia), but summaries of government and cabinet decisions (statutory instruments, ministerial decisions) appear thin. This is an inference, because GOV.UK's own notification services were not researched.

### Gaps
- Canada: not researched (for example openparliament.ca, LEGISinfo, the Canada Gazette). Coverage is unknown.
- Congress.gov: whether an official mobile app exists today was not verified. One search found nothing.
- LegiScan, FastDemocracy, Ballotpedia, Open States (now part of Plural): not verified in this session.
- Countable/Causes consumer app status in 2026: no shutdown announcement or date was found. The enterprise pivot dates from 2020; whether the consumer app is still in the stores is unknown.
- TheyWorkForYou and OpenAustralia funding and status in 2025-2026, and the UK and Australian Parliament apps: not found.
- App ratings or download counts for every US service listed: not found.

## 3. EU and member states (Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Poland, Czech Republic)

### Takeaway
In every EU country researched, the official layer is a free website, often with a basic official app that mirrors the gazette or parliament (BOE app in Spain, Bundestag app in Germany). On top sit paid or professional services (buzer.de alerts, ASPI) and a few civic projects (La Fabrique de la Loi, Hlídač státu). No country researched had a consumer app with short neutral summaries of new laws and government decisions. The Czech Republic stands out: the official e-Sbírka (2024) is authoritative but clunky, and the main free app, Zákony pro lidi, is a law reader with 25 ratings and no news feed, push notifications or AI.

### Cited Findings
**Germany**
- The Bundestag app was built by the Czech studio Ackee after a tender. It has parliament news, live plenary broadcasts, members and committee programmes. — [Ackee](https://www.ackee.cz/blog/en/glossary/the-bundestag-app/)
- It is free and German-only, per Similarweb's listing. — [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/es/app/google/de.bundestag.android/germany/)
- buzer.de is a private initiative with free access to current and past federal law. It sends a daily email alert of law changes and of every announcement in the Bundesgesetzblatt Part I, filterable by law or subject. The alerts become a subscription after a trial. — [buzer.de notes](https://www.buzer.de/h.htm)

**France**
- La Fabrique de la Loi is a project of Regards Citoyens and Sciences Po that visualises how bills change through Parliament. Launch coverage said about 300 laws promulgated since 2010 could be analysed. — [Slaw, June 2014](https://www.slaw.ca/2014/06/16/visualizing-law-and-legislation-la-fabrique-de-la-loi/); [Next.ink](https://next.ink/23375/87835-avec-la-fabrique-loi-suivez-l-evolution-textes-votes-au-parlement/)
- Légifrance: no app rating was found. Its Trustpilot page has a website score of 3.5 from just 2 reviews, which says nothing meaningful. — [Trustpilot](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/legifrance.gouv.fr)

**Spain**
- The national gazette BOE has a free official Android app for reading full texts and downloading them as PDF or EPUB. — [Xataka Android](https://www.xatakandroid.com/aplicaciones-android/el-boe-ya-tiene-aplicacion-oficial-para-android)
- Andalusia's regional gazette BOJA has an iOS/Android app and a PWA. — [Junta de Andalucía](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/eboja/preguntas-frecuentes/faq5.html)
- "Diarios", a third-party tool listed on Spain's open-data portal, searches several official gazettes by keyword and date. — [datos.gob.es](https://datos.gob.es/en/aplicaciones/diarios)

**Italy**
- The "Gazzetta Ufficiale" iOS app comes from an individual developer (Dario La Rosa), not the state. It says its texts are non-authentic and has too few ratings to show a score. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id827084020)

**Poland**
- Sejm Marshal Szymon Hołownia announced a Sejm app containing the Constitution's text, searchable by keyword. Bezprawnik argued it adds little. Its current availability was not confirmed. — [Tabletowo](https://www.tabletowo.pl/?p=869030); [Bezprawnik](https://bezprawnik.pl/aplikacja-konstytucyjna/)
- The government super-app mObywatel carries an MP's digital ID card (legitymacja poselska), not legislative news. — [gov.pl](https://www.gov.pl/web/mobywatel-w-aplikacji/zalacznik-legitymacja-poselska)

**Czech Republic (and Slovakia)**
- e-Sbírka, the official electronic Collection of Laws run by the Ministry of the Interior, launched in early 2024 after years of delay. It is free and unlimited, and its texts are legally binding. Lupa.cz found it behind commercial portals in presentation and still missing some regulations. — [Lupa.cz](https://www.lupa.cz/clanky/co-nabizi-nova-e-sbirka-a-jak-se-s-ni-pracuje/); [Ministry of the Interior](https://mv.gov.cz/soubor/predstaveni-projektu-e-sbirka-a-e-legislativa.aspx)
- After a 2024 amendment, commercial services (Zákony pro lidi, ASPI) showed the change before e-Sbírka did. — [Finance.cz on the Lupa.cz analysis](https://www.finance.cz/aktuality/e-sbirka-zakonu/)
- The Zákony pro lidi iOS app (AION CS, s.r.o., Zlín): free, rated 4.3 from **25 ratings**, last updated to v3.2.1 on 11 May 2025. It covers Czech laws since 1945 and treaties, with search, offline mode, favourites and version comparison. **No notifications of new laws and no AI summaries are mentioned.** A paid "Plus" tier is planned. November 2025 reviews report login problems, which the developer blamed on flood-style cyberattacks. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cz/app/id6698877007)
- Hlídač státu is a non-profit open-data and anti-corruption project. It links the contracts registry with company, insolvency and other registers, and also tracks party funding and lobbyists. — [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://test.civictech.guide/listing/hlidac-statu)
- Hlídač státu's alerts are a daily morning email about contracts, tenders and subsidies linked to a followed organisation or person, not about laws. — [hlidacstatu.cz](https://www.hlidacstatu.cz/subjekt/VazbyOsoby/05965527)
- The Chamber of Deputies has a mobile app with live floor broadcasts, per a Lupa.cz tag page. This is a weak source: it is a tag page, not an article. — [Lupa.cz](https://www.lupa.cz/n/sledovani/)
- Slovakia: Wolters Kluwer's ASPI "Monitor of the legislative process" emails daily summaries of changes in the legislative process. It is a paid professional product. — [Wolters Kluwer SK](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/sk-sk/solutions/aspi/monitor-legislativneho-procesu)

### Inferences
- The typical EU stack is official gazette or parliament (free, dry, full texts), then professional alerts (paid, email), then civic visualisations (web, niche). None of them offers "short neutral summary of each new law or cabinet decision in my language, on my phone". This is an opening in every EU language researched.
- The Czech Republic looks like a strong opening: a small market (one language), a newly digitised official source with machine-readable texts (e-Sbírka), and a leading free consumer app with only 25 iOS ratings and no feed. The rating count is a weak proxy for usage.
- Germany and Spain have official apps from parliament or gazette, but they mirror the raw output. A summarising feed would complement them rather than compete with them.

### Gaps
- EUR-Lex app or alert features, and Legislative Observatory status: not researched.
- e-Sbírka's own mobile app or notification service: not found. Its API or open-data feed was not checked.
- Google Play installs for the Bundestag app, the BOE app and Zákony pro lidi: not obtained.
- Poland ISAP/ELI and the official Sejm app's features and ratings: not found. Czech "e-Legislativa", the public bill-tracking side, was not checked.
- Netherlands, Nordics, Romania, Hungary, Portugal, Baltics: not researched.

## 4. Ukraine and Russian-speaking markets

### Takeaway
Ukraine has a lot of legal-information products, but none is a consumer feed of neutral summaries of official decisions. LIGA:ZAKON (LIGA360) is a paid B2B legal system that added personalised AI analysis of legislative changes in 2025. Opendatabot (3M users by 2021) monitors registries and courts, not laws. Diia (24M+ users) delivers state services, not decision news. The Rada apps found are unofficial vote trackers, and the official app announced in 2021 was not confirmed to have shipped. Russian-speaking markets are dominated by paid professional systems (ConsultantPlus, Garant) with limited free mobile apps. The official portals in Russia, Kazakhstan, Belarus and Uzbekistan are web-first, and the only apps found were unofficial wrappers.

### Cited Findings
**Ukraine**
- Diia: more than 24 million app users and almost 7 million portal users in 2026, per the Ministry of Digital Transformation on the government portal (May 2026). — [kmu.gov.ua](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/news/mintsyfry-zastosunkom-diia-korystuiutsia-ponad-24-miliony-ukraintsiv)
- In October 2025, Minister Fedorov reported more than 23 million users, up 2 million since October 2024. — [Mezha](https://mezha.ua/news/diya-23-milyona-koristuvachiv-305421/amp/)
- Diia is being extended to individual legal notices. For example, draft law No. 7315 (passed at first reading) lets courts send hearing dates and decisions through Diia. No source was found showing Diia pushing summaries of new laws or Cabinet decisions to citizens. — [Sud.ua](https://sud.ua/ru/news/publication/242909-ukrayintsiv-budut-informuvati-pro-sudovi-zasidannya-ta-rishennya-cherez-diyu-rada-pidtrimala-zakonoproyekt-7056d0)
- Opendatabot: 3,000,000 users as of March 2021 (company's own figure), up from 1 million in January 2020. It monitors over 20 state registries and sends change notifications, and has offered court-case notifications since October 2021. The app is free with in-app purchases. — [Opendatabot About](https://opendatabot.ua/en/open/about); [Opendatabot, 1 million](https://opendatabot.ua/en/analytics/409-one-million); [Opendatabot, claim notifications](https://opendatabot.ua/en/analytics/claim-notifications); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ua/app/%25D0%25BE%25D0%25BF%25D0%25B5%25D0%25BD%25D0%25B4%25D0%25B0%25D1%2582%25D0%25B0%25D0%25B1%25D0%25BE%25D1%2582/id1463927156)
- LIGA:ZAKON now sells one product, LIGA360, "enhanced by AI", for business, lawyers, compliance officers and accountants (November 2025 company release). Users can monitor a legal act, an article or a field of law and get email alerts on changes. — [LIGA:ZAKON](https://buh.ligazakon.net/news/240289_34-roki-liga-zakon-rozvitok-tannovatsyn-rshennya)
- Ekonomichna Pravda reported that LIGA360 launched "the first in Ukraine personalised AI analysis of legislative changes", available through test access. It is paid and B2B. — [Ekonomichna Pravda](https://epravda.com.ua/biznes/liga360-zapuskaye-pershiy-v-ukrajini-personalizovaniy-ai-analiz-zakonodavchih-zmin-809460/)
- LIGA360 Legal Solution (November 2023) analyses legislation and court practice for professionals. — [Jurliga](https://jurliga.ligazakon.net/news/223556_liga360-legal-solution-novinka-dlya-analzu-zakonodavstva-y-sudovo-praktiki)
- Verkhovna Rada: in November 2021 the Rada's chief of staff Korniienko announced a Rada mobile app for laws, news, bill paths and alerts on followed bills by messenger or email. No confirmation was found that it launched. — [Slovo i Dilo, 22 Nov 2021](https://www.slovoidilo.ua/2021/11/22/novyna/polityka/zakonoproyekty-smartfoni-korniyenko-anonsuvav-mobilnyj-dodatok-rady)
- Unofficial Rada apps: "Svidomist" (iOS, individual developer Pavlo Kravchenko) shows votes on bills by party and member. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6449873274)
- "Rada" (iOS) tracks bill status, sponsors, stages and votes in real time from the public API at data.rada.gov.ua. — [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/rada/6767016634)
- PolitScanner (iOS/Android, NGO "Institute of Political Information") shows MPs' biographies and incomes. — [LIGA:ZAKON](https://biz.ligazakon.net/news/198939_navedi-smartfon-na-litso-deputata-i-uznay-o-nem-vsyu-informatsiyu-novoe-prilozhenie)
- "Slovo i Dilo" calls itself Ukraine's first fact-checking media outlet. It tracks politicians' promises. It is media, not an official source. — [Slovo i Dilo](https://www.slovoidilo.ua/pro-proekt)

**Russia**
- ConsultantPlus has kept the "ConsultantPlus: основные документы" app on all major platforms for years, with core material free. — [iPhones.ru](https://www.iphones.ru/iNotes/399262); [ConsultantPlus press release](https://www.consultant.ru/about/presscenter/pressa3/pr_505/)
- Garant: a legal reference system since 1990 with more than 80 million documents. A base subscription costs from 12,000 RUB/month, with a 5-day trial. These figures come from a third-party review site, so treat the price as indicative. — [Toolfox](https://toolfox.ru/services/s/garant)
- The official portal pravo.gov.ru has no official app found. An unofficial RuStore app, "Право — законы и правовая информация России", wraps it and states it is not state-affiliated. — [RuStore](https://www.rustore.ru/catalog/app/ru.pravo.app)
- The commercial SPS "Право.Ru" has had iOS/Android apps; the coverage is old. — [Pravo.ru](https://pravo.ru/edition/view/76111/)

**Kazakhstan, Belarus, Uzbekistan**
- Kazakhstan Adilet (adilet.zan.kz): a free web database of all legal acts in Kazakh and Russian, with a mobile web version. — [Turan University guide](https://last.turan-edu.kz/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/instructions-for-working-with-the-information-and-legal-system-of-regulatory-legal-acts-of-the-republic-of-kazakhstan.pdf)
- Adilet's English layer is small: about 2,202 English documents out of about 449,000, per its database-status line. — [Adilet](https://adilet.zan.kz/eng/index/docs/dt=2023-11)
- Belarus pravo.by: the single official publication source for legal acts, available in Russian and English. It claims more than 8 million visitors a year in its own advertising deck. No app was found. — [pravo.by deck 2025](https://pravo.by/upload/pdf/reklama_na_pravoby_presentation_2025.pdf)
- Uzbekistan: lex.uz is the only official electronic resource for all legislative acts. — [dgov.uz](https://dgov.uz/en/solution-detail/38)
- A "LexUz" iOS app exists from an individual publisher, not the state: first released 14 March 2022, v1.0.4. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1608798574)

### Inferences
- Ukraine has a large, digitally fluent audience (Diia's 24M+ users) and rich open data (data.rada.gov.ua, registries). The products serve professionals (LIGA360, paid), businesses checking counterparties (Opendatabot) or political watchdogs (Svidomist, Slovo i Dilo). A free, neutral feed of Rada laws, Cabinet resolutions and presidential decrees in Ukrainian appears unserved. The closest new threat is LIGA360's AI change analysis, which is B2B and paid.
- In the Russian-language markets, paid professional systems and dry official portals leave a gap for consumers. Entering Russia raises its own issues (app-store availability, payments, sanctions, political risk of "neutral" summaries), which were not researched here. Kazakhstan, and Uzbekistan through lex.uz in Russian, could be reached with Russian-language summaries alongside the local language. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- Google Play installs for Diia, Opendatabot, LIGA360, ConsultantPlus and Garant: not obtained, because the Play page fetch failed.
- Whether Diia has a "news" or "state decisions" section today: not confirmed either way. One search only.
- Moldova, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Kyrgyzstan: not researched.
- Opendatabot user count after 2021: not found.
- LIGA360 pricing and user numbers: not found.

## 5. Latin America, Asia, Africa and the Middle East

### Takeaway
Official gazette and parliament apps exist in several countries (Brazil's Câmara Infoleg, Mexico's DOF app, Buenos Aires Province's Boletín Oficial app, Saudi Arabia's BOE app, the UAE Legislation apps, India's PRS app). They give raw texts or parliamentary data, sometimes with alerts, but none was found offering short neutral summaries. Japan and Indonesia are web-first or fragmented by agency. Africa has free open-access repositories (Laws.Africa, Kenya Law) but no consumer news feed.

### Cited Findings
**Latin America**
- Brazil, Câmara dos Deputados: the official "Infoleg" app (Google Play and App Store) covers bills in progress, plenary sessions, committees, deputies and main laws in force. "Infoleg Orçamento" tracks budget amendments, with favourites and update notifications. — [Câmara apps page](https://www2.camara.leg.br/aplicativos/); [Infoleg Orçamento](https://www2.camara.leg.br/infoleg/aplicativo/orcamento); [Rota Jurídica, launch](https://www.rotajuridica.com.br/?p=38355)

### Inferences
- The Gulf states have strong official apps (Saudi BOE, UAE Legislation), which cover the "official text" need well. Their low review counts (20 for BOE) suggest little consumer use. A summarising feed would add a layer above them, and in the UAE and Saudi Arabia it would most likely be read in Arabic and English.
- In Brazil, Mexico and Argentina, official sources are well digitised (DOF digital-only, Câmara Infoleg). In Spanish and Portuguese, the new AI summaries in Google Discover draw on media, not official sources. A neutral official-source feed in Spanish or Portuguese looks unserved.
- India: PRS is a trusted civic brand in English for Parliament. A summary feed of Gazette of India notifications and Cabinet decisions, possibly in Hindi, would be distinct. This is an inference; Indian consumer law apps were not researched.

## 6. Where consumer coverage is strong (hard to compete) and where there is only professional, paid or clunky official coverage (opening)

### Takeaway
No country researched has a consumer app that does what Official News does: short, neutral AI summaries of official decisions, from official sources only, in a mobile feed. Competition is strongest in the US, which has many civic sites and new AI bill summarisers, though mostly web or email and fragmented. Elsewhere the competition is official raw-text apps and paid professional systems. The clearest openings found are Ukraine (Ukrainian), the Czech Republic (Czech), Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking Latin America, and the Gulf (Arabic). The Russian-language markets are open on product grounds, but their risks were not assessed.

### Inferences
- **Hardest to stand out:** the US. It is crowded rather than dominated: many small AI bill summarisers have appeared and the professional platforms already run AI summaries. A wider scope (executive orders, agency rules, cabinet-level decisions) and the mobile feed are the differentiators. All of these launch dates and positions are drawn from sections 1 and 2; there is no market-share data.
- **Strongest openings by product fit:** Ukrainian, Czech, and Spanish and Portuguese for Latin America. In each, digitised official sources and open data exist, but consumers get either raw official texts or paid professional tools. Ukraine adds a very large, app-native audience (Diia 24M+), though it also has the most sophisticated domestic legal-tech player (LIGA360) moving into AI analysis.
- **Arabic (UAE, Saudi Arabia):** the official apps are good, but they mirror raw texts and have very few reviews. A neutral summary layer in Arabic and English would be additive. Political sensitivity of "summaries" in these markets was not assessed.
- **Russian-language:** by product fit this is an opening (paid professional systems, dry official portals, unofficial wrappers). The commercial, legal and app-store risks in Russia and Belarus are unresearched and could outweigh the fit. Kazakhstan, which publishes in Kazakh and Russian, may be a lower-risk entry to Russian-language summaries. This is an inference.
- Popularity proxies are weak everywhere: App Store rating counts of 20-25 for official or legal apps in the Czech Republic and Saudi Arabia suggest small consumer use of the existing tools. This is an estimate from rating counts, not downloads.

### Gaps
- Download ranges or monthly active users for almost every app listed: not obtained. The Google Play fetch failed and companies do not publish them. Using Appfigures or Sensor Tower, or opening each Play listing manually, would settle this.
- Coverage for China, South Korea, Turkey, Canada, South Africa, the Netherlands and the Nordics: not researched.
- Civic-tech directories (the opening-parliament.org PMO directory, Code for All members, mySociety's international partners): not consulted directly. They would list more parliament-monitoring sites per country.
- Legal, political and regulatory risk per market, for example AI-summary liability, Russia sanctions and app-store availability, and content rules in the Gulf: out of scope and not researched.
