# News habits and trust by country (demand signals for an app of AI-summarised official government decisions)

Research date: 2026-10-15. Main dataset: Reuters Institute Digital News Report (DNR) 2026 (published 16 June 2026; YouGov online survey, 97,520 respondents, about 2,000 per market, 48 markets, fieldwork early 2026). Every figure below carries its year. "(chg)" means the change in percentage points vs the previous year's DNR. Markets marked * in DNR (India, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa) are online, mostly English-speaking, younger and urban samples that the DNR says are "not nationally representative".

How the DNR 2026 country figures were collected (method note, for the report writer): trust, avoidance, paying and internet penetration were read from the 48 DNR 2026 country web pages (one link per country in the tables). AI-chatbot weekly news use and the top social/video networks for news were read from the country pages of the DNR 2026 PDF ([DNR 2026 PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/DNR%202026%20FINAL_2.pdf)); the page order was checked against the PDF table of contents and spot-checked against the Executive Summary chart (UK 4%, Greece 12%, South Korea 14%, Brazil 13%, Spain 8%, USA 6%, Germany 5%, Taiwan 7%, all matching). Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Russia, other Russian-speaking markets, the Gulf states and most of Africa are NOT in the DNR; other sources are used for them and flagged.

## Key question 1: Smartphone as main news device, use of news apps/aggregators, and reliance on social media / video platforms, by country

### Takeaway
In 2026, for the first time, social media and video networks are the most-used way to get online news across the 48 DNR markets (54% weekly vs 51% for news websites/apps), and 30% name them as their MAIN source; reliance is highest in Africa, Southeast Asia, Latin America and South-East Europe (single platforms reach 50-72% weekly for news in the Philippines, Thailand, Bulgaria, Peru, Morocco, Hungary, Mexico, Nigeria, Kenya, Romania, Poland), and lowest in Northern/Western Europe and Japan. Country-level "smartphone as MAIN device" figures are not published in DNR 2024-2026 text (gap), but the smartphone is the anchor device everywhere measured (UK/US first-device-in-morning ~37-39%, 57-58% of under-35s; Ukraine 91%).

### Inferences
- A mobile app is the right form factor everywhere, but "app-first" news consumption (opening a dedicated news app) is a minority behaviour: across markets only ~8% name aggregators as their main gateway, while social (30-43%) and search (22-23%) dominate. Distribution will depend on social/messaging sharing (WhatsApp, Telegram, Viber, LINE, Facebook) more than app-store discovery, especially in Latin America, Africa, SE Asia and Eastern Europe.
- Portal/aggregator cultures (Japan: Yahoo! News 57%; South Korea: Naver 56%; Czech Republic: Seznam Zprávy 49%; Indonesia: Detik 52%; Poland: Onet/WP/Interia) show audiences already accept "a feed of other people's content"; these are markets where a dedicated summary feed has a familiar mental model — but also entrenched competitors.
- Messaging-led news markets where share-to-chat is the main growth loop: Ukraine (Telegram), Nigeria/Kenya/India/Indonesia/Malaysia/Brazil (WhatsApp), Serbia (Viber), Taiwan/Thailand/Japan (LINE), Switzerland/Austria (WhatsApp).

## Key question 2: Trust in news media, trust in government, trust in official sources, news avoidance and news fatigue/overload, by country

### Takeaway
Trust in news fell to 37% across 48 markets in 2026 (lowest since 2015; down in 29 markets), and 42% sometimes/often avoid the news (29% in 2017). Lowest news trust: Hungary 17%, Greece 18%, Slovakia 19%, Bulgaria 21%, Serbia 22%, Romania 23%; highest: Kenya/Nigeria 68%*, Finland 63%, Denmark 55%. Avoidance peaks in Croatia 63%, Bulgaria/Greece/Turkey 60%, Serbia 53%, India 52%*, Philippines 51%, UK 50%. Government trust is generally as low as or lower than media trust in democracies (OECD 2023 average 39%; Czechia 19%, UK 27%), but much higher than media trust in Gulf states and some Asian markets (Edelman 2026: Saudi Arabia 89% govt vs 66% media; Singapore 76% vs 60%). Nearly four in ten feel "worn out" by the amount of news (39% in 2024).

### Inferences
- In most established democracies government is trusted about as little as the media (OECD 2023 averages 39.3% vs 38.9%). An app whose credibility rests on "official source" will not automatically inherit higher trust there; its pitch has to be "primary source + neutral wording", not "the government says".
- The gap "government trusted much more than media" is largest in Gulf states, Singapore, Malaysia, India and (OECD) Switzerland/Luxembourg/Mexico. These are the markets where an official-only feed could be perceived as more credible than news — but Gallup's caveat about autocracies and RSF press-freedom issues mean this can also be read as a state-media product; reputational risk to note.
- Ukraine has an unusual profile: low trust in government as an institution (Gallup 35%) and low trust in most media, but very high trust in the military (90%+) and a still-majority-trusted president; official wartime channels (General Staff 64%) are among the most trusted information sources.

## Key question 3: Paying for online news, and interest in / comfort with AI-summarised news, by country

### Takeaway
Paying for online news is a minority habit almost everywhere (17% across 20 richer markets in 2026; 6-15% in Central/Eastern and Southern Europe and Latin America), so a free/freemium model is the realistic default outside the Nordics. Weekly use of AI chatbots for news rose to 10% in 2026, highest in South Korea (14%), Turkey (14%), Brazil (13%), Hong Kong (13%), Greece/Indonesia (12%) and lowest in the UK (4%); summarisation is one of the top three uses, and "summarised versions of articles" was the most-wanted AI feature (27%, 2025). But comfort with news made mostly by AI is low in Europe (Croatia 9%, Finland/Denmark/Belgium 10%, UK 11%) and higher in India (44%), Thailand (39%), Indonesia (37%) and South Africa (34%).

### Inferences
- An app that is openly "AI-written" faces its weakest reception in Northern/Western Europe (comfort 9-11% in Croatia, Finland, Denmark, Belgium, UK) and its warmest in India, Thailand, Indonesia, South Africa and (via behaviour) South Korea, Turkey, Brazil, Greece. Framing matters: "AI summary of an official document, with a link to the original" maps onto the tasks people are comfortable with (summarisation, translation, simplifying) rather than "AI-produced news".
- Translation is a demonstrated use (24% interest; 53% comfort): multilingual summaries of foreign governments' decisions (e.g. EU/German/US decisions in Ukrainian or Polish) align with stated demand, especially in small-language markets.
- Low willingness to pay in Central/Eastern Europe (Hungary 6%, Serbia 7%, Croatia/Greece/Italy/Portugal 8%, Poland/Slovakia/Germany 11%) and Latin America (10-15%) implies these markets are reach plays, not revenue plays.

### Gaps
- DNR 2026 did not repeat the country-by-country "comfort with AI-produced news" chart in the text extracted; latest country comfort data is 2025, and only the top 4 and bottom 5 countries are named.
- No country-by-country figure for interest in "summarised versions" (27% is an all-market average, 2025).
- AI chatbot news use for India and the African markets in 2026 was not extractable from the PDF (India 18% in 2025).
- No AI-in-news attitude data found for Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Arab states or most of Africa.
- Payment data are not shown for India, Kenya, Morocco, Nigeria, South Africa, Thailand, Turkey in DNR 2026.

## Key question 4: Which countries combine high smartphone/platform news use + low trust in media + high avoidance (sweet spot), and which combine low trust in both media and government (risk)?

### Takeaway
Using DNR 2026 thresholds (trust in news at or below 33% vs global 37%; avoidance at or above 45% vs global 42%; a single social/video network reaching 45%+ weekly for news), the clearest "sweet spot" markets are Bulgaria, Croatia, Greece, Hungary, Romania, Slovakia, Serbia (probable), Turkey, Morocco, Colombia, Mexico and the Philippines, with Poland, Brazil, Argentina, Peru and Chile close behind. Of these, Greece, Slovakia, Czechia (near-miss) and Colombia also have low government trust (OECD 2023: 31.7%, 31.5%, 18.6%, 32.1%), which is the "both low" risk profile; the UK, France, US, Spain and Japan show low trust in both media and government (Edelman 2026) but are already English-covered or have low platform dependence.

### Cited Findings
- Low trust (≤33%) AND high avoidance (≥45%), DNR 2026: Hungary 17/45, Greece 18/60, Slovakia 19/47, Bulgaria 21/60, Serbia 22/53, Romania 23/47, USA 25/45, Colombia 25/49, Argentina 26/46, Morocco 28/46, Philippines 28/51, Turkey 28/60, Croatia 29/63, UK 30/50, Mexico 31/45 — country pages, e.g. [Hungary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/hungary), [Greece](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/greece), [Bulgaria](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/bulgaria), [Croatia](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/croatia), [Mexico](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/mexico), [Colombia](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/colombia)
- Of those, a single network reaches ≥45% weekly for news (DNR 2026 PDF country pages): Bulgaria Facebook 66, Hungary Facebook 57, Mexico Facebook 57, Romania Facebook 56, Greece Facebook 52, Slovakia Facebook 51, Colombia Facebook 51, Croatia Facebook 50, Morocco Facebook 59, Philippines Facebook 72, Turkey Instagram 48; Serbia rank-3 network (Instagram) is already 40; USA (Facebook 36), UK (21) and Argentina (43) fall short — [DNR 2026 PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/DNR%202026%20FINAL_2.pdf)

- Low DNR news trust but high Edelman government trust: Malaysia (DNR news 30 vs Edelman govt 72), Indonesia (32 vs 68), India* (39 vs 75), Singapore (46 vs 76), Thailand (47 vs 57) — country pages, e.g. [Malaysia](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/malaysia), [Indonesia](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/indonesia); [ZeroHedge/Edelman 2026](https://www.zerohedge.com/geopolitical/do-people-trust-media-or-government-more)
- Founder-language markets: Ukraine — 86% social media news use, 37% social-only, 91% smartphone, Telegram dominant, 46% war-news fatigue, government confidence 35% (2025), media trust low and fragmented — [UkraineWorld/Internews 2025](https://ukraineworld.org/articles/opinions/ukrainians-consume-news-2025-survey); [Gallup 2025](https://news.gallup.com/poll/693293/charts-show-ukrainians-shifting-views-leadership.aspx); [Rating Group 2025](https://www.ratinggroup.ua/en/news/media-aug2025). Czech Republic — trust 31, avoid 41, Seznam portal 49, govt trust 18.6 (OECD 2023) — [DNR 2026 Czech Republic](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/czech-republic). Germany — trust 46 (+1), avoid 40, WhatsApp 23 top network, AI chatbots 5, pay 11, govt 42 (Edelman)/36 (OECD) — [DNR 2026 Germany](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/germany)

### Inferences
- Strongest demand signal for a neutral, official-only summary app (low news trust + high avoidance + platform dependence): South-East and Central Europe (Bulgaria, Croatia, Greece, Hungary, Romania, Slovakia, Serbia), Turkey, and Spanish-speaking Latin America (Mexico, Colombia; then Argentina, Peru, Chile). Language leverage: one Spanish build covers Mexico, Colombia, Argentina, Peru, Chile and Spain; Portuguese covers Brazil (and Portugal, which however has high trust 51%).
- Among founder-language markets, Ukraine and Poland show the strongest behavioural fit (very high platform/Telegram or Facebook dependence, fatigue/avoidance, falling trust); the Czech Republic fits on avoidance/portal habits but carries the "low trust in government" risk (OECD lowest at 18.6%); Germany is a weaker fit on demand signals (moderate trust 46%, low platform dependence, very low AI comfort in Europe) but a large, stable market. These are inferences from the indicators, not tested demand.
- Risk profile (low trust in both media AND government — an "official sources" frame may not reassure): Czechia, Slovakia, Greece, Slovenia, Chile, Colombia, UK, France, USA, Spain, Japan; and Ukraine at institutional level (with the military/president exception). In these, positioning should stress primary-document transparency and neutrality rather than authority.
- Markets where government is trusted far above media (Gulf, Singapore, Malaysia, India, Indonesia) may welcome official-only content but carry press-freedom/state-media optics risk.

### Gaps
- Government-trust data are missing for several sweet-spot markets (Bulgaria, Croatia, Hungary, Serbia, Turkey, Morocco, Philippines, Peru; Romania only secondary), so the "both low" risk cannot be confirmed or ruled out for them.
- Thresholds are my own analytic choice (relative to DNR global averages), not a published index; different cut-offs would move borderline countries (Poland, Brazil, Chile, Argentina).
- No smartphone-as-main-device data by country (see Key question 1), so "high smartphone use" is proxied by platform dependence.

## Key question 5: Evidence on demand for "primary source" / "official source" news and for tracking legislation or government decisions among the general public

### Takeaway
Direct survey evidence on demand for legislation tracking is thin and old (US 2009: 22% of online adults had read or downloaded the text of legislation). The strongest current proxies are: official sources (e.g. government websites) are the second most-named place people go to check doubtful news (35% across 48 markets in 2025, and the TOP destination, 44%, among those who check via search); 45% prefer news "without a particular point of view" (2026); and in Kazakhstan 14% name official government websites as a main source of domestic news (2025).

### Cited Findings
- 2025 (48 markets): if checking something they suspect is false, people would go to a news source they trust 38%, an official source (e.g. government website) 35%, search engine 33%, fact-checker 25%, someone they know 19%, comments 18%, Wikipedia 17%, social/video 14%, AI chatbot 9%; 13% don't know — [DNR 2025 PDF, chapter 2.1 How the Public Checks](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf); [DNR 2025 Executive Summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary)
- 2025: among those who would check via search, the most-sought result type is an official source (e.g. government website) 44%, ahead of specialist sites/experts 35%, Wikipedia 26%, traditional news outlets 26%, fact-checkers 25%, "the summary of information at the top of the search page" 19% — [DNR 2025 PDF, Graph 2](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf)
- 2025: official sources named by 37% of 18-34s vs 34% of 35+ (young people slightly MORE likely), by 43% of highly educated vs 25% of low-education respondents, and roughly evenly across left/centre/right (about 35-40%) — [DNR 2025 PDF, Graphs 3-5](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf)
- 2026: 45% across all markets prefer news from sources that don't have a particular point of view vs 22% from sources that share their view and 15% from sources that challenge it; half or more prefer this in 24 markets and a plurality in 16 more; in 7 of the remaining 8 markets (including "many important, populous countries in the Global South") a plurality prefer like-minded news; preference for impartiality correlates negatively with social media as main source at country level — [DNR 2026 impartiality chapter](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/can-public-demand-impartial-news-survive-platforms-and-polarisation)
- 2026: outlets positioned as relatively neutral (commercial broadcasters, online portals, public service media) are often among the highest-reach brands — [DNR 2026 impartiality chapter](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/can-public-demand-impartial-news-survive-platforms-and-polarisation)
- 2024: the three most important "user needs" globally are "update me", "educate me" and "give me perspective" — [DNR 2024 Executive Summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/dnr-executive-summary)
- 2025: 20% of news avoiders say "there is nothing I can do with the information" and 18% that news "doesn't feel relevant to my life" — [DNR 2025 PDF, Graph 38](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf)
- Kazakhstan 2025: 14% turn to official government websites as a main source of domestic news (vs 66.8% social networks) — [Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/07/kazakh-audience-turns-to-social-media-as-primary-news-source-study-finds/)
- Ukraine 2025: military leadership (General Staff/Ministry of Defence) trusted as an information source by 64% — [UkraineWorld/Internews 2025](https://ukraineworld.org/articles/opinions/ukrainians-consume-news-2025-survey)
- OLDER DATA (USA, survey late 2009, n=2,258): 22% of online adults had read or downloaded the text of legislation in the past year; 48% of internet users had looked up information on a public policy or issue with a government agency online; 82% of internet users (61% of all adults) had used a government website; 44% found the government site via a search engine — [Pew Research Center, Government Online (2010)](https://pewresearch.org/pubs/1575/how-americans-interact-with-government-online)
- 2026: people across markets see politicians/government officials as the group with the most influence over news, which DNR links to lower trust — [DNR 2026 Executive Summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)

### Inferences
- "Go to the official source" is already a mainstream verification habit (about a third of all respondents; top choice in search), and it is at least as common among under-35s. An app that pairs a neutral summary with the official document link matches an existing behaviour, but as a verification tool rather than a daily-reading habit.
- The 45% preference for no-point-of-view news is a supportive signal for a neutral format, but it is weakest in social-media-heavy markets — exactly where the sweet-spot indicators are strongest (Key question 4). Expect a tension between "high need" and "lower stated appetite for neutrality" in markets like the Philippines, Kenya, Nigeria, India.
- Avoiders' complaints ("nothing I can do", "not relevant") suggest summaries should state what a decision changes for ordinary people (who, what, from when) rather than political framing.

### Gaps
- No 2020-2026 survey found that measures general-public interest in following bills, laws or cabinet decisions, or use of legislation-tracking apps, in any country; the only figure found is Pew's 2009 US survey.
- No country-by-country breakdown of "official sources" as a verification destination was found in the DNR 2025 text (all-market averages only).
- No data on usage of government websites/official channels (e.g. gov.pl, diia.gov.ua, Telegram channels of ministries) as news sources outside Kazakhstan.
- Eurostat "interaction with public authorities online" data (EU e-government use by country) was not retrieved and could serve as a proxy.
