# Search demand for laws and government decisions, and market size, per language and country

Researched 2026-10-15. All web-traffic figures are third-party modelled estimates (Similarweb, Ahrefs, StatCounter) unless stated otherwise. "Proxy" marks a figure that stands in for search demand without measuring it directly. "Estimate (computed)" marks arithmetic done in these notes from the cited inputs.

## 1. Search demand: how much do people look for new laws and government decisions, per language and country?

### Takeaway
I could not obtain any direct keyword volumes ("закон", "zákon", "nueva ley", "neues Gesetz" and so on). Google Trends returned HTTP 429, Yandex Wordstat needs a login, Keyword Planner needs an Ads account, and Seznam was not reachable. The best proxy is traffic to each country's official legal database and parliament site, divided by that country's internet users. On that measure Spain (BOE), Ukraine (zakon.rada.gov.ua / rada.gov.ua) and Russia (consultant.ru) lead with about 19 to 31 visits per 100 internet users. France, Czechia and Brazil sit around 10, Germany around 6.5, and Turkey, Mexico, Indonesia and India are at 3.5 or below. Ukraine stands out most clearly on the parliament site: about 20 visits per 100 users, against 1 to 3 for Germany, Brazil, Czechia and Russia.

### Inferences
- **Legal-database visits per 100 internet users** (estimate, computed: Similarweb Sept-2026 figure ÷ DataReportal Oct-2025 internet users; see section 2 for the user counts). This is a proxy, not search demand.
  - Spain, boe.es: 14.4M / 46.1M ≈ **31**
  - Russia, consultant.ru + pravo.gov.ru: 29.6M / 136M ≈ **22** (consultant.ru alone ≈ 19; mostly professional use)
  - Ukraine, zakon.rada.gov.ua + ligazakon: 7.48M / 35.3M ≈ **21** (zakon alone ≈ 19)
  - Czechia, zakonyprolidi.cz + e-sbirka.gov.cz: 0.99M / 9.96M ≈ **10**
  - France, legifrance: 6.3M / 63.4M ≈ **10**
  - Brazil, planalto: 17.5M / 185M ≈ **9.5**
  - Germany, gesetze-im-internet: 5.1M / 78.5M ≈ **6.5**
  - Turkey, mevzuat: 2.7M / 77.5M ≈ **3.5**
  - Mexico, DOF: 2.6M / 110M ≈ **2.4**
  - Indonesia, peraturan.bpk: 3.4M / 230M ≈ **1.5**
  - India, indiacode: 1.1M / >1B ≈ **0.1**
- **Parliament-site visits per 100 internet users** (estimate, computed): Ukraine rada.gov.ua ≈ **19.8**; Poland sejm.gov.pl ≈ 5.9 (low confidence, unconfirmed 2M); Germany ≈ 3.2; Brazil ≈ 2.3; Czechia ≈ 2.2; Russia (Duma) ≈ 1.0. Ukraine's per-user interest in its parliament is roughly 6 to 20 times that of the other measured countries. Of everything here, this is the clearest proxy signal of interest in legislation.
- BOE's lead is probably inflated: the gazette also publishes public-sector job competitions and official notices, so not all of its traffic is interest in laws. consultant.ru is a professional reference tool (accountants and lawyers). Both measure professional demand as well as public curiosity.
- Ukrainian top searches are full of government and administrative terms (mobilization order, NABU, the OK-7 certificate). That fits a wartime population that has to track decrees and rules which affect it directly, and supports a high-demand reading for Ukrainian.
- Google-based keyword tools would undercount Russia (Google has about 28% of search) and slightly undercount Czechia (Seznam about 15%).
- Weighed against the DNR finding that 48% prefer news without a point of view, a neutral summary format plausibly appeals in markets where trust is falling.

### Gaps
- **Direct keyword volumes were not obtained** for any language. Google Trends returned HTTP 429 for the "закон" explore page, Yandex Wordstat needs a logged-in account, Google Keyword Planner needs an Ads account, and I did not reach Seznam's keyword tool. One action would settle this: in a logged-in Google Trends session, compare the topic "Law" (or "Legislation") by country over 12 months, and pull Keyword Planner monthly volumes for "new law" in each target language.
- The Similarweb "Total Visits Last 3 Months" label is ambiguous (one month or three). The ratios compare countries correctly with each other, but they should not be quoted as monthly figures.
- I could not separate rada.gov.ua from its zakon subdomain; the two figures (7.0M and 6.6M) probably overlap.
- No figures for the US or UK per internet user (internet-user counts not fetched), for Egypt, or for Kazakhstan's legal portal (adilet.zan.kz not checked).
- No Arabic-wide or Hindi-language demand measurement. Indian legal portals are English-first.

## 2. Market size: internet users per language, smartphone users per country, iOS vs Android share

### Takeaway
By language, the online population ranks English, Chinese, Spanish (about 364M users, 2020), Arabic (about 237M), Indonesian, Portuguese, French, Japanese, Russian and German. The latest per-language user counts date from 2020. Country figures for internet users are from DataReportal, October 2025. Because the app ships on iOS first, iOS share matters. In September 2026 iOS has a majority or near-majority in the US, Saudi Arabia, the UK and Japan; above-average shares in Ukraine (37.7%), Poland (36.2%), France (34.6%) and Czechia (30.9%); and low shares in India (6%), Argentina (12.6%) and Egypt (16.6%). Multiplying the two out, the largest iOS-reachable non-English pools are India, Indonesia, Brazil, Russia and Mexico. Ukraine's 13.3M is larger than Spain's or Poland's.

  - Indonesia ≈ 47.8M

  - **Ukraine ≈ 13.3M**

  - Poland ≈ 12.3M

- For an iOS-first launch, Ukraine and Poland are better than their population suggests (iOS share of 36 to 38%), while Indonesia, Brazil, Turkey, Egypt, Argentina and India are mostly Android markets where most of the value arrives only with the Android release.
- The Ukrainian and Polish iOS shares look high next to older figures from memory, which I did not verify. Check StatCounter's 12-month trend before quoting them.
- Per-language user counts (IWS 2020) are six years old and should be treated as order-of-magnitude figures. Spanish and Arabic are the two largest non-English, non-Chinese language pools online.

### Gaps
- No per-language internet-user count newer than March 2020 (IWS), and none at all for Ukrainian, Czech, Polish, Turkish or Hindi.
- No current (2025-2026) per-country smartphone-user counts. The Newzoo figures run from 2017 to 2022. DataReportal country pages give mobile connections, not unique smartphone users.
- DataReportal internet-user counts were not fetched for the US, UK, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Kazakhstan, Argentina or Japan (only ITU percentages).
- DataReportal does not say whether Ukraine's 39.3M population figure accounts for refugees and occupied territories.

## 3. App store size: news-category downloads and revenue by country, top news apps, store restrictions

### Takeaway
No free source ranks news-category downloads by country across the year. Sensor Tower's free quarterly "top 5 news" posts give weekly snapshots. In Q4 2025, X and Reddit led the news category in most countries; national public broadcasters (tagesschau, 2.7M active users) and government-information apps (Brazil's Bolsa Família lookup, Czechia's MUNIPOLIS) also appear, which shows demand for official information inside the news category. Overall, India, the US and Brazil are the three largest download markets (Q2 2026), and the US leads on spending. Russia is the main store-access problem: Google Play paid billing has been blocked since 2022, and Apple ended App Store payments on 1 April 2026. China needs an ICP filing plus a CAC news permit.

**Store availability and payment restrictions**

- **Russia, regulatory pressure:** Interfax reported on 30 Mar 2026 (single anonymous government source) that the digital ministry was weighing pressure on Apple, including over Apple ignoring the legal requirement to install the RuStore app store. — [Interfax, 30 Mar 2026](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/116863/)
- **China:** since 29 Sep 2023 Apple requires an ICP filing for new China App Store apps (in practice a China-registered company or local partner), and news apps also need an Internet News Information Permit from the Cyberspace Administration of China. — [Singular](https://www.singular.net/blog/ios-apps-china/); [BusinessDay/Reuters](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/telecoms-and-technology/2023-10-03-apple-enforces-new-check-on-apps-in-china)

### Inferences
- The news category in Ukraine is dominated by civil-defence alert apps with 6 to 8M active users. Utility-style official information is what Ukrainians install, which is close to an "official decisions" app's job.
- In Brazil, the #1 and #5 news-category apps are government-benefit and labour-rights lookups. That is direct evidence of mass demand for plain-language explanations of official rules in Portuguese.
- A free app with no in-app purchases is the format least affected by the Russia restrictions, but monetisation in Russia through either store is effectively closed (estimate based on the restrictions above). Whether free App Store downloads remain fully available in Russia after April 2026 is not confirmed.
- China is out of scope in practice for a foreign team without a local entity and a CAC permit.

### Gaps
- No country-by-country annual totals for news-category downloads or revenue: data.ai and Sensor Tower keep these behind paid access, and AppMagic was not found.
- No free Sensor Tower news snapshot was checked for Spain, Poland, Turkey, Indonesia, India, Saudi Arabia or Egypt (the URL pattern is predictable; follow-up is cheap).
- No official Apple statement confirming that free apps remain downloadable in Russia after 1 April 2026.
- Apple App Store or Google Play restrictions for Iran, Belarus and other sanctioned markets were not checked.

## 4. Language reach beyond borders: one translation covering many countries

### Takeaway
Spanish (about 636M speakers, 20+ countries), French (334M, most of them second-language speakers across Africa), Arabic (335M Modern Standard Arabic users across a region of about 496M people) and Portuguese (269M, mostly Brazil) give the most countries per translation. Russian reaches about 210M speakers, but its main market is commercially closed on both stores. Ukrainian reaches about 35M internet users at home plus 4.41M people under EU temporary protection, concentrated in Germany, Poland and Czechia. A Ukrainian version therefore also serves a sizeable group inside Czechia (390K, the highest per capita in the EU).

### Inferences
**Countries reached per translation.** One translation reaches several markets:
- Spanish: Spain, Mexico, Argentina and 16 more countries, plus the US.
- French: France plus Francophone Africa, mostly L2 speakers.
- Arabic: about 20 countries, though legal texts follow national dialect-free MSA, which helps.
- Russian: Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Central Asia, plus Russian-speaking Ukrainians and diaspora.
- Portuguese: effectively Brazil.

Ukrainian, Czech, Polish and Turkish each cover essentially one country plus diaspora.

**Draft ranking for "which language next"** (inference). It weighs the demand proxy (section 1), iOS-reachable users (section 2), store access (section 3) and multi-country reach (section 4):
1. **Spanish.** The highest legal-portal interest per user (Spain ≈ 31/100), two G20 sources already in scope (Mexico, Argentina), about 43M iOS-reachable users in Spain plus Mexico alone, and 636M speakers.
2. **Ukrainian.** The strongest per-user interest in parliament and legislation of any country measured (rada.gov.ua ≈ 20/100 against 1 to 3 elsewhere), a high iOS share (37.7%), 13.3M iOS-reachable users plus 4.41M in the EU, and a founder who is a native speaker (cheap quality control of AI summaries). Ukraine is not G20, so it needs new sources.
3. **Portuguese (Brazil).** The third-largest iOS-reachable pool measured (39.2M, after India and Indonesia), a top-3 download market, and proven demand for apps explaining official benefits.
4. **German / French.** Rich, high-income markets (21.7M / 21.9M iOS-reachable), moderate legal-portal interest (6.5 and 10 per 100), and French's second-language reach in Africa.
5. **Russian.** Large demand (≈ 22/100 legal-reference visits, 136M users) and a founder who speaks it, but both stores block monetisation in Russia. Yandex dominance hides it from Google tools, and the main Russian-language audience outside Russia (Belarus, Kazakhstan, Russian-speaking Ukrainians at 13% + 19% bilingual) is the more reachable part.
6. **Indonesian, Turkish, Polish, Arabic, Japanese, Hindi.** Large populations but a low legal-portal per-user proxy (Indonesia 1.5, Turkey 3.5) or low iOS share. Japan is the exception, with an iOS share of 64.7% and a G20 source already, but Japanese legal-portal demand was not measured. Hindi has about 6% iOS share and little official legal content online in Hindi.
7. **Czech.** Small (9.96M users, ≈ 3.1M iOS-reachable), but per-user legal interest is similar to France (≈ 10/100), and Czechia hosts the EU's densest Ukrainian refugee population, so a Ukrainian version with Czech sources is a plausible combined step.

### Gaps
- No current per-country breakdown of Spanish, French or Arabic speakers who are online; no 2024-2026 counts of Russian speakers in post-Soviet states.
- Ukrainian refugees outside the EU (UK, US, Canada, Moldova) were not counted.
- Ethnologue figures for Polish, Ukrainian and Czech were not in the extracted table.
- The ranking is an inference from proxies. It should be checked against the direct keyword volumes listed as a gap in section 1 before a decision.
