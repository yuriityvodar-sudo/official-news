# Ukrainians in Czechia: how many live and work there, officially and unofficially, before and after February 2022

Research date: 2026-10-15. Each figure gives its year and source. Official counts are kept apart from estimates. Where a number came from a search-engine summary and I did not open the page myself, it is marked "(via search summary)". My own calculations are only in the Inferences sections, and each one shows its method.

## 1. Official series: Ukrainians resident in Czechia by year (Interior Ministry / CZSO), 2009–2026

### Takeaway
Official counts of Ukrainians with permanent or temporary residence peaked at about 132,000 in 2009. They fell to about 104,000 by 2014, rose to about 197,000 by the end of 2021, and jumped to about 636,000 at the end of 2022 once temporary protection was included. At the end of 2025 the figure was 612,953: 118,718 with permanent residence, about 393,000 with temporary protection, and roughly 100,000 with other temporary residence.

### Cited Findings
- 2009: Ukrainians with long-term or permanent residence peaked at 131,932. — [Uherek 2016, CMR Working Paper 93(151), p. 7](https://www.migracje.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WP93151.pdf)
- The decline after 2008 came from the economic crisis, which hit construction and manufacturing, where Ukrainians worked. Long-term residences fell while permanent residences grew, and the two were almost equal by 2011. — [Uherek 2016](https://www.migracje.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WP93151.pdf)
- Year-end counts (CZSO tables, as compiled on Wikipedia with CZSO citations): 2011: 118,932; 2012: 112,549; 2013: 105,138; 2016: 110,245; 2018: 131,302; 2019: 145,518; 2020: 165,654; 2021: 196,875; 2022: 636,282; 2023: 574,447; 2024: 589,456. — [Wikipedia, Ukrainians in the Czech Republic (citing CZSO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_the_Czech_Republic)
- 2014: 104,156. 2015: 105,614. Uherek calls 105,614 the end of the decline. His text dates 104,156 to "2010", which looks like a slip, because CZSO tables give it for 2014. — [Uherek 2016](https://www.migracje.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WP93151.pdf); 2014/2015 CZSO values (via search summary) — [CZSO table](https://www.czso.cz/documents/11292/27914491/1412_c01t05.pdf)
- 2017: about 118,000 legally resident at year end (secondary; via search summary). — [Vox Ukraine](https://voxukraine.org/?p=76039)
- 2022: CZSO also published 635,857 registered Ukrainian citizens, 57.1% of all foreigners, up 439,407 in a year (via search summary). It differs slightly from the 636,282 above. — [CZSO](https://www.czso.cz/documents/10180/190557310/29002623k3.pdf)
- 31 Dec 2025 (Interior Ministry quarterly migration report IV/2025):
  - 1,131,197 foreigners in total: 394,265 with permanent residence, 343,876 with temporary residence and 393,056 with registered temporary protection.
  - Ukrainians: 612,953 (54.2%). 118,718 had permanent residence. 494,235 had "temporary residence", a figure the report footnotes as including active temporary-protection holders.
  - Foreigners make up 10.38% of the population (103.8 per 1,000).
  - Temporary protection: 393,056 active registrations, 36.1 per 1,000 inhabitants. 749,328 grants were issued from 24 Feb 2022 to 31 Dec 2025, of which 89,358 were issued in 2025.
  - About 351,000 people extended their protection to the end of March 2026.
  - Prague holds 101,648 temporary-protection holders, Central Bohemia 56,989 and South Moravia 41,809.
  - Among working-age holders, 56.4% are women and 43.6% men.
  — [MV ČR, Čtvrtletní zpráva o migraci IV/2025](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx)
- The same report gives foreigners in total at 595,881 at the end of 2019. — [MV ČR IV/2025](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx)
- Third-country nationals on temporary residence for employment were 34,043 in 2017, 73,669 in 2019, 115,756 in 2021 and 105,793 in 2025. These are all nationalities, not only Ukrainians. — [MV ČR IV/2025](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx)
- CZSO gives the same headline: 612,953 Ukrainians at the end of 2025, 54% of foreigners. — [CZSO, Počet cizinců](https://csu.gov.cz/pocet-cizincu-demograficke-udalosti)
- Census of 27 March 2021: 150,178 Ukrainian citizens counted, up 29.3% on the 2011 census (via search summary). — [CZSO first census results](https://csu.gov.cz/produkty/csu-predstavil-prvni-vysledky-scitani-2021)
- In the 2011 census, 53,253 people declared Ukrainian nationality. Nationality was a voluntary question (via search summary). — [Government Council for National Minorities / CZSO](https://vlada.gov.cz/assets/ppov/rnm/aktuality/Prezentace-CSU-k-vysledkum-Scitani-lidu--domu-a-bytu-2021.pdf)
- Temporary protection, Eurostat: 390,810 on 30 June 2026 and 395,225 on 31 July 2026. Czechia had the highest number per head in the EU, 36.2 per 1,000 in July. These are secondary reports, cited in the sibling notes file. — [Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/08/12/a-record-4-41-million-ukrainians-are-under-eu-protection-and-few-are-going-home/); [Censor.net citing Eurostat](https://censor.net/en/news/4017921/over-4-4-million-ukrainians-hold-eu-temporary-protection)
- Special long-term residence (from Lex Ukrajina VII):
  - In 2025 about 82,000 people registered interest, and only about 16,000 met the legal conditions.
  - In April 2026, 56,503 people in 33,415 households registered interest.
  - Conditions: at least two years in Czechia, economic self-sufficiency (household income above CZK 440,000 for tax year 2025, plus CZK 110,000 for each additional person), a clean record and housing. The permit runs for 5 years and gives free access to the labour market.
  - EU temporary protection runs to 4 March 2027.
  — [MV ČR, "O zvláštní dlouhodobý pobyt má v roce 2026 zájem 56 tisíc osob" (via search summary)](https://mv.gov.cz/migrace/o-zvlastni-dlouhodoby-pobyt-ma-v-roce-2026-zajem-56-tisic-osob)

### Inferences
- Ukrainians with ordinary temporary residence (not temporary protection) at the end of 2025 ≈ 494,235 − ~393,000 ≈ **~101,000**. This is approximate: the 393,056 temporary-protection registrations include a small number of non-Ukrainians.
- Official counts can also *overstate* who is physically present. In March 2021 the census counted 150,178 Ukrainian citizens, while the registers showed about 165,000 at the end of 2020 and about 197,000 at the end of 2021. In 2025, about 351,000 extended their protection against about 393,000 active registrations. Both gaps fit circular migrants and returnees who stay on the register while living elsewhere. This is a reading of the gap, not a measured fact.
- The 749,328 temporary-protection grants since 2022, plus about 197,000 pre-war residents, put an **upper bound of roughly 0.95 million** on Ukrainians who held legal status in Czechia at some point since 2022. Double counting is possible, since 2025 grants may include re-registrations. At any one moment the stock has been about 0.57–0.64 million.

### Gaps
- I could not download the CZSO time-series table directly (the page loads its data by script), so the years 2010 and 2017 rest on secondary values.
- No official breakdown of Ukrainians by residence type for each year 2010–2021.
- Number of special long-term residence permits actually granted in 2026: not yet published, as far as I found.

## 2. Unofficial, circular, Polish-visa and undeclared Ukrainian workers: estimates and evidence

### Takeaway
No official or academic source gives a measured total for undeclared Ukrainians. Before the war, experts' figures ran from "at least tens of thousands", to "at least 200,000 illegal foreign workers (all nationalities)", to an IOM upper bound of half a million (2017; not clear whether only Ukrainians). Registered employment of Ukrainians was already at about the level of the resident count by 2021–22, which points to a sizeable short-term/circular registered workforce on top of residents. I found no source anywhere near "a couple of million" at any one time.

### Cited Findings
**Registered employment (Labour Office) against residence**
- Labour Office records of employed Ukrainians: 81,700 in December 2017. More than 195,100 in December 2021, 35% (about 51,000) more than in December 2019, so roughly 144,000 at the end of 2019 (via search summary). — [ČTK/ceskenoviny.cz, early 2022](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/cr//zpravy/pracovniku-z-ukrajiny-pribyvalo-i-za-epidemie-za-dva-roky-o-35-procent/2161340)
- End of February 2022, mostly before refugees entered work: **209,658 employed from Ukraine** (86,870 women, 122,788 men). By type: work permit 49,393; employee card 78,805; blue card 212; no permit needed 86,248. End of July 2022: 248,920, including more than 107,000 refugees with temporary protection. — [ČTK/ceskenoviny.cz, 23 Aug 2022](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/praci-ma-107-000-uprchliku-s-vizem-k-ochrane-jurecka-to-oznacil-za-uspech/2246440)
- In March 2022 about 240,000 Ukrainians were on Labour Office records (57% men). Just under half needed no work permit. About half worked as machine operators or in manual jobs. — [ČNB, Monetary Policy Report spring 2022, box by A. Ruschka, 6 May 2022](https://www.cnb.cz/cs/menova-politika/zpravy-o-menove-politice/boxy-a-clanky/Obcane-ukrajiny-na-ceskem-pracovnim-trhu/)
- February 2026: just over 400,000 Ukrainians (Interior Ministry data, apparently temporary protection). Labour Office data showed almost 200,000 temporary-protection holders in employment. The ministry calls its employment data "only an estimate" with "inaccuracies" from technical problems and employer coding errors: 86,342 Ukrainians were recorded as armed forces or police in June 2024 because of a coding error. — [Seznam Zprávy, 25 Feb 2026](https://www.seznamzpravy.cz/clanek/domaci-zivot-v-cesku-pres-85-tisic-ukrajincu-v-ceske-armade-a-policii-bizarni-chyba-uredni-evidence-299698)
- "Režim Ukrajina" (fast track for Ukrainian workers) started in August 2016 with 3,800 places a year, later raised to 9,600 and then 19,600 (via search summary; undated document). — [AMSP](https://www.amsp.cz/media/amsp/3464/Navyseni-poctu-zadosti-v-Rezimu-Ukrajina-ma_KORNB8EJ8MRP.pdf)
- In February 2020 Ukraine's ambassador said Czechia planned quotas of "about 40,000 people a year". — [Radio Svoboda, 19 Feb 2020](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30443050.html)

**Expert and official statements on unregistered Ukrainians**
- Ambassador Yevhen Perebyinis (February 2020):
  - About 140,000 Ukrainians live and work legally in Czechia.
  - The number of illegal workers is "unknown".
  - Working in Czechia on a Polish visa is illegal. Those caught get a stamp ordering them to leave within a month, and such cases are common, but he gave no count.
  - Illegal workers live "10 people to a room".
  — [Radio Svoboda, 19 Feb 2020](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30443050.html)
- Perebyinis (July 2021): nearly 180,000 Ukrainian citizens live in Czechia according to Czech statistics. He gave no estimate of undocumented workers. On the same occasion Poland's ambassador gave about 1.5 million Ukrainians working in Poland. — [ZN.ua, 20 Jul 2021](https://zn.ua/UKRAINE/posly-nazvali-kolichestvo-ukraintsev-v-polshe-i-chekhii.html)
- Echo24, 27 March 2017:
  - Radovan Burkovič, head of the Association of Employment Agencies: "according to our estimates at least 200,000 illegal employees from abroad work in Czechia". This covers all nationalities.
  - Jan Schroth of IOM puts the upper bound of estimates at **half a million people**. The article does not say whether this means only Ukrainians.
  - The article sums up that experts confirm "hundreds of thousands of foreigners, mainly Ukrainians" work without registration, and notes that none of it can be verified.
  — [Echo24, Petr Holub, 27 Mar 2017](https://www.echo24.cz/a/wCRAe/take-cesko-zaplavili-migranti-z-ukrajiny-potrebujeme-je)
- Same article: a Polish audit office survey from autumn 2016 found only about 150,000 of 900,000 Ukrainian visa holders actually in Poland, and assumed the rest were elsewhere in the EU. This is secondary; the article does not name the institution. — [Echo24, 2017](https://www.echo24.cz/a/wCRAe/take-cesko-zaplavili-migranti-z-ukrajiny-potrebujeme-je)
- Labour-market experts: Ukrainians working through "clients" (the client system) number "at least tens of thousands". The share of Ukrainians working illegally through clients cannot be determined. It is common for papers to be in order while employment rules are broken (via search summary; article date not confirmed). — [E15](https://www.e15.cz/domaci/nizka-nezamestnanost-drti-firmy-ukrajincu-neprichazi-dost-1330019)
- The Association of Personnel Service Providers (APPS) estimated about 200,000 workers in illegal "pseudo-agencies" in 2020, more than half of them foreigners (via search summary; industry estimate). — [TN.cz](https://tn.nova.cz/zpravodajstvi/clanek/407513-statisice-lidi-mohou-skoncit-na-ulici-nelegalni-agentury-jim-nepomohou)
- An older Ukrainian estimate (Ihor Markov, undated): 4.5 million Ukrainian labour migrants in total, of which "Czech Republic – 150 thousand (51 thousand)", the bracket being the official figure (via search summary). The official 51,000 matches Czech registers around 2001–2002, so the estimate dates from about then. That implies roughly 3 times the official count. — [KHPG](https://khpg.org/1207780827)
- Melehanych (2021): at the start of 2021, "more than two hundred thousand people of Ukrainian origin" lived temporarily or permanently in Czechia (via search summary). — [Uzhhorod National University](https://dspace.uzhnu.edu.ua/jspui/bitstream/lib/36168/1/Melehanych%2c%2008.pdf)

**Polish-visa route**
- Trčka, Moskvina, Leontiyeva, Jirka and Lupták (2018), "Employment of Ukrainian Workers with Polish Visas in the Czech Republic":
  - Polish visas are one way for low-skilled Ukrainians to work in Czechia despite strong demand.
  - Companies and intermediaries use them as a staffing strategy.
  - They create a "Polish visa worker" category of semi-legal residence.
  - The abstract gives no headcount.
  — [TUL repository](https://dspace.tul.cz/items/a871fa05-f96c-413b-8235-a9f306879cbd)
- ČT24, 15 June 2018: intermediaries in Lviv sold Polish visas for about USD 120 as a faster route to work in Czechia. Arranging a Czech visa cost about USD 400 through an agency, against an official fee about ten times lower. No count of Polish-visa workers. — [ČT24](https://ct24.ceskatelevize.cz/clanek/domaci/misto-ceskych-viz-dostavaji-ukrajinci-pokoutne-polska-zprostredkovatele-jim-namlouvaji-ze-to-nevadi-78737)
- Poland issued about 876,000 visas to Ukrainians in 2018, about 240,000 fewer than in 2017, after visa-free travel began on 11 June 2017 (via search summary). — [Ukrinform](https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-society/2618888-poland-issued-almost-900000-visas-to-ukrainians-last-year.html)
- A Polish regional border-guard report (April 2018) described Ukrainians with Polish work visas who went straight to work in Czechia (via search summary). — [wiadomosci.ox.pl](https://wiadomosci.ox.pl/woleli-czechy-od-polski,40796/pdf)

**Enforcement counts (detected cases only)**
- 2018: Czech police detained 4,992 foreigners staying illegally, nearly 1,500 of them Ukrainian. Most had entered legally and overstayed. — [Radio Prague International](https://english.radio.cz/node/8125005)
- 2018: inspections found 4,583 people working illegally, 2,873 of them Ukrainian. — [Yevropeiska Pravda](https://www.eurointegration.com.ua/rus/news/2019/04/25/7095655/). The Labour Ministry put it at about 3,800 illegal foreign workers, about 80% of them Ukrainian. — [Yevropeiska Pravda](https://www.eurointegration.com.ua/news/2019/07/10/7098293/)
- 2025: 10,838 people recorded in illegal migration (mostly illegal stay). Ukrainians, Moldovans and Vietnamese made up 64.2%. — [MV ČR IV/2025](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx)
- The CZSO notes that registered counts exclude foreign workers who "did not register with the foreign police". — [CZSO, 16 Jan 2020](https://csu.gov.cz/produkty/vice-nez-12-zamestnanosti-v-cesku-tvori-cizinci)

**Ukrainian-side surveys and macro estimates**
- Ukraine's Labour Force Survey migration module, 2017: 1.303 million labour migrants in total. Czechia 123,000 (152,000 in 2012), Poland 507,000, Russia 343,000. Partner-country administrative data gave 116,000 for Czechia in 2017. Vox estimates at least 2 million labour migrants from Ukraine in 2017 by adding about one third of LFS migrants to partner-country data, to cover irregular temporary migrants. (Via search summary; the page returned 403 to me.) — [Vox Ukraine](https://voxukraine.org/?p=76039)
- Ella Libanova (Ptoukha Institute), July 2021: at least 3 and at most 5 million Ukrainians work abroad, all countries combined. — [Ukrinform](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-society/3283862-up-to-five-million-ukrainians-work-abroad-institute-for-demography.html); [ZN.ua](https://zn.ua/UKRAINE/posly-nazvali-kolichestvo-ukraintsev-v-polshe-i-chekhii.html)
- A 2019 government figure (State Migration Service and Border Guard): 3.2 million Ukrainians permanently employed abroad and 7–9 million working abroad "part-time". This is from a search summary; I did not open the article, and it covers all countries. — [UNIAN](https://www.unian.info/society/10634088-over-3-million-ukrainians-working-abroad-government)

**Scale checks**
- Czech total employment in 2019: about 5.30 million (5,303,100 in main job; LFS via secondary analysis). — [AMSP analysis of CZSO data](https://amsp.cz/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/ANALÝZA_ZAMĚSTNANCI-final_Def.pdf)
- CZSO, January 2020: foreigners make up "more than 12%" of employment in Czechia. — [CZSO](https://csu.gov.cz/produkty/vice-nez-12-zamestnanosti-v-cesku-tvori-cizinci)

### Inferences
- **Registered workers versus residents (December 2021 – February 2022).** Labour Office employment of Ukrainians (195,100 in December 2021; 209,658 in February 2022) was at or above the number of resident Ukrainians of all ages (196,875). The resident count includes children, pensioners and non-working spouses, so perhaps 55–75% of residents (110,000–150,000) were the employed ones. The remaining **~60,000–100,000 registered workers were probably short-term or circular**: seasonal work permits, short-term visas, people not on residence statistics. Stale records would lower this, so a cautious low end is ~30,000.
- **Polish-visa and other-Schengen workers.** No count exists. Detected cases were in the low thousands a year, enforcement catches only a small fraction, and agency and expert statements speak of "tens of thousands". A defensible range is **~10,000–100,000 at peak (2017–2019)**, with low confidence.
- **Undeclared workers (overstays, tourist or visa-free work, client system), 2017–2021.** Burkovič's "at least 200,000 illegal foreign workers" times the ~80% Ukrainian share of detected cases gives ~160,000. Schroth's 500,000 upper bound times 0.8 gives ~400,000, if it covers all foreigners. "Tens of thousands" gives the low end. Range: **~30,000 / ~75,000–100,000 / ~300,000** (low / central / high). The high end rests on figures their own authors called unverifiable.
- **Scale check against the claim of "a couple of million".** 2 million Ukrainians would be about 18–19% of the Czech population (about 10.9 million, derived from the MV's 103.8 foreigners per 1,000). If ~1.5 million of them worked, that would be about 28% of all employment (5.3 million), at a time when CZSO put the foreign share of employment at about 12%, or ~640,000 workers of all nationalities. Undeclared work on that scale would have to be invisible to national accounts, the LFS, remittance data and Ukraine's own LFS (123,000 in Czechia in 2017) all at once. I found no source supporting it.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed estimate of undeclared Ukrainians in Czechia. The Drbohlav et al. Delphi study (2005–06) and the book *Nelegální ekonomické aktivity migrantů* (Karolinum 2008) probably contain one, but I could not reach the figures — [Drbohlav (ed.) 2008](https://is.muni.cz/publication/823839?lang=en).
- No count of Ukrainians entering Czechia visa-free (since June 2017), or of short-term work visas issued to Ukrainians each year, was found.
- No Czech Labour Inspectorate (SÚIP) annual counts for Ukrainians were retrieved beyond 2018.
- The Trčka et al. (2018) full text could not be opened (HTTP 429). It may hold field estimates for Polish-visa workers.

## 3. Remittances from Czechia to Ukraine as an independent check (NBU)

### Takeaway
According to the NBU, private remittances from Czechia were USD 834M (2020), USD 877M (2021), USD 982M (2023), USD 704M (2024) and USD 558M (2025), about 6–9% of all remittances to Ukraine. Scaling against Poland, where 1.3–1.5 million Ukrainians lived before the war, the Czech amount points to roughly 250,000–280,000 Ukrainians in 2021. That fits a few hundred thousand, not millions. Most of the Czech figure is NBU estimation rather than recorded bank flows, so the check is not fully independent.

### Cited Findings
- NBU, remittances by major country (official plus unofficial channels), USD million and share of total:

  | Country | 2020 | 2021 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Czech Republic | 834 (7.0%) | 877 (6.3%) | 982 (8.7%) | 704 (7.5%) | 558 (7.1%) |
  | Poland | 3,300 | 4,647 | 4,163 | 3,243 | 2,230 |
  | Total to Ukraine | 11,980 | 14,019 | 11,292 | 9,415 | 7,824 |

  No country breakdown exists for 2022, because money-transfer data were not collected in February–April 2022. — [NBU, Remittances (Perekaz_y_en.pdf)](https://bank.gov.ua/files/ES/Perekaz_y_en.pdf)
- Official channels only (banks plus money-transfer systems), Czech Republic: USD 150.5M (2020), 158.6M (2021), 79.9M (2023), 77.5M (2024), 76.9M (2025). Most of the Czech total therefore comes from NBU estimates of compensation of employees and informal channels. — [NBU](https://bank.gov.ua/files/ES/Perekaz_y_en.pdf)
- Informal channels for Ukraine as a whole: USD 5.9bn in 2021. Compensation of employees is defined as income of "border, seasonal, and other short-term workers who are employed in nonresident economy less than one year". — [NBU](https://bank.gov.ua/files/ES/Perekaz_y_en.pdf)
- Older (pre-revision) NBU figures for Czechia: USD 846M in 2018, up almost 50% — [UNIAN](https://www.unian.info/economics/10498248-labor-migrants-transfer-nearly-us-11-bln-to-ukraine-in-2018-nbu.html); USD 1.1bn in 2019 (preliminary) — [Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/en/amp/news/683319-most-of-remittances-from-labor-migrants-come-from-poland-russia-in-3rd-place-in-2019). These sit higher than the current 2020–21 series. The NBU notes a methodology review (note 1 in the PDF), so the series may not be comparable.

### Inferences
- **Proxy calculation, 2021.** The Czech share of Polish remittances is 877 / 4,647 = 18.9%. Applied to Poland's estimated 1.3–1.5 million Ukrainians at the start of 2022 (CMR; see section 6), this gives **~245,000–283,000 Ukrainians in Czechia**, assuming similar remittances per person.
  - Bias down: Czech wages are higher, so each person may send more, implying fewer people.
  - Bias up: Czech Ukrainians are more often settled with their families and send less, implying more people.
- **Reverse test.** If 2 million Ukrainians had been in Czechia in 2021, each would have sent only about USD 440 a year. On Poland's figures, Ukrainians there sent about USD 3,100–3,600 per person. A seven-to-eightfold gap between neighbouring countries with similar migrant profiles is implausible.
- **Caveat.** NBU country figures for informal channels and short-term wages are modelled partly from assumptions about migrant numbers, so they are not a fully independent measurement. Only USD ~159M (2021) is directly recorded from Czechia.
- The fall from USD 982M (2023) to USD 558M (2025) fits a shift from circular earners, who send money home, to resident families, who spend locally. It does not necessarily mean fewer people.

### Gaps
- World Bank/KNOMAD bilateral remittance and migration matrices for Czechia–Ukraine were not retrieved.
- NBU 2014–2019 country breakdowns on the current methodology were not found in the annual file.

## 4. Regional origin: how many Ukrainians in Czechia come from Western Ukraine

### Takeaway
Before 2022, Ukrainian officials and Czech researchers agree that most labour migrants came from the west: Zakarpattia, Lviv, Ternopil and Ivano-Frankivsk. No source gives a measured share. After 2022 the refugee population is more eastern (Kharkiv, Donetsk, Dnipro, Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia), but in a 2022 UNHCR sample Zakarpattia was still the largest single oblast of origin (24%). More than a third of refugees chose Czechia because family or friends already lived there.

### Cited Findings
- Ambassador Perebyinis (February 2020): most Ukrainian labour migrants in Czechia come from Zakarpattia, Lviv, Ternopil and Ivano-Frankivsk regions, with growing arrivals from central and eastern Ukraine, including Donbas. — [Radio Svoboda, 19 Feb 2020](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30443050.html)
- History: Western Ukraine and the Czech lands were in the same state (Austria) until 1918, and Sub-Carpathian Ruthenia (Zakarpattia) was part of Czechoslovakia from 1919 to 1938. Migration awareness persisted in Transcarpathia, and a study by Uherek et al. (2008b) is titled "Labour migration from Transcarpathian Ukraine to the Czech Republic". — [Uherek 2016](https://www.migracje.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WP93151.pdf)
- An older Czech teaching text (University of South Bohemia; undated) says Zakarpattia "probably supplies the most Ukrainian workers in Czechia" (via search summary). — [JČU](https://old.pf.jcu.cz/stru/katedry/pgps/ikvz/podkapitoly/b05cizinci/02/06.pdf)
- UNHCR intention survey, Czechia, 2–21 August 2022 (n=588, convenience sample, not representative):
  - Oblast of origin: Zakarpatska 24%, Kharkivska 15%, Kyiv 9%, Donetska 7%, Khersonska 5%.
  - Reasons for choosing Czechia: family or friends already there 36%, temporary protection 33%, work 24%.
  — [UNHCR / Sociofactor, Aug 2022](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2083358/CZ__Profile_And_Intentions_Of_Refugees_From_Ukraine_August_2022+%281%29.pdf)
- IOM DTM Czechia 2023 (via search summary):
  - Q1 2023: Kharkivska 14%, Donetska 9%, Dnipropetrovska 8%.
  - Q2 2023: Kharkivska 11%, Kyiv city 10%, Zaporizka 10%, Donetska 9%.
  — [IOM Czechia DTM report 2023](https://migrant-integration.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-04/iom-czechia-dtm-report-2023_en.pdf)
- Uherek (2016) found the 2014–15 war in Donbas did not distinctly change Ukrainian migration to Czechia, apart from more permanent-residence, citizenship and asylum applications and the resettlement of several hundred descendants of 19th-century Czech settlers. — [Uherek 2016](https://www.migracje.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/WP93151.pdf)

### Inferences
- Zakarpattia's 24% among refugees in August 2022, a region with almost no fighting, suggests that many "refugees" from Zakarpattia were families of pre-existing labour migrants, or circular workers who converted to temporary protection. This supports the founder's point that Western Ukrainian networks were already large in Czechia. It still does not imply millions.
- The prewar Western Ukrainian share is probably a majority, but the size of that majority is unmeasured.

### Gaps
- No quantitative oblast-of-origin breakdown of pre-2022 Ukrainian residents or workers in Czechia was found. The Ukrainian LFS 2017 regional tables (ILO/SSSU report) and Pozniak (2021) may contain it but were not retrieved — [Pozniak 2021](https://dse.org.ua/arhcive/44/44_7en.html).

## 5. Language, media habits and information needs of Ukrainians in Czechia

### Takeaway
Most Ukrainians in Czechia speak Ukrainian, and many also speak Russian. Facebook and Telegram are their main information channels, with Viber and YouTube next. They trust community channels more than official websites, and their top information gaps are about longer-term residence, healthcare, employment and legal processes. I could not find public member counts for the largest Czech-Ukrainian Telegram or Facebook communities.

### Cited Findings
- UNHCR, August 2022 (n=588): 90% of respondents spoke Ukrainian, 70% Russian, 10% Hungarian, 7% English and 6% Czech. — [UNHCR Aug 2022](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2083358/CZ__Profile_And_Intentions_Of_Refugees_From_Ukraine_August_2022+%281%29.pdf)
- IOM DTM interviews in Czechia (2023) were run in Russian and Ukrainian (via search summary). — [IOM DTM 2023](https://migrant-integration.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-04/iom-czechia-dtm-report-2023_en.pdf)
- Interior Ministry communication-channels survey (Socio.Faktor, funded by UNICEF, published 24 May 2024):
  - 79.4% of refugees say they get good-quality information from the state, and 80.9% get it on time.
  - More than half want more information about the requirements for longer stays, especially healthcare and employment.
  - The ministry's Facebook page "Naši Ukrajinci" is well rated.
  — [MV ČR, 24 May 2024](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/clanek/vyzkum-ukrajinsti-uprchlici-ziskavaji-od-statu-potrebne-informace-rychle-a-kvalitne-vysledky-resort-vnitra-vyuzije-pro-dalsi-zlepsovani-sluzeb.aspx)
- Same survey, summary document (via search summary; sample size and fieldwork dates not confirmed):
  - Social networks used: Facebook 68.6%, Telegram 47.6%, YouTube 34.8%, Viber 32%, Instagram 30.3%.
  - Top five information channels: Facebook, friends from the community, the ministry's foreigners portal (IPC), family, Telegram.
  - Websites rated: IPC 57.3%, other ministries 34.5%, Ukrainian embassy 33.1%.
  — [MV ČR summary](https://migrant-integration.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/MVCR%20INFO%20V%C3%BDzkum_komunika%C4%8Dn%C3%ADch_kan%C3%A1l%C5%AF_shrnut%C3%AD.pdf)
- Charles University MINTE project (22 interviews): Facebook and Telegram channels are the main source of information about life in Czechia. Respondents have limited trust in official web pages, and they trust information from Telegram, Facebook and Viber groups once others' experience has confirmed it (via search summary). — [Charles University, MINTE policy brief 1](https://iss.fsv.cuni.cz/sites/default/files/uploads/files/Policy%20brief%201_%20How%20the%20Czech%20authorities%20disseminate%20information%20and%20what%20information%20sources%20Ukrainian%20forced%20migrants%20believe.pdf)
- PAQ Research, "Hlas Ukrajinců – two years on" (14 Feb 2024):
  - What would help find work: better job information in Ukrainian, 12% (June 2023) and 18% (August 2022). Better Czech, 71–77%.
  - Help that refugees need but do not use (November 2023): legal-process counselling 34%, job-search help 35%, recognition of education 35%.
  — [PAQ Research](https://www.paqresearch.cz/content/files/2024/02/PAQ_Hlas_Ukrajincu_Dva_roky_pote.pdf)
- PAQ/Institute of Sociology wave 8, identity: 33% identify most as "citizen of Ukraine", 23% as "refugee" and 17% as "resident of the Czech Republic". — [CVVM / Institute of Sociology CAS, Hlas Ukrajinců waves 8–9](https://cvvm.soc.cas.cz/images/articles/files/5935/vyzkumnazpravahlasukrajincuvlna8vlna9.pdf)
- A multi-country survey of Ukrainians abroad found Telegram was the top messenger for news about Ukraine in all seven countries. Use was lowest in Czechia at 85% (Spain highest at 92%) (via search summary). — [Visit Ukraine](https://visitukraine.today/de/blog/2486/are-ukrainian-refugees-interested-in-events-in-ukraine-survey-results)
- The "Наші в світі" network (UNIAN, 9 January 2024, promotional) claims more than 500,000 subscribers across its country channels, including one for Czechia. No per-country count is given. Its Czech invite link no longer showed a channel when I checked on 2026-10-15. The article says Ukrainian-language foreign news on Telegram is scarce, and information on payments, services and work is hard to find. — [UNIAN](https://www.unian.ua/society/informaciyniy-vakuum-ukrajinciv-za-kordonom-12505431.html)
- Czech Television began translating news into Ukrainian (headline only; not opened). — [Suspilne](https://suspilne.media/217725-telebacenna-cehii-vidteper-perekladae-svoi-novini-ukrainskou-movou/)
- Rules that create information demand:
  - Temporary-protection extensions are run every year (the next one at the start of 2026).
  - Special long-term residence: an interest window in April and income thresholds.
  - EU temporary protection ends on 4 March 2027.
  - Employers must report temporary-protection workers to the Labour Office (form C-S15), and a single monthly employer report (JMHZ) starts on 1 April 2026.
  — [MV ČR IV/2025](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx); [MV ČR 2026](https://mv.gov.cz/migrace/o-zvlastni-dlouhodoby-pobyt-ma-v-roce-2026-zajem-56-tisic-osob); [Seznam Zprávy, Feb 2026](https://www.seznamzpravy.cz/clanek/domaci-zivot-v-cesku-pres-85-tisic-ukrajincu-v-ceske-armade-a-policii-bizarni-chyba-uredni-evidence-299698)

### Inferences
- A Ukrainian-language feed of Czech government decisions matches a stated gap: rules on residence, healthcare and employment change yearly, official websites are trusted less than Telegram and Facebook groups, and "more information on long-term stay conditions" is wanted by more than half of refugees.
- Bilingual Ukrainian/Russian ability is common (70% speak Russian), but Ukrainian is near-universal (90%) and is the language the Czech state itself uses for this group. Ukrainian alone is enough.

### Gaps
- I could not get member or subscriber counts for the largest Czech-Ukrainian Telegram channels or chats, Facebook groups or Viber communities. TGStat search needs a login, and guessed channel names returned only small chats (for example "Прага UA • Українці в Празі": 763 members on t.me, checked 2026-10-15; not representative). Checking by hand inside Telegram and Facebook is the remaining route.
- No survey measuring Ukrainian versus Russian *home* language among Ukrainians in Czechia, or among pre-war labour migrants, was found.

## 6. Comparison: Poland, the other large destination

### Takeaway
In Poland, the gap between estimates and registered counts was large before the war. Researchers estimated 1.3–1.5 million Ukrainians at the start of 2022, against about 600,000 paying social insurance (an embassy figure). Today Statistics Poland's experimental register-based estimate is about 1.68 million, against about 950,000 under temporary protection. Czechia's gap looks proportionally similar or smaller, and absolutely much smaller. Poland's system (employer declarations, national work visas) let many people work without a residence card, and Polish visas were also a route into Czech jobs.

### Cited Findings
- Górny and Kaczmarczyk (CMR Warsaw, *Spotlight*, February 2023): the number of Ukrainians in Poland at the start of 2022 "had been estimated at around 1.3–1.5 million (Duszczyk et al., 2023)". Ukrainians made up 70–95% of labour migration to Poland. PESEL UKR registrations were nearly 1.5 million at the end of summer 2022 and about 1 million by the end of 2022. — [UW/CMR Spotlight Feb 2023](https://inicjatywadoskonalosci.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2023/02/Spotlight-FEBRUARY-2023.pdf)
- Ambassador Andriy Deshchytsia (July 2021): about 1.5 million Ukrainians work in Poland, about 600,000 of them paying social insurance. — [ZN.ua, 20 Jul 2021](https://zn.ua/UKRAINE/posly-nazvali-kolichestvo-ukraintsev-v-polshe-i-chekhii.html)
- Statistics Poland (GUS), experimental study: about 2.3 million foreigners at the end of 2025, 73% of them Ukrainian (about 1.675 million). GUS says the results are experimental and not comparable with official population statistics, and that they ignore absences (via search summary). — [EADaily citing GUS](https://eadaily.com/en/news/2026/07/03/in-poland-they-counted-how-many-ukrainians-there-are-in-the-country-the-figures-are-impressive); [Censor.net citing CMR](https://censor.net/en/news/4015932/73-of-ukrainians-in-poland-plan-their-future-there)
- Temporary protection in Poland: 953,060 (31 July 2026, Eurostat via secondary reports). — [Censor.net](https://censor.net/en/news/4017921/over-4-4-million-ukrainians-hold-eu-temporary-protection)
- A self-selected Ipsos online survey in 2022 claimed about 3 million Ukrainians in Poland, an example of a high outlier. — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-ua/node/956381)
- Remittances from Poland, USD million: 3,300 (2020), 4,647 (2021), 4,163 (2023), 3,243 (2024), 2,230 (2025). — [NBU](https://bank.gov.ua/files/ES/Perekaz_y_en.pdf)
- Ukraine's LFS 2017: 507,000 labour migrants in Poland against 123,000 in Czechia (via search summary). — [Vox Ukraine](https://voxukraine.org/?p=76039)

### Inferences
- Before the war, Poland's estimate-to-insured ratio was about 1.5M / 0.6M ≈ 2.5×. In Czechia, my central estimate of ~300,000 against ~197,000 residents (end 2021) is about 1.5×, and the high end, ~500,000, is about 2.5×. The "official undercount" pattern is real in both countries. In Czechia it is in the hundreds of thousands, not millions.
- The founder's sense of "millions" may be accurate for the Western Ukrainian experience across Poland *and* Czechia together. Poland alone had 1.3–1.5 million before the war, and some Polish-visa holders physically worked in Czechia.

### Gaps
- Poland's official count of valid residence permits held by Ukrainians in 2019–2021 was not retrieved, so the Polish "official versus estimated" ratio uses social-insurance numbers instead.

## 7. Synthesis: low / central / high ranges and a verdict on the claim of "a couple of million before the war"

### Takeaway
At any one time, the evidence supports about **0.25–0.6 million Ukrainians in Czechia in 2019–2021 (central ~0.3–0.35 million)**, and about **0.53–0.76 million in 2025–2026 (central ~0.63 million)**. Official figures undercount the pre-war population by perhaps 1.3–2.5 times, mostly through circular, short-term, Polish-visa and undeclared workers. The founder is right that official numbers undercount, and right that there was a large Western Ukrainian presence with families. "A couple of million" at any one time before 2022 is not supported by any data source I found. It is 4–10 times the highest expert estimate and inconsistent with remittance, labour-market and Ukrainian survey data. It could only be approached as a *cumulative* count of everyone who worked in Czechia at some point over many years, and no source measures that.

### Cited Findings
- The inputs are cited in sections 1–6. Key anchors:
  - Residents: 196,875 (2021) and 612,953 (2025) — [Wikipedia/CZSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_the_Czech_Republic); [MV ČR IV/2025](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx)
  - Registered employed: 209,658 (February 2022) — [ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/praci-ma-107-000-uprchliku-s-vizem-k-ochrane-jurecka-to-oznacil-za-uspech/2246440)
  - IOM upper bound of 500,000 and agencies' "at least 200,000 illegal foreign workers" (2017) — [Echo24](https://www.echo24.cz/a/wCRAe/take-cesko-zaplavili-migranti-z-ukrajiny-potrebujeme-je)
  - Ukrainian LFS: 123,000 in Czechia (2017) — [Vox Ukraine](https://voxukraine.org/?p=76039)
  - NBU remittances from Czechia: USD 877M (2021) — [NBU](https://bank.gov.ua/files/ES/Perekaz_y_en.pdf)
  - Ambassador Perebyinis: about 140,000 legal, illegal "unknown" (2020) — [Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30443050.html)

### Inferences
**(a) 2014–2021, at the peak around 2019–2021; numbers at any one time**

| Component | Low | Central | High | Method / basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Official residents (permanent + temporary) | 146k (2019) / 197k (2021) | same | same | MV/CZSO registers (official count) |
| Short-term / circular registered workers not in residence stats | ~30k | ~60k | ~100k | Labour Office employed (195–210k) minus estimated employed residents (55–75% of residents) |
| Polish-visa and other-Schengen-visa workers | ~10k | ~30k | ~100k | No count. Detected cases in the low thousands a year, "tens of thousands" in expert talk. Low confidence |
| Undeclared / irregular (overstays, tourist or visa-free work, client system) | ~30k | ~75k | ~300k | Agency estimate of 200k illegal foreign workers × ~80% Ukrainian share of detected cases; IOM upper bound of 500k × 0.8 |
| **Total Ukrainians present, 2021** | **~0.25M** | **~0.3–0.35M** | **~0.5–0.6M** | Sum (components overlap slightly; rounded) |

- Cross-checks for the central estimate:
  - NBU remittance scaling against Poland gives ~245,000–283,000.
  - Ukraine's LFS gives 123,000 in 2017, when residents were ~118,000; with the Vox rule (administrative data plus one third of LFS) that is ~157,000 in 2017, and scaled to 2021 residents roughly ~260,000.
  - Melehanych gives "over 200,000 of Ukrainian origin" at the start of 2021.
- For 2014–2016, residents were ~104,000–110,000, and the visa-free regime (June 2017), Režim Ukrajina (August 2016) and the Polish-visa boom (2016–2018) were not yet fully in place. A plausible total is **~0.15M / 0.2M / 0.35M**.
- Overstay and undeclared-work numbers can be read the other way too. Ukraine's LFS counts only migrants whose households stayed in Ukraine, so whole families who moved are missed. The Czech census in 2021 counted fewer Ukrainians than the registers. Neither error source is large enough to reach millions.

**(b) 2022–2026; numbers at any one time**

| Component | Low | Central | High | Method / basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Permanent residents | 119k | 119k | 119k | MV, end 2025 (official) |
| Other temporary residence (work, family, study) | ~100k | ~100k | ~100k | 494,235 − ~393,000 temporary protection, end 2025 (derived) |
| Temporary protection holders | ~350k | ~393k | ~395k | MV 393,056 (end 2025); Eurostat 395,225 (July 2026); ~351k extended (low = people actually present) |
| Unregistered / undeclared (short-term visa-free workers, people who never registered) | ~10k | ~40k | ~150k | No source. Temporary protection gives free labour-market access, which cuts the incentive to work illegally; agencies are still reported to prefer unregistered refugees |
| Registered but absent (returned to Ukraine or moved on) | −50k | −20k | 0 | Census/register gap and extension-uptake gap (inference) |
| **Total Ukrainians present, 2025–26** | **~0.53M** | **~0.63M** | **~0.76M** | Sum, rounded |

- Of these, roughly 200,000 temporary-protection holders were in registered employment (February 2026), plus an unknown number of the ~220,000 permanent and other temporary residents. A plausible total for working Ukrainians is **~0.3–0.4 million**, which is an inference.
- **Cumulative** count: up to ~0.95 million Ukrainians have held legal status in Czechia at some point since 2022 (749,328 temporary-protection grants plus ~197,000 pre-war residents; some double counting). So "far more after 2022" is true: the stock roughly tripled. But the stock at any time is ~0.6 million, not several million.

**Verdict on the claim**
- *Supported:* official 2014–2021 residence counts (104,000–197,000) understate Ukrainian presence. Circular, seasonal, Polish-visa and client-system work was widespread and mainly Western Ukrainian. Families often came along. Registered employment already matched or exceeded residents by 2021–22, and experts in 2017 spoke of hundreds of thousands working unregistered.
- *Not supported:* "a couple of million before the full-scale war" as a number present at one time. On every source found it would mean 18–19% of Czechia's population, more than three times the CZSO-implied foreign workforce of all nationalities (~640,000 in 2019), and remittances of only ~USD 440 per person a year. The most generous expert bound (IOM, 2017) is 0.5 million, and Ukraine's own embassy never claimed more than official counts.
- *Possible reconciliation:* "millions" fits (i) everyone who has worked in Czechia at some point over two decades of circular migration, (ii) Western Ukrainian labour migration to Poland and Czechia combined, or (iii) total Ukrainian labour migration across the EU (Libanova: 3–5 million in all countries). None of these is a Czech stock figure.

### Gaps
- There is no capture-recapture, residual or Delphi-based estimate of irregular Ukrainians in Czechia after 2010. The best academic attempt (Drbohlav et al., 2005–2008) predates the visa-free and Polish-visa period, and I could not read its figures.
- No measure of the cumulative number of distinct Ukrainians who worked in Czechia over 2000–2021 exists in the sources found. That number is what would test the founder's "millions" claim in its most favourable form.
- Labour Office employment series for Ukrainians for 2023–2026 were found only as one figure (February 2026), and the ministry describes them as error-prone.
