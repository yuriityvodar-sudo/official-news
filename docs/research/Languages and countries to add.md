# Ship Ukrainian first, then Spanish and Portuguese

Build a **Ukrainian version first, Spanish second and Portuguese for Brazil third**, then Czech and Polish, whose sources the Ukrainian version needs anyway, and Russian for readers outside Russia. Ukrainian has the strongest measured interest in legislation of any country checked: about **20 visits to the parliament site per 100 internet users, against roughly 1 to 6 elsewhere** (Similarweb traffic, a proxy rather than search data). It also has a high iOS share, **4.4 million Ukrainians under EU protection** concentrated in Germany, Poland and Czechia, and a founder who can check summary quality himself. Spanish reaches 20+ countries with one translation, and two of its sources are already in the feed; Brazil is the largest non-English iOS audience measured that also shows mass demand for official information. Ukrainian's catch is fetching: Ukraine's parliament sites did not answer from abroad and the Cabinet's feed is closed by robots.txt, so version one should translate decisions from governments already covered (Germany, EU, US, Canada, UK) plus new Polish and Czech sources, and one test fetch from the deployed Worker decides when Ukraine itself joins. A language costs little app work and about **8 extra AI Neurons per article** (estimate, on a measured base of 15.7), so the free tier still fits about 420 articles a day with one extra language and 250 with three, against 70 to 170 new articles a day today. Russia stays excluded as a market and a source country, but Russian as a language ranks sixth: it has a large, scattered readership in Kazakhstan, Central Asia, the Baltics, Israel and Germany, clean Kazakh sources and a founder who reads it, but no measured demand, and whether it alienates Ukrainian readers is a judgement call. On Ukrainians in Czechia, the founder is right that the registers undercounted: the evidence supports about **0.3 million present in 2021 (range 0.25 to 0.6 million) against 0.2 million registered**, and about 0.6 million today, though no source reaches "a couple of million" at one time.

The order weighs four signals per country: interest in legislation, measured as visits to official law and parliament sites per 100 internet users (Similarweb, September 2026, a traffic proxy); iOS reach, estimated as internet users times StatCounter's iOS share of mobile web traffic; how cleanly official sources can be fetched, from live probes on 15 October 2026; and how weak the competition is. **No direct keyword volumes were obtained** (Google Trends rate-limited the request, and Keyword Planner and Yandex Wordstat need accounts), so treat the order as a hypothesis for the validation steps at the end. It mostly matches the plan's Phase 2 (Ukraine, Poland, Spain); the changes are pulling Czechia, and for Russian Kazakhstan, forward from Phase 3 (`docs/plan.md`). Ranks 5 and 6 are close, and the in-app vote should decide between them.

The gap is open everywhere checked. In about 30 searches, no country had a consumer app with short, neutral summaries of official decisions. Official apps such as Spain's BOE app show full texts ([Xataka](https://www.xatakandroid.com/aplicaciones-android/el-boe-ya-tiene-aplicacion-oficial-para-android)), professional tools are paid, and AI news apps such as Particle summarise media, not governments ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/particles-ai-news-app-listens-to-podcasts-for-interesting-clips-so-you-you-dont-have-to/)). Apple paused its AI news summaries in January 2025 after false headlines ([T3](https://t3.com/tech/apps/apple-intelligence-news-notification-summaries-have-been-suspended)), which makes "summary of the official document, with a link to it" a real point of difference. The habit behind it exists: **35% of people across 48 markets** would turn to an official source such as a government website to check doubtful news ([DNR 2025](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/dnr-executive-summary)).

## Ukrainian has the strongest demand signal and the hardest sources

Ukrainians look up their laws far more than other nations do. The parliament site rada.gov.ua drew about 7 million Similarweb visits, **about 20 per 100 internet users, against about 3 for Germany's Bundestag and about 2 for Brazil's and Czechia's chambers** (computed from Similarweb and DataReportal; good for comparing countries, not a monthly figure) ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/rada.gov.ua/); [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-ukraine)). Ukraine's top 2025 searches included legal terms such as "mobilization order" and "NABU" ([LIGA.net](https://news.liga.net/en/amp/society/news/blackout-charts-the-bachelor-squid-game-2-what-ukrainians-googled-most-often-in-2025)), and its news-app category is led by air-raid alert apps with 6 to 8 million active users, so Ukrainians already install apps for official, practical information ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-news-units-ua-600abc3f241bc16eb850846d)). iOS holds **37.7% of mobile web traffic**, about 13 million iPhone-reachable users (estimate; web share is not the installed base, and the figure is high next to older data, so check StatCounter's 12-month trend before quoting it) ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/ukraine)).

The audience abroad is large and concentrated. **4.43 million** Ukrainians held EU temporary protection on 31 July 2026: **1.29 million in Germany, 0.95 million in Poland and 0.40 million in Czechia** ([Censor.net citing Eurostat](https://censor.net/en/news/4017921/over-4-4-million-ukrainians-hold-eu-temporary-protection)), with about 0.55 million more outside Europe ([Interfax-Ukraine citing UNHCR](https://en.interfax.com.ua/news/general/1186730.html)). Their right to stay depends on government decisions with hard dates: EU protection runs to 4 March 2027 ([EUAA](https://dip.euaa.europa.eu/node/802)), and Poland tightened its PESEL UKR rules during 2026 ([SUD.UA](https://sud.ua/en/news/abroad/364710-ukrayintsi-v-polschi-zishtovkhuyutsya-z-novimi-obmezhennyami-ta-vidmovami-u-vidachi-pesel-ukr)). Refugees named information in a language they cannot read as their biggest barrier ([CLEAR Global](https://clearglobal.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Language-and-information-gaps-for-people-fleeing-war-in-Ukraine-CLEAR-Global-September-2022.pdf)). Ukrainian, not Russian, is the first language to offer them: 63% of Ukrainians speak Ukrainian at home and 13% Russian (KIIS, 2025) ([Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/en/amp/news/1078925-13-of-citizens-speak-russian-at-home-another-19-speak-two-languages-kiis-survey)), and the choice is sensitive ([Forced Migration Review](https://www.fmreview.org/?p=2174)).

The habits fit a calm, sourced feed, and nobody offers one. Telegram is the main news source for **51%** of Ukrainians ([Ukrainska Pravda](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/09/25/7532494)), and **46%** report fatigue with war news ([UkraineWorld](https://ukraineworld.org/articles/opinions/ukrainians-consume-news-2025-survey)). Diia reaches 24 million users but delivers services, not news of decisions ([Cabinet of Ministers](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/news/mintsyfry-zastosunkom-diia-korystuiutsia-ponad-24-miliony-ukraintsiv)); LIGA360's new AI analysis of legal changes is a paid tool for professionals ([Ekonomichna Pravda](https://epravda.com.ua/biznes/liga360-zapuskaye-pershiy-v-ukrajini-personalizovaniy-ai-analiz-zakonodavchih-zmin-809460/)); and a Rada app announced in 2021 was never confirmed to have shipped ([Slovo i Dilo](https://www.slovoidilo.ua/2021/11/22/novyna/polityka/zakonoproyekty-smartfoni-korniyenko-anonsuvav-mobilnyj-dodatok-rady)).

The catch is fetching Ukraine's own decisions. In probes on 15 October 2026 from a Kenyan IP, **zakon.rada.gov.ua, data.rada.gov.ua and rada.gov.ua all timed out** ([probe](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/main/r)), and data.rada.gov.ua also refused connections from a second vantage point ([probe](https://data.rada.gov.ua/open/main/api)). The Cabinet's RSS works but sits under `/api/`, which its robots.txt disallows, as it does the list of acts ([kmu.gov.ua robots.txt](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/robots.txt)); the President's site returned 403 ([probe](https://www.president.gov.ua/documents)). No public statement says the Rada blocks foreign traffic, and a Cloudflare Worker leaves from different IPs, so **one test fetch from the deployed Worker settles it**. Until then, version one can summarise in Ukrainian what the feed already fetches for the diaspora (Germany, EU, US, Canada, UK) plus new Polish and Czech sources. Summaries of residence rules read close to legal advice, so each should carry a short "not legal advice, read the official source" line next to the link (inference).

## Spanish, Portuguese, Czech, Polish and Russian trade reach against cost

**Spanish** buys the most countries per translation. Instituto Cervantes counts **635.7 million** potential speakers ([La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/cultura/mas-de-635-millones-de-personas-hablan-espanol-crece-5-en-un-ano-y-se-afianza-como-la-tercera-lengua-nid28102025/)), and the feed already carries Mexico's DOF and Argentina's SAIJ (`docs/sources.csv`). Spain's gazette, boe.es, has the highest traffic per user measured, **about 31 visits per 100 internet users**, though public-job notices inflate it ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/boe.es/)). Latin America has the profile a neutral feed suits: trust in news is 31% in Mexico, 25% in Colombia and 26% in Argentina, and 45 to 49% avoid the news ([DNR Mexico](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/mexico); [DNR Colombia](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/colombia); [DNR Argentina](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/argentina)). The trade-off is iOS: 28% in Mexico and **12.6% in Argentina** ([StatCounter Mexico](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/mexico); [StatCounter Argentina](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/argentina)), so much of the value waits for the Android release. Spain and Mexico together still give about 43 million iOS-reachable users (estimate).

**Portuguese** is effectively Brazil: 99% of planalto.gov.br's visitors are Brazilian ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/planalto.gov.br/)). Brazil has 185 million internet users and a 21% iOS share, about **39 million iOS-reachable users** (estimate), the largest non-English pool measured after India and Indonesia, which both show weak interest in legislation ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-brazil); [StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/brazil)). It is the third-largest app download market ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1064275)), and its top news-category app in late 2025 was a **Bolsa Família benefit lookup**, with a workers'-rights app fifth: direct evidence that Brazilians install apps to understand official rules ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q4-unified-top-5-news-units-br-600abc3f241bc16eb850846d)). Trust in news fell 6 points to 36%, and 13% already use AI chatbots for news weekly, among the highest of 48 markets ([DNR Brazil](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/brazil); [DNR 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/DNR%202026%20FINAL_2.pdf)). The costs: one country, 79% Android, and heavier extra sources.

**Czech** has the weakest competition found. The leading free law app, Zákony pro lidi, has **25 ratings** and no notifications or AI ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cz/app/id6698877007)); Hlídač státu tracks contracts and subsidies, not laws ([hlidacstatu.cz](https://www.hlidacstatu.cz/)); and the paid ASPI legislative monitor costs 3,990 CZK per user per year ([Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/cs-cz/solutions/aspi/svet-aspi/nastroje/aspi-monitor-legislativniho-procesu)). Czechs visit law portals at about the French rate, about 10 visits per 100 users (proxy) ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/zakonyprolidi.cz/)), trust news at 31% and avoid it at 41% ([DNR Czech Republic](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/czech-republic)). Trust in government is the lowest in the OECD at **18.6%** ([OECD via CSO Ireland](https://cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-trsic/trustsurveyinternationalcomparisons2023/results)), so the pitch must be "primary source, neutral wording", not authority. The market is small, about **3 million iOS-reachable users** (estimate), and Seznam holds 15% of search plus the leading news portal ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/czech-republic)), so the app stores and Telegram are the realistic channels. Czech sources pay twice, though: they also feed the Ukrainian version for about 0.6 million Ukrainians in Czechia.

**Polish** offers the cleanest source found: the Sejm's ELI API returned this year's 1,335 gazette acts as JSON, with no robots.txt limits ([probe](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/eli/acts/DU/2026)). Trust in news fell **8 points to 39%**, 46% avoid news ([DNR Poland](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/poland)), and iOS holds 36% ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/poland)). The demand proxy is weak and low-confidence, about 6 parliament-site visits per 100 users from an unconfirmed Similarweb figure ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/sejm.gov.pl/)), and Polish competitors were barely researched.

**Russian, for readers outside Russia,** has a large but scattered audience. Ethnologue counts 210 million speakers ([Ethnologue via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_languages_by_total_number_of_speakers)), and Russian Wikipedia is the third most-read edition, at about 29 million human page views a day (a reading proxy) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_used_on_the_Internet)). Outside Russia and Ukraine the largest pools, from censuses of mixed years and definitions, are Kazakhstan (14.4 million total speakers, 2021), Kyrgyzstan (3.4 million), Germany (1.9 million), Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia (0.9 to 1.9 million each) and Israel (1.2 million, 2011) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_distribution_of_Russian_speakers)); Belarus was not assessed for risk. Among Ukrainians, 13% speak Russian at home and 19% both languages ([Ukranews](https://ukranews.com/en/amp/news/1078925-13-of-citizens-speak-russian-at-home-another-19-speak-two-languages-kiis-survey)), and 70% of refugees surveyed in Czechia speak Russian, but 90% speak Ukrainian ([UNHCR](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2083358/CZ__Profile_And_Intentions_Of_Refugees_From_Ukraine_August_2022+%281%29.pdf)), so Russian adds little reach among Ukrainians beyond what Ukrainian covers. Kazakhstan is the clearest new market: Russian is used alongside Kazakh in state administration ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_distribution_of_Russian_speakers)), Apple has no Kazakh listing language, so Russian or English is the store language there ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/app-store-localizations)), and iOS holds 30.8% ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/kazakhstan)), about 4 million iOS-reachable Russian readers (rough estimate from the 2021 census share, ITU's 93.4% internet penetration and StatCounter). Its sources are clean: the presidency's Russian RSS answered and robots.txt allows everything ([probe](https://www.akorda.kz/ru/rss)), and the legal database Adilet allows act pages for all agents, barring only AI-training crawlers, its API and search ([probe](https://adilet.zan.kz/robots.txt)). Official sources already matter there: **14% of Kazakhs name government websites as a main source of domestic news**, against 66.8% for social networks ([Astana Times](https://astanatimes.com/2025/07/kazakh-audience-turns-to-social-media-as-primary-news-source-study-finds/)). Consumer competition is thin: the Russian-language legal market is led by professional systems such as Garant, from about 12,000 roubles a month in a third-party review ([Toolfox](https://toolfox.ru/services/s/garant)), and ConsultantPlus, whose free app holds core documents ([iPhones.ru](https://www.iphones.ru/iNotes/399262)), while Adilet, pravo.by and lex.uz are web portals with only unofficial apps found ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1608798574)).

The trade-offs are real. No demand proxy was measured for any Russian-reading country outside Russia. Each group wants its own government's decisions, and of these countries only Germany is in the feed; the plan puts Israel in Phase 2, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and the Baltics in Phase 3, and Kyrgyzstan in Phase 4 (`docs/plan.md`). And a Russian version from a Ukrainian founder during the war can read to Ukrainian readers as normalising Russian. That is a product judgement, not a measured finding; framing it as "for Russian readers in Kazakhstan, the Baltics, Israel and Germany", offering Ukrainian first, and keeping Russia excluded as a market would limit it (judgement). One AI call can write Ukrainian and Russian together, and the two share Cyrillic fonts and the founder's review, but each language still adds about 8 Neurons per article (estimate).

## Registers undercounted Ukrainians in Czechia, but not by millions

A common view, rooted in Western Ukraine's long history of work in Czechia, is that a couple of million Ukrainians lived and worked there before the full-scale war, far above the official counts. The evidence agrees that the official counts were too low; it disagrees on the size.

Official counts first. Ukrainians with residence permits numbered about **105,000 in 2013 and 196,875 at the end of 2021**, then 636,282 at the end of 2022 once temporary protection began ([Wikipedia, citing CZSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_the_Czech_Republic)). At the end of 2025 the Interior Ministry counted **612,953**: 118,718 permanent residents, about 393,000 under temporary protection and about 100,000 on other permits ([MV ČR](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/volby/soubor/ctvrtletni-zprava-o-migraci-iv-2025.aspx)).

That view is right on several points. At the end of February 2022, before refugees entered work in numbers, the Labour Office recorded **209,658 employed Ukrainians, more than all registered Ukrainian residents** including children and pensioners, so tens of thousands of short-term and circular workers sat outside residence statistics ([ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/praci-ma-107-000-uprchliku-s-vizem-k-ochrane-jurecka-to-oznacil-za-uspech/2246440)). In 2017 an agency association head estimated "at least 200,000" illegal foreign workers, an IOM expert put the upper bound at half a million, and the article concluded that hundreds of thousands, mainly Ukrainians, worked unregistered ([Echo24](https://www.echo24.cz/a/wCRAe/take-cesko-zaplavili-migranti-z-ukrajiny-potrebujeme-je)). Working in Czechia on Polish visas was common and uncounted, with visas sold in Lviv for about USD 120 ([ČT24](https://ct24.ceskatelevize.cz/clanek/domaci/misto-ceskych-viz-dostavaji-ukrajinci-pokoutne-polska-zprostredkovatele-jim-namlouvaji-ze-to-nevadi-78737); [Trčka et al.](https://dspace.tul.cz/items/a871fa05-f96c-413b-8235-a9f306879cbd)). Ukraine's ambassador said in 2020 that most labour migrants came from Zakarpattia, Lviv, Ternopil and Ivano-Frankivsk, and called the number of illegal workers "unknown" ([Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/30443050.html)). Even among refugees surveyed in Czechia in 2022, **Zakarpattia was the top region of origin at 24%** despite little fighting there, which fits families joining earlier workers (inference) ([UNHCR](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2083358/CZ__Profile_And_Intentions_Of_Refugees_From_Ukraine_August_2022+%281%29.pdf)).

| Ukrainians in Czechia | Official count | Present at one time, range (estimate) | Central (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| End of 2021, before the full-scale war | 196,875 residents | 0.25 to 0.6 million | **0.3 to 0.35 million** |
| 2025 to 2026 | 612,953 residents | 0.53 to 0.76 million | **about 0.63 million** |
| Since February 2022, cumulative | 749,328 protection grants plus about 197,000 pre-war residents | up to about 0.95 million held legal status at some point | — |

The ranges add registered residents, circular workers on the employment register, Polish-visa workers and undeclared workers, each with a low, central and high value. The high end rests on expert figures that their own authors called unverifiable.

"A couple of million" at one time does not fit the other data. Two million would be about 18 to 19% of Czechia's population; if 1.5 million of them worked, they would hold about 28% of the country's 5.3 million jobs ([AMSP](https://amsp.cz/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/ANAL%C3%9DZA_ZAM%C4%9ASTNANCI-final_Def.pdf)), when foreigners of all nationalities held "more than 12%" ([CZSO](https://csu.gov.cz/produkty/vice-nez-12-zamestnanosti-v-cesku-tvori-cizinci)). Ukraine's own 2017 labour survey counted 123,000 labour migrants in Czechia ([Vox Ukraine](https://voxukraine.org/?p=76039)). Remittances agree: Czechia sent **USD 877 million** to Ukraine in 2021 ([NBU](https://bank.gov.ua/files/ES/Perekaz_y_en.pdf)), about USD 440 per person if two million were there, against roughly USD 3,100 to 3,600 per Ukrainian in Poland. Scaling to Poland's 1.3 to 1.5 million Ukrainians gives about 245,000 to 283,000 people in Czechia (estimate; the NBU models part of the Czech figure) ([CMR Warsaw](https://inicjatywadoskonalosci.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2023/02/Spotlight-FEBRUARY-2023.pdf)).

"Millions" does hold in three other senses, none of them a Czech head count at one time: everyone who worked in Czechia at some point over two decades of circular migration (no source measures this); Western Ukrainian labour migration to Poland and Czechia combined, since Poland alone had 1.3 to 1.5 million Ukrainians before the war ([CMR Warsaw](https://inicjatywadoskonalosci.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2023/02/Spotlight-FEBRUARY-2023.pdf)); and all Ukrainians working abroad, 3 to 5 million in 2021 ([Ukrinform](https://ukrinform.net/rubric-society/3283862-up-to-five-million-ukrainians-work-abroad-institute-for-demography.html)).

For the product, today's number is what counts: about **0.6 million**, the highest share per head in the EU at 36.2 per 1,000 ([Censor.net citing Eurostat](https://censor.net/en/news/4017921/over-4-4-million-ukrainians-hold-eu-temporary-protection)). In a 2022 sample, 90% spoke Ukrainian and 70% Russian ([UNHCR](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2083358/CZ__Profile_And_Intentions_Of_Refugees_From_Ukraine_August_2022+%281%29.pdf)). They get information mainly from Facebook (68.6%) and Telegram (47.6%) ([MV ČR survey summary](https://migrant-integration.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/MVCR%20INFO%20V%C3%BDzkum_komunika%C4%8Dn%C3%ADch_kan%C3%A1l%C5%AF_shrnut%C3%AD.pdf)) and trust official websites less than community groups ([Charles University](https://iss.fsv.cuni.cz/sites/default/files/uploads/files/AC%20MINTE%20CZ%20%20REPORT%202.pdf)). Most (79.4%) rate state information as good, but more than half want more on the rules for longer stays ([MV ČR](https://archiv.mv.gov.cz/clanek/vyzkum-ukrajinsti-uprchlici-ziskavaji-od-statu-potrebne-informace-rychle-a-kvalitne-vysledky-resort-vnitra-vyuzije-pro-dalsi-zlepsovani-sluzeb.aspx)). The next big moments are the end of EU protection on 4 March 2027 and the special long-term residence permit, for which 56,503 people registered interest in April 2026 ([MV ČR](https://mv.gov.cz/migrace/o-zvlastni-dlouhodoby-pobyt-ma-v-roce-2026-zajem-56-tisic-osob)).

The AI cost is now measured, and it is lower than the notes assumed. Cloudflare's Workers AI analytics (GraphQL `aiInferenceAdaptiveGroups`, read on 15 October 2026) show that on 14 October the summarizer made **167 calls to glm-4.7-flash using 2,615 Neurons**, with 234,379 input and 36,429 output tokens: **about 15.7 Neurons per article**, or about 1,400 input and 218 output tokens each (measured; the ~65 in `docs/decisions.md` was an estimate). The published rates of 5,500 Neurons per million input tokens and 36,400 per million output tokens ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)) reproduce it: 234,379 × 0.0055 + 36,429 × 0.0364 ≈ 1,289 + 1,326 = 2,615. English alone therefore fits about **10,000 / 15.7 ≈ 640 articles a day**. Each extra language written in the same call adds about 218 output tokens × 36,400 / 1,000,000 ≈ **8 Neurons** (estimate: it assumes a translation is about as long in tokens as the English text; some languages split into more tokens than English, so Cyrillic may cost more ([Petrov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15425))). That gives about 15.7 + 8 ≈ 24 Neurons and **≈ 420 articles a day with one extra language**, about 15.7 + 24 ≈ 40 Neurons and **≈ 250 a day with three**, and about 63 Neurons and ≈ 160 a day with all six shortlisted languages (estimates). Today's volume is about 70 to 170 new articles a day, so the free tier covers several languages for now; the headroom shrinks as sources grow toward the planned 85. The 1,500-token output cap fits several languages too: at 218 tokens each, English plus five extra languages is about 1,300 tokens, though long summaries will reach the cap sooner, and all six would need it raised slightly. Writing the languages in the same call keeps the project's "one AI call per article" rule. A separate translation call would cost a little more, about 12 Neurons (estimate, assuming about 700 input tokens), and breaks that rule unless it is amended. Translating on the phone with Google ML Kit costs nothing on the server but is meant for "casual and simple" translations ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/language/translation)), and Workers AI's dedicated translation model, m2m100, is not cheaper, at about 22 Neurons per translation (estimate: 500 input and 218 output tokens at 31,050 per million) ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). Store translation is the cheapest step, but its payoff is uncertain: the one controlled test found 33% more conversions in China and no change in the Netherlands ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2016/03/design-app-pages-for-better-conversion-case-study-of-paper-by-fiftythree/)).

Quality needs a check before launch. The current model's card lists only English and Chinese and publishes no multilingual scores ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-4.7-Flash)), while Qwen3 lists Ukrainian, Czech, Polish and Arabic among 119 languages ([Qwen](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/)) and Gemma 3 publishes translation scores ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-3-12b-it)). A native speaker should read about 20 summaries per language before release; the founder can do this for Ukrainian. Any change to the model, the token cap or the one-call rule goes into `docs/decisions.md`.

## Validate with listings, device language and a vote before translating

Two facts shape the tests. App Store Connect reports by storefront, which follows the billing address ([Apple](https://developer-rno.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-analytics-filters-and-dimensions)), so **a Ukrainian with a German Apple ID counts as Germany**; device language is the better signal. And the app has no analytics package today (`app/pubspec.yaml`), so device language must come either from Firebase Analytics, which records it automatically ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/analytics/flutter/user-properties); [Google](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9755590?hl=en)), or from the app sending its locale with feed requests for the Worker to count (suggestion, not tested).

| Step | Cost | What it measures | Read it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Check Apple Ads keyword popularity for terms like "закони", "рішення уряду", "законы", "nueva ley", "zákony" per storefront | Free account, no campaign needed | Search demand inside the App Store | Before writing listings |
| 2. Translate the store listing (Ukrainian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese) before the app, stating "summaries in English, Ukrainian coming"; add a custom product page per language with its own keywords | Hours | Impressions, page views and downloads per storefront, against the English baseline | After 4 weeks |
| 3. Count device language per country | About an hour with Firebase | How many current users read Ukrainian, Spanish, Portuguese, Czech, Polish or Russian, split by country so Russian readers in Kazakhstan or Germany are counted apart from Russia | Continuously |
| 4. In-app "Read in: Українська / Español / Čeština / Русский" vote, clearly labelled as a poll | Free | Tap rate among users with a matching device language, with 10% of other users as a control | At 100 users shown and 10 taps |
| 5. Telegram channel mirroring the Ukrainian summaries, with a tracked store link | Free, plus daily review | Subscribers, views per post, forwards | After 4 to 6 weeks |

Apple allows up to 70 custom product pages, each localised with its own keywords, and says they add 2.5 points of conversion against a 1.6% default ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/custom-product-pages/)). Apple Ads popularity is a relative 5-to-100 score per storefront, reportedly hidden below 35 (one blog, unverified) ([Sonar](https://trysonar.app/blog/apple-search-popularity)); whether Apple Ads covers the Ukrainian and Czech storefronts was not checked. For reading results, about 100 users give a rate within ±10 points and about 385 within ±5, and telling 8% from 12% needs about 880 page views per variant (standard sample-size arithmetic at 95% confidence and 80% power). Treat a vote rate of 15% or more as strong and below 3% as weak, and Telegram views above 30% of subscribers after four weeks as real reading; these thresholds are judgement calls, not industry benchmarks. No public News-category conversion benchmark exists, and the US App Store average from page view to download is 8.6% ([AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/aso-blog/mobile-app-store-conversion-rate-benchmarks-per-category)), so compare each language with the app's own English baseline. One gap is worth closing first: a logged-in Google Trends and Keyword Planner check of "new law" in each language, which this research could not run.

## Conclusion

The first new language is less a market than an audience spread over four countries. Ukrainians in Ukraine, Germany, Poland and Czechia all read Ukrainian, but each group wants a different government's decisions. That turns the rollout question around: the cheapest strong move is not entering a new store but translating decisions the feed already fetches and adding two clean neighbours, Poland and Czechia, whose sources later carry Czech and Polish versions too. Ukraine itself becomes a country in the feed only if the Worker can reach its sites, and that one fetch is the most valuable test to run next.

The binding limits are fetchable sources and quality review, not app work or, for now, the AI budget: at the measured cost, three extra languages still fit about 250 articles a day against today's 70 to 170. Languages should still arrive one at a time, because each needs new sources and a native-speaker check, and only after a vote and store data clear a bar. One claim stays unproven for every language: that people will read official decisions daily rather than look them up when they need them. The strongest evidence found (people checking official sources to verify news, Brazilians installing benefit lookups, Ukrainians installing alert apps) points to utility rather than habit, so validation should track whether readers come back, not only whether they install.
